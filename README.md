# ABook

A fiction project built around one central idea: **prison is not one experience**. The series follows different people moving through the same system, with each point-of-view revealing a different prison life, set of pressures, loyalties, fears, routines and choices.

## Book One

**Title:** *Hear All, See All, Say Nothing*

This title is now the canonical Book One title for manuscripts, reading copies, PDFs and future publication material. Older references to *Big Shark in a Creek* are superseded and should be replaced when those documents are next regenerated or revised.

Book One begins with Connor, a young first-time prisoner who arrives green, uncertain and unprepared for prison politics. On reception/induction he notices another arrival, Craig, whose name already carries weight. Craig is deliberately kept mysterious: the reader never gets his point of view in Book One and learns about him only through other characters.

As rumours spread that there is serious money attached to attacking Craig, Connor is pulled into a situation he barely understands. When trouble reaches Craig, Connor acts on instinct and gets involved. That decision changes Connor's entire prison experience and puts him close to a man everybody else is watching.

The phrase **“Hear all. See all. Say nothing.”** is also an important piece of prison advice given to Connor by Mason and becomes a recurring thematic line throughout Book One.

The wider series can follow characters when they are transferred, released, recalled or reunited in other establishments. Minor characters can later become leads, allowing the world to expand almost indefinitely without losing continuity.

## Storytelling format

- Multi-POV novel.
- Main chapters are dates, containing short clearly labelled character POV sections.
- Connor is the main emotional entry point in Book One.
- Craig has **no POV sections in Book One**, preserving his mystery.
- Serious situations are balanced with dry humour, strange everyday routines and memorable side characters.
- The prose should be original, highly readable, vivid and character-led, with a warm/wry British sensibility rather than imitating any specific author.
- Violence and criminality serve the story and consequences; they are not written as instruction manuals.

## Build process

1. Lock the world and character rules.
2. Build the full Book One story map.
3. Draft the opening and establish the voice.
4. Draft the complete manuscript while continuously checking continuity.
5. Developmental edit: plot, pacing, character arcs and unanswered questions.
6. Line edit: clarity, humour, dialogue and consistency.
7. Format the finished manuscript for ebook and paperback publication.
8. Approve a narration sample, then generate and review the audiobook edition.

The goal is not just one novel. It is a reusable fictional universe capable of supporting a long-running series and spin-offs.
