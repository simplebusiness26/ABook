# Book One — Draft V2 Structure

**Working title:** *Big Shark in a Creek*

## Structural decision

Book One now uses **dated chapters containing multiple clearly labelled POV sections**.

The opening remains heavily Connor-led because the reader needs to experience his arrest, police custody, court, remand and arrival at HMP Lewes from inside his fear. Once Connor reaches Lewes, the novel opens outward into a fast ensemble structure.

The exact calendar year is deliberately not printed in Draft V2 yet. Chapter headings use day and month only. Once the story year is locked, all period-specific legal/prison details can be checked and the weekday/year added if useful.

## Why this structure fits the story

The central idea of the series is that there is no single answer to “what is prison like?” A dated mosaic lets several people experience the same day as completely different worlds.

It also lets the Craig threat move through the prison almost like a signal:

Craig arrives → prisoners recognise him → word travels → Big Sharky hears → money is attached → Malik hears → friendship groups discuss it → some refuse involvement → some are tempted → staff notice atmosphere without knowing the whole reason → Connor remains largely unaware → danger arrives in front of him → Connor chooses to help Craig.

That chain should be felt through quick cuts rather than explained in one exposition chapter.

## Draft V2 opening run

### 14 OCTOBER — ARRIVAL
Primary anchor: Connor.

Connor's ordinary night collapses into armed arrest. He learns the aggravated burglary he thought was behind him has caught up with him. His solicitor reveals that blood from the recovered Mercedes C63 AMG is attributed to Connor; Connor suddenly remembers the cut on his middle finger. Witnesses allege a gun was present. No firearm has been recovered and Connor does not admit one. He gives a no-comment interview and does not identify the other people involved.

Connor is remanded, transported to HMP Lewes, frightened by reception and first sees Craig in the holding area. End: “Craig's here.”

### 15 OCTOBER — THE NAME
POVs: Mason, Leah, Connor, Malik.

Mason sees Connor's terror as familiar first-timer behaviour and recognises the significance of Craig's arrival. Leah sees Craig as a risk/problem rather than a legend. Connor has his first night, tries to contact home and has his first proper conversation with Craig. Malik hears that people outside are already asking questions about Craig.

### 16 OCTOBER — THE MESSAGE
POVs: Big Sharky, Jay, Connor, Bennett, Malik.

Big Sharky receives confirmation that Craig is at Lewes. We do not learn their history, only that the news is personal. Serious money becomes attached to Craig. Jay hears the first version through his circle and dislikes outsiders treating prisoners as disposable. Connor is still preoccupied by family and his own case. Bennett's separate coercion storyline begins to tighten. Malik realises the rumour is not just gossip.

### 17 OCTOBER — EVERYBODY KNOWS SOMETHING
POVs: Connor, Bruce, Leah, Deano, Malik, Mason.

The message mutates. Different people have different figures, different stories and different ideas about what Big Sharky wants. Connor starts learning the social map of Lewes but does not know why people are looking at Craig. Bruce provides comic pressure-release while demonstrating an unexpectedly large network of acquaintances. Leah notices unusual interest around Craig. Deano does not want somebody else's feud swallowing his circle.

### 18 OCTOBER — LINES
POVs: Leon, Jay, Connor, Bennett, Macca, Malik.

People begin being asked where they stand. Leon refuses involvement. Jay faces pressure from outside associates but does not want his people carrying consequences for somebody else. Connor and Craig talk more naturally. Bennett discovers that a problem he thought he could compartmentalise is following him through work. Macca's family concerns show how irrelevant Craig can seem to somebody whose sentence is measured in missed home life. Malik knows enough to become nervous.

### 19 OCTOBER — PRESSURE
POVs: Big Sharky, Deano, Connor, Bruce, Leah, Mason.

Big Sharky's patience is shown without revealing motive. Men around Lewes discuss the money more seriously, but practical attack mechanics remain off-page. Connor begins to be noticed simply because he is often near Craig. Bruce recognises the atmosphere before Connor does. Leah sees Connor drifting into somebody else's risk. Mason warns Connor about borrowed reputations.

### 20 OCTOBER — THE CHOICE
POVs: Connor, Jay, Leah, Mason, Connor.

The threat finally reaches Craig during an ordinary prison movement/induction setting. The violence is brief and non-graphic. Connor has only seconds to choose whether to move away or help. He acts instinctively and backs Craig. Staff intervene. The important event is not the mechanics of the attack; it is Connor's decision and the fact that everybody sees it.

By the end of the date, Connor's social meaning inside Lewes has changed. He entered as an unknown frightened first-timer. He ends the opening movement as the young prisoner who stood beside Craig when it mattered.

## This is the handover point into the middle of Book One

After 20 October, the next section of the novel can develop:

- Connor becoming informally close to Craig;
- Connor learning that his old cannabis line ultimately sat inside Craig's wider network;
- Connor enjoying status and then discovering its cost;
- Leah's curiosity about Craig becoming personal;
- Bennett's corruption/coercion arc worsening;
- Jay and Deano dealing with consequences of the failed attack;
- Malik discovering more about where the order originated;
- Darren, Macca, Leon and other lives showing that Craig's conflict is only one version of prison;
- Connor's girlfriend/outside relationship beginning to deteriorate;
- Connor eventually overreaching by acting as though Craig's reputation belongs to him;
- Craig's case moving toward sentence and eventual transfer into the dispersal system.

## Draft rule

Draft V2 is allowed to be expansive and imperfect. The goal is to create enough connected story that the creator can react to characters, pace and plot from actual scenes rather than from an abstract outline. We will revise aggressively after the opening run exists.
