# Connor backs Craig - canonical attack beat

This note supersedes any shorthand in earlier drafts such as **"He backed Craig"** where the physical action is left vague.

The scene should be **clear, immediate and physical**, so the reader understands exactly why other prisoners later decide Connor stood with Craig. It should not become gory, nor should it explain attack planning or weapon construction.

## Canonical beat

Connor is waiting with Craig during an ordinary movement when a prisoner suddenly drives towards Craig.

Connor sees the movement before he fully understands it. He grabs Craig by the sleeve/shoulder and pulls him off the attacker's direct line while stepping forward himself. Connor and the first attacker collide hard enough to knock Connor off balance.

Before Connor can recover, a second prisoner comes in from the side. Connor moves between him and Craig rather than backing away. He takes another hard impact, is knocked against a bench/wall, gets back up and pushes the second man away from Craig.

Craig is now reacting too, staying on his feet and protecting his own space. The first attacker tries to come forward again. Connor moves back beside Craig and refuses to leave him isolated until staff force their way between everyone.

Connor's actions are messy and instinctive rather than skilled or heroic. He does not execute a clever plan. He simply keeps moving toward the danger when he could have stepped away.

When staff finally separate them, Connor is breathing hard, sore and shocked by what he has just done. Craig looks at him with genuine surprise because most people would have moved away.

The important social fact witnessed by the wing is:

**Connor physically placed himself between Craig and the men coming at him, got knocked down/off balance, got back up, and stayed beside Craig until staff took control.**

That is why the story mutates into "Connor saved Craig" even though the reality was faster, messier and less cinematic.

## Tone rule

Violence can be tense and concrete - shoves, collisions, blows, falling into furniture, breathlessness, staff forcing people apart - but avoid gore or lingering injury description. The emotional and political consequence matters more than bodily damage.
