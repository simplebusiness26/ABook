# Connor backs Craig - canonical attack beat

This note supersedes any shorthand in earlier drafts such as **"He backed Craig"** where the physical action is left vague.

The scene should be **clear, immediate and physical**, so the reader understands exactly why other prisoners later decide Connor stood with Craig. It should be vivid without becoming gory, and it should not explain attack planning or weapon construction.

## Canonical fight beat

Connor is waiting with Craig during an ordinary movement when a prisoner suddenly drives towards Craig.

Connor sees the movement before he fully understands it. He grabs Craig by the sleeve/shoulder and pulls him off the attacker's direct line while stepping forward himself. Connor and the first attacker collide hard enough to knock Connor off balance.

Before Connor can recover, a second prisoner comes in from the side. Connor moves between him and Craig rather than backing away. He takes another hard impact, is knocked against a bench/wall, gets back up and pushes the second man away from Craig.

Craig is now reacting too, staying on his feet and protecting his own space. The first attacker tries to come forward again. Connor moves back beside Craig and refuses to leave him isolated until staff force their way between everyone.

Connor's actions are messy and instinctive rather than skilled or heroic. He does not execute a clever plan. He simply keeps moving toward the danger when he could have stepped away.

The important social fact witnessed by the wing is:

**Connor physically placed himself between Craig and the men coming at him, got knocked down/off balance, got back up, and stayed beside Craig until staff took control.**

That is why the story mutates into "Connor saved Craig" even though the reality was faster, messier and less cinematic.

## Immediate staff response

The alarm is already sounding as staff rush in. In the first seconds, officers are dealing with bodies and movement rather than anybody's explanation of who started what.

Everyone visibly involved is restrained and separated. Connor is treated as one of the participants because staff saw him rush into the violence and fight back; they do not yet know whether he was protecting Craig, joining the attackers or involved in something arranged beforehand.

Connor is taken away from the wing and placed in segregation while staff establish what happened. The two men who attacked Craig are also removed from the normal wing population.

Staff later review CCTV and compare what they saw with accounts of the incident. The footage supports Connor's version: he was not part of the original attack; he reacted after the first man moved at Craig.

Connor is therefore cleared of being one of the original attackers, but he still spends the **night in segregation** because he physically entered the fight and the prison needs time to untangle the incident.

## Connor's night alone

This night matters emotionally.

The adrenaline disappears and Connor is left alone with the consequences of a decision that took only seconds. He replays the moment repeatedly:

- he could have moved away;
- he did not know the attackers;
- he barely knew Craig;
- he may have just attached himself to a feud he does not understand;
- he does not know whether he has helped himself or made prison much more dangerous.

This is the first time Connor has space to realise that instinctive loyalty can create consequences before he has time to choose whether he wants them.

He should remember Mason's advice - **Hear all. See all. Say nothing.** - and recognise, with some irony, that the one thing Mason did not tell him was what to do when his body moved before his brain did.

## Return to C Wing - birth of "Rush"

The following day Connor is released from segregation and returned to **C Wing**, where Craig is also back.

By then the story has travelled ahead of him. Connor does not get to explain himself before the wing has already decided what happened.

Bruce is the person who turns the incident into Connor's nickname.

The preferred beat is casual rather than ceremonial. Connor comes back onto the landing expecting questions or awkwardness. Bruce clocks him first and says something along the lines of:

**"Here he is. Rush."**

Connor does not understand.

Bruce explains that Connor saw trouble and *rushed straight into it* before anybody with sense could stop him. Jamo laughs. Somebody else repeats "Rush" from further down the landing. The name spreads because it is funny, accurate and attached to the story everybody is already discussing.

Connor does **not** choose the nickname.

By the end of the day someone Connor has never spoken to calls him **Rush**, and that is when he realises the prison has named him.

Craig should continue calling him **Connor**, which quietly distinguishes the real relationship from the identity the wing is building around him.

## Tone rule

Violence can be tense and concrete - shoves, collisions, blows, falling into furniture, breathlessness, staff forcing people apart - but avoid gore or lingering injury description. The emotional and political consequence matters more than bodily damage.
