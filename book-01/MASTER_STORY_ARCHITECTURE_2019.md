# Hear All, See All, Say Nothing — Master Story Architecture

**Status:** primary Book One structural blueprint.

**Story year:** 2019 into early 2020.

**Opening date:** **Friday 20 September 2019.**

**Target ending:** February 2020, before COVID-19 restrictions become part of ordinary prison life.

This document supersedes the old placeholder October weekday/date structure wherever dates conflict.

---

# What the novel is

Connor is the **timeline**. The reader enters prison with him and leaves HMP Lewes with him.

Craig is the **mystery/gravity**. He gives the novel a powerful unanswered question, but Book One never enters his head.

The prison is the **real subject**. Every major recurring character must reveal a different prison, even though they share the same walls.

The book must therefore feel like **different lives repeatedly smashing together because they happen to occupy the same institution**.

A major incident should rarely belong to one storyline only. A lockdown caused by Craig's world might ruin Macca's family call, stop Leon's gym session, cost Jamo an interview, trap Darren behind his door at the wrong moment, give Malik five contradictory rumours and leave staff writing paperwork while Connor worries about reputation.

---

# Structural rules

- Dated chapters containing clearly labelled POV sections.
- Connor is the most frequent viewpoint but not the only major character.
- Craig has **NO POV in Book One**.
- Big Sharky has **NO interior POV in Book One**. He exists through calls, messages and other people's reactions.
- Some POV sections can be only a page or two. Important emotional scenes can breathe.
- Ordinary prison life is plot: work, education, canteen, visits, applications, jobs, IEP status, cell moves, searches, laundry, gym lists, servery arguments, TV problems, property and disciplinary waiting.
- Contraband, substance use and corruption can shape the story but are never written as operational instructions.
- Violence remains concrete enough to understand but non-graphic.
- Humour is essential. Prison cannot read as continuous misery.

---

# Legal / prison convergence

## Connor

Connor is arrested at the true beginning of his case. He arrives at Lewes as a frightened first-time remand prisoner, later pleads guilty, then suffers ordinary sentencing delays that keep returning him to Lewes when he expects certainty.

He ultimately receives **five years** and is allocated onward toward a **Category C prison**.

## Craig

Craig's Lewes arrival is **not** the beginning of his case.

He has already been remanded at another prison. A security decision moves him out of that establishment and into HMP Lewes. His large conspiracy case is already further advanced than Connor's when they physically land in Lewes at roughly the same time.

Craig contests important parts of the case. His proceedings run longer, but because his case began earlier the two legal timelines can converge naturally.

Craig ultimately receives a major sentence and is allocated into the **high-security / dispersal estate**.

## Final image

Connor and Craig entered the reader's Lewes story together for completely different reasons.

They leave Lewes at roughly the same time for completely different prison worlds.

The final sequence should cross-cut the two departures rather than manufacture an implausible identical journey.

# MOVEMENT ONE — ENTERING THE WORLD

## Friday 20 September — ARREST

### CONNOR

Normal evening. Connor has spent the day around his cannabis line, comes home, finishes the night with friends/runners and believes the old aggravated burglary has disappeared into the past.

Armed police arrive.

The phrase **aggravated burglary** brings the forgotten offence crashing back.

End with Connor in police custody, unable to stop thinking: **They know.**

Purpose: the reader loses normal life at exactly the same speed Connor does.

---

## Saturday 21 September — CUSTODY

### CONNOR

Solicitor consultation. The recovered Mercedes C63 contains blood attributed to Connor. Only then does he remember the small cut on his middle finger.

Witnesses allege a firearm was present. No firearm has been recovered and Connor does not admit possessing one.

Connor gives a no-comment interview and does not identify co-offenders.

Cell → solicitor → interview → cell → waiting.

Numbers begin building in his head: ten years, twelve, fifteen.

---

## Sunday 22 September — WAITING

### CONNOR

A deliberately quieter chapter.

Mum. Brothers. Sister. Little brother. Girlfriend. Co-offenders. Blood. Gun allegation. Sentence numbers.

Connor tries to calculate how old his little brother would be after fifteen years and stops.

The event of the chapter is lack of control.

---

## Monday 23 September — COURT / LEWES / G WING

### CONNOR

Court. The word **remanded**. Connor finally understands he is not going home.

Prison transport past ordinary life.

HMP Lewes reception.

An imposing female officer and intimidating peer workers make Connor feel younger than he has felt in years.

In reception/holding he notices another arrival whose presence changes the room.

### MASON

Mason, working induction/reception, sees two very different arrivals:

- one frightened young first-timer trying not to look frightened;
- one man prison already knows.

Craig has arrived following a security transfer from another establishment.

### CONNOR

Someone says quietly:

**"Craig's here."**

Connor does not know why that matters.

End on G Wing.

---

## Tuesday 24 September — THE NAME

POVs: Mason, Leah, Connor, Malik.

- Mason sees Connor trying to learn everything at once.
- Leah initially treats Craig as risk information rather than mythology.
- Connor experiences his first full prison morning and first real conversation with Craig.
- Malik hears that people outside already care where Craig has landed.

Craig appears surprisingly ordinary to Connor.

---

## Wednesday 25 September — THE MESSAGE

POVs: Jinx, Connor, Bennett, Malik, remote call sequence involving Big Sharky's voice.

- Big Sharky learns Craig is at Lewes through a call/message chain. No internal narration.
- Serious money becomes attached to Craig.
- Jinx dislikes outsiders expecting men inside to carry consequences for them.
- Connor is still mostly thinking about home and his own case.
- Bennett's separate coercion problem begins to surface.
- Malik realises the Craig rumour is not ordinary prison nonsense.

---

## Thursday 26 September — C WING

POVs: Connor, Jamo, Leah, Mason.

Connor and Craig move from G to C Wing.

Connor arrives with property and feels everybody looking at the new face.

An alarm interrupts ordinary noise. Connor sees the non-graphic aftermath of a serious shower-area assault and is disturbed by how quickly experienced prisoners return to normal conversation.

**G Wing had been prison with stabilisers on. C Wing had just taken them off.**

Connor meets Jamo, his first proper cellmate.

Jamo has been inside about six months, is doing education and thinks he understands the prison. To Connor, he practically does.

Jamo starts introducing the social map.

---

## Friday 27 September — LINES

POVs: Leon, Jinx, Connor, Bennett, Macca, Malik.

- Leon wants routine and neutrality.
- Jinx receives pressure from outside but refuses to volunteer his people.
- Connor talks more naturally with Craig.
- Bennett realises his outside problem follows him through the gate.
- Macca's world is family calls, not Craig.
- Malik knows enough to become nervous.

---

## Saturday 28 September — PRESSURE

POVs: Deano, Connor, Bruce, Leah, Mason.

- Deano wants Craig's feud kept away from his circle.
- Bruce appears, talks like Bruce, and somehow knows more than he should.
- Leah sees tension collecting around Craig.
- Mason gives Connor the line that becomes the title and moral refrain:

**"Hear all. See all. Say nothing."**

Connor understands the words but not yet the lesson.

---

## Sunday 29 September — THE CHOICE

POVs: Jinx, Connor, Leah, Mason, Connor.

The attack on Craig occurs during an ordinary movement/association period.

Connor reacts before understanding.

He pulls Craig off line, takes the first collision, is knocked away, gets back up, steps back beside Craig when another man comes in and remains there until staff force the situation apart.

Staff arriving cannot instantly know Connor's role. Everyone visibly involved is separated.

CCTV later shows the attackers moved first and Connor reacted afterwards.

Connor spends the night separated/segregated while the incident is untangled.

In silence he replays the same few seconds repeatedly.

For the first time since arrest he is not thinking about fifteen years.

He is thinking about ten seconds.

---

## Monday 30 September — RUSH

POVs: Connor, Malik, Leah, Bruce, Craig only through Connor's scene.

Connor is returned to C Wing.

Bruce sees him first.

**"Here he is. Rush."**

The joke travels faster than Connor does.

Craig later calls him **Connor**.

Craig asks why he got back up.

Connor eventually answers:

**"You were still there."**

Craig refuses to call him Rush because:

**"Because I know your name."**

Movement One ends with Connor socially transformed before he has had time to understand what changed.

---

# MOVEMENT TWO — BEING RUSH FEELS GOOD

**Period:** October 2019.

The book now expands beyond the opening threat. Craig remains important, but several chapters should pass where somebody else's prison is bigger than Craig.

## Early October — ATTENTION

POVs: Connor, Jamo, Malik, Bruce, Macca.

Connor discovers the rewards of reputation.

People who ignored him now greet him. Casual tests reduce. Information comes toward him instead of away from him.

Jamo is amused at first, then notices their cell becoming a place people approach because of Connor.

Macca has a family problem Connor barely notices.

Bruce enjoys having named something that has become real.

Connor starts liking Rush.

---

## Early October — DARREN

Darren gets a substantial POV introduction independent of Craig.

He is visibly struggling with addiction but still funny, human and socially connected.

Ordinary comments begin:

- "Darren looks rough lately."
- "Darren's bang on the spice."

Officer Grant identifies Darren as vulnerable and potentially useful for information.

Grant's pressure begins small enough that Darren can lie to himself about it.

---

## Early/Mid October — THE VISIT MISTAKE

Connor is pulled into a risky prohibited favour connected to a prison visit by people who recognise that the new "Rush" wants to prove he belongs.

The chapter focuses on fear, waiting and Connor realising too late that other people have placed the risk on the youngest willing person.

No concealment/security mechanics are described.

Afterwards Connor expects respect.

Craig gives him something more useful: a quiet dressing-down.

The lesson is not "how to commit crime better." It is that Connor is mistaking **being used** for **being important**.

Craig tells him he sees something in him, but if Connor keeps volunteering to carry everybody else's consequences, he will never become anything except useful to people who know better.

This is the beginning of the mentor/right-hand dynamic rather than the attack itself.

---

## Mid October — THE SPIN

Connor and Jamo's cell is searched in the wider fallout around Connor's risky behaviour.

Prohibited property / concerns create institutional consequences without the novel explaining concealment methods.

Connor's security/risk profile worsens.

Jamo, who has done education for months and is hoping for a trusted visits-orderly job, finds his opportunity frozen or lost amid the fallout around their shared cell and Connor's reputation.

This gives Jamo a **real reason** to resent Connor.

Connor may insist Jamo had nothing to do with it, but institutional unfairness does not disappear because Connor says so.

Jamo begins thinking: *I did everything right. He arrived five minutes ago, got into trouble and somehow became important.*

---

## Mid October — JOSH ARRIVES

### JOSH

Josh enters as a young first-timer on a vehicle-related offence.

He is high-energy, impulsive, distractible, funny, endlessly curious and immediately feels younger than the prison around him.

He is **not introduced as doomed**.

His ordinary outside plans matter: mum, family, driving, work, girls, getting home.

### DARREN

Josh gravitates toward Darren because Darren knows how the wing works.

For perhaps the first time in a long time, another prisoner looks at Darren and sees somebody competent.

Darren likes that feeling.

Their friendship begins warmly.

---

## Late October — JOBS

POVs: Connor, Jamo, Leon, Mason.

Prison work/education becomes normal life rather than exposition.

- Mason is established as induction orderly.
- Leon's gym orderly job is central to his routine.
- Jamo is furious about losing his route toward visits orderly.
- Connor learns that his security/reputation means trusted jobs are not available to him.

Connor ends up as a **wing cleaner**.

This should be funny and humiliating in exactly the right way: Rush may be getting known around the wing, but staff are still telling him to clean somebody else's mess properly.

His prisoner status rises while his institutional opportunities shrink.

---

## Late October — TYLER ARRIVES

Tyler enters as another young first-timer but reacts completely differently from Josh or Connor.

He talks too loudly, exaggerates who he knows and tells home that prison is easy.

On the phone his mum calls him **Ty** and immediately punctures the persona.

The reader understands he is frightened before most prisoners do.

---

# MOVEMENT THREE — EVERYBODY'S PRISON

**Period:** November to Christmas 2019.

This movement should make the book feel truly ensemble. The Craig mystery keeps moving, but several independent stories become equally compelling.

## Jamo and Connor fracture

Jamo's lost trusted-job opportunity, Connor's new attention and the constant tension around their cell finally erupt into a proper argument.

Jamo accuses Connor of becoming somebody else.

Connor hears only jealousy.

Both are partly right.

Jamo moves out.

Connor briefly thinks he has won a single cell.

He has not.

---

## Stuart moves in

Stuart is an African-born university student lawfully living/studying in Britain, on remand while contesting a case he says arose from self-defence during a scissors attack in student accommodation.

He is spiritual, openly Christian, gentle but not weak.

There is enough language/cultural difference for warmth and misunderstanding without caricature.

Stuart talks about righteousness, forgiveness and the sort of man Connor is becoming.

Connor mostly takes the piss.

He listens more than he admits.

Stuart is one of the few people who consistently calls him **Connor**, not Rush.

---

## Darren / Josh warmth

Josh follows Darren everywhere.

Their scenes need ordinary comedy before tragedy:

- Josh asks too many questions;
- Darren pretends to be annoyed but enjoys answering;
- canteen becomes an event;
- Josh talks about outside plans as if release is around the corner;
- other prisoners begin treating them as a pair.

Darren's addiction is still present, but Josh initially sees only the experienced older mate.

---

## Grant / Darren / Malik collision

Grant pushes Darren for more information.

Darren gives something he tells himself is minor.

That information contributes to a search / staff intervention affecting somebody in Jinx's wider circle.

Malik notices that staff appear to know something they should not obviously know.

Information changes from Malik's hobby into a liability.

Jinx begins wondering whether somebody around him is talking.

Do not turn this into a detective puzzle with operational informant methods. The pressure is social and emotional.

---

## Jinx and Deano

The failed Craig attack has damaged trust around Jinx's circle because Rico was involved.

Outside people continue making demands.

Deano initially wants nothing to do with it, but suspicion around informing and the continuing Craig fallout begin threatening relationships beyond one crew.

Jinx and Deano discover they occasionally need each other to stop other people's conflict swallowing their own friends.

Their cooperation should feel reluctant, practical and human.

---

## Yusuf

Yusuf becomes the fun-loving bridge between groups.

He is funny because he enjoys people, not because he exists to provide jokes.

His first real test arrives when two people/groups he genuinely likes expect incompatible loyalty.

Yusuf refuses to become anybody's possession.

Being everyone's mate suddenly costs him something.

---

## Tyler's invented reputation grows

Tyler keeps borrowing an older cousin/local reputation.

At first people laugh.

Then somebody takes one of Tyler's claims seriously and expects him to prove he can sort a problem he plainly cannot sort.

Tyler is now trapped by the fake person he invented to hide his fear.

Bruce is one of the first people to realise Tyler's stories do not add up, but Bruce initially treats it as comedy rather than exposing him.

---

## Connor learns the scale of Craig

Connor finally discovers that his supposedly independent cannabis line on the outside sat, several layers up, inside Craig's wider network.

The revelation should be simple rather than an exposition lecture.

Connor's sense of his own criminal importance shrinks in a single conversation.

Craig understands Connor's family/provider logic because he recognises the type of young man Connor is.

Connor becomes closer to Craig after being humbled rather than after being praised.

---

## Leah and Bennett — mirror arcs

Bennett's coercion worsens. Leaving the prison at the end of shift no longer feels like escaping prison.

Leah's interest in Craig has moved from professional curiosity into emotional attachment.

They begin crossing boundaries for opposite reasons:

- Bennett because he is frightened;
- Leah because she cares too much.

Any prohibited favours remain high-level and non-operational.

Leah starts recognising Bennett's behaviour because she knows what compromised judgement feels like from inside.

---

## Macca — the outside world keeps moving

Give Macca a substantial family chapter almost entirely unrelated to Craig.

A lockdown / regime disruption caused by somebody else's prison problem ruins an important call, visit or family moment.

Macca's anger reminds the reader that the largest event on the wing may be completely irrelevant to the largest event in one prisoner's life.

---

## Leon — routine becomes political

Leon wants to train, work, eat, read and sleep.

Pressure from different people turns neutrality into a position others interpret.

His gym-orderly role gives him something tangible to lose.

Leon eventually has to choose between protecting his carefully controlled routine and helping somebody when neutrality would effectively mean allowing harm.

The choice changes how others see him.

---

# CHRISTMAS MOVEMENT — ORDINARY LIFE AT ITS STRANGEST

Christmas should occupy several dated chapters rather than one montage.

The prison becomes emotionally contradictory:

- decorations / jokes / special food beside loneliness;
- canteen arguments beside family grief;
- Josh acting overexcited;
- Stuart talking about faith;
- Macca measuring the day by contact with home;
- Connor pretending he does not care that his girlfriend is becoming distant;
- Darren trying to stay level while Josh increasingly copies his world;
- Bruce saying something ridiculous and then unexpectedly kind;
- staff also wanting to be home.

Connor's girlfriend storyline should noticeably deteriorate here.

The outside world is beginning to stop waiting for him.

---

# MOVEMENT FOUR — THE COST OF EVERYBODY'S CHOICES

**Period:** January 2020.

The first half rewarded characters for adaptations that now begin costing them.

## Connor's court path

Connor has already indicated/entered his guilty plea by this stage.

He expects sentencing and psychologically prepares for certainty.

Then it is adjourned / knocked back for an ordinary legal or administrative reason that will be researched before final publication.

Connor returns to Lewes furious and frightened because waiting has become worse than the number itself.

The old fifteen-year panic briefly returns.

---

## Craig begins disappearing to court more often

Craig's large case is approaching / in contested proceedings.

His physical absences from the wing create more mythology than his presence.

Everybody has a different version of what is happening in court.

Connor realises how little Craig actually tells him.

Big Sharky's voice appears only through remote communications and reactions.

The reader should feel the two unseen legal machines moving toward sentence.

---

## Connor overreaches

Rush now feels natural to Connor.

He speaks with confidence in a situation where he assumes Craig's reputation belongs partly to him.

He implies or states what Craig will tolerate without Craig authorising him.

The mistake creates a genuine problem.

Craig makes Connor fix his own mess rather than rescuing him.

This is the first major moment where Connor understands that **standing beside power is not the same as possessing it**.

Mason's advice returns.

---

## Stuart is pushed out

Connor, increasingly invested in control/status and wanting his cell to fit his new prison life, causes Stuart to move out.

He tells himself it is just a cell move.

Stuart ends up sharing with another prisoner whose life is more heavily shaped by spice.

Over time Connor notices Stuart changing.

Connor feels the beginning of guilt but suppresses it.

Stuart's repeated language about righteousness now becomes irritating precisely because Connor knows why it bothers him.

---

## Tyler's lie collapses

Tyler can no longer deliver on the reputation he invented.

The truth becomes public enough to humiliate him.

The scene should not simply punish him physically. The deeper punishment is being seen as frightened after spending months constructing a person who was never frightened.

A later private conversation with Connor, Mason or Macca lets Tyler finally admit that he has been scared since reception.

This is a quiet victory: he begins becoming a real person instead of defending a fictional one.

---

## Darren / Josh deterioration

Josh's change should now be visible enough that other prisoners comment on it.

The contrast with his arrival matters.

He is still recognisably Josh, but some of the energy has gone in the wrong direction.

Darren knows he has helped make his own damaged world normal to somebody younger.

He does not know how to undo it.

Grant continues pressuring Darren for information, making Darren's guilt and dependency pull in opposite directions.

---

## Leah sees Bennett

Leah finally understands that Bennett is not simply acting strangely: he is frightened and trapped.

Because Leah has crossed her own professional boundaries for Craig, she cannot approach the situation from moral purity.

She turns to Craig for help before Bennett destroys himself.

Craig's influence causes the immediate pressure around Bennett to recede or change, but the exact mechanism stays off-page.

This should be morally uncomfortable, not triumphant.

Leah has used one compromised relationship to rescue another compromised officer.

Her own risk has increased.

Do **not** resolve whether Leah is caught in Book One.

---

## Stuart's trial / release

Stuart eventually wins his self-defence case and is found not guilty.

He leaves Lewes.

Connor should be happy for him and disturbed by how easily the prison simply closes over the space somebody occupied.

Stuart entered insisting on righteousness, was changed by remand, and leaves legally cleared but not untouched.

Connor cannot avoid wondering whether pushing him out helped make the worst part worse.

He still does not fully face the answer.

---

# MOVEMENT FIVE — CONSEQUENCES

**Period:** late January into February 2020.

## Josh's final arc

Josh suffers a serious substance-related medical collapse and dies.

The event is depicted through discovery, emergency response, absence and aftermath rather than physical detail.

The book must not reduce Josh to "the overdose victim." The emotional impact comes from remembering the noisy, funny lad who could not sit still, asked too many questions and talked about ordinary plans outside.

Darren survives.

That is the harder outcome for Darren.

He cannot honestly tell himself that his addiction affects only him anymore.

He did not intend Josh's death and Josh made his own choices, but Darren knows he helped make that world normal.

Grant's informant pressure now collides with Darren's guilt.

Darren's end-of-book movement is not miraculous recovery. It is a first meaningful refusal to keep pretending nothing matters.

---

## Malik chooses silence

Malik works out more than most people about Darren, Grant and the information leak.

Earlier Malik would have traded knowledge for status.

Now he deliberately keeps something to himself because passing it on would create more damage than truth would repair.

This is the title principle reaching somebody other than Connor:

**Hear all. See all. Say nothing.**

Malik finally understands information can be responsibility rather than currency.

---

## Jinx chooses people over outside authority

Jinx reaches the point where obeying outside pressure would sacrifice people physically around him.

He refuses.

The decision costs him standing with somebody outside / above him but protects his immediate circle.

Leadership becomes protecting people who have to live with the consequences, not simply obeying people who make demands from elsewhere.

Deano and Yusuf both play roles in preventing the fallout from becoming another wing-wide conflict.

---

## Leon's choice pays off

Leon accepts that complete neutrality was an illusion.

His routine survives in altered form, but he has chosen what kind of man he is when routine and conscience conflict.

His gym role may be threatened or changed, giving the decision a real cost.

---

## Macca's family payoff

Macca gets a meaningful family moment that is neither magically perfect nor relentlessly bleak.

His story should end with the outside world still complicated but emotionally real.

Prison has cost him time he cannot recover, and he knows it.

---

## Mason's long-view payoff

Mason watches Connor near the end and realises the frightened boy from induction now understands the prison far too well.

Connor once envied Mason's competence.

Now Connor sees the sadness inside being completely at home in prison.

Mason's own future can remain open, but there should be a hint that freedom / release is something he finds more frightening than he admits.

---

# THE DOUBLE SENTENCE

Connor and Craig's court outcomes should occur close enough to form one final movement.

## Connor

Connor finally receives **five years**.

After months of imagining ten, twelve and fifteen, five creates a morally strange reaction: relief.

He knows five years is enormous.

It still feels merciful compared with the sentence he has been serving in his imagination.

His Category C allocation becomes real.

## Craig

Craig's contested case ends badly for him.

He receives a major sentence.

The number should be researched and locked later based on the final charge facts, but everybody understands immediately that Craig is moving deeper into the system.

The dispersal/high-security estate is now real rather than rumour.

Big Sharky's shadow becomes more important at exactly the point Craig is leaving Lewes.

---

# CONNOR'S FINAL TEST

Before departure, Connor should face a situation deliberately echoing the September attack.

In September his body moved before his brain and prison named him Rush.

Near the end, something happens where the old version of Rush would automatically step in, speak, threaten, volunteer or involve himself.

This time Connor hears Mason's line internally:

**Hear all. See all. Say nothing.**

He pauses.

He does not necessarily become passive or cowardly. The point is that for the first time he **chooses** rather than simply reacts.

That choice proves an internal change more important than any nickname.

---

# FINAL LEWES CONVERSATION — CONNOR AND CRAIG

Both know they are leaving.

Do not turn this into a sentimental goodbye speech.

Craig still calls him Connor.

Connor has spent months becoming Rush, but Craig's refusal to use the nickname now lands differently.

Connor understands that Craig saw the person before Connor became obsessed with the reputation.

Connor can ask one final version of the Big Sharky question.

Craig gives him enough to prove there is a real history but not enough to resolve it.

Book One must pay off Craig emotionally without solving Craig intellectually.

---

# FINAL DEPARTURE SEQUENCE

Use cross-cutting.

### CONNOR

Property. Waiting. Transport. Category C destination.

He is no longer the young man who arrived at Lewes frightened by every noise.

That is both reassuring and worrying.

### CRAIG

Seen from outside only.

His departure creates immediate myth-making behind him.

Everyone already has a different version of what Craig's sentence means.

### BRUCE

Bruce gets one last strange appearance or line that seems like comic relief but hints that his own road may eventually take him into the same high-security world as Craig.

Do not explain how yet.

### DARREN

Alive, carrying Josh.

### JAMA / Jamo

Connor and Jamo should have at least a partial reconciliation before Connor leaves. Not a reset to how they were, but recognition that both hurt the friendship.

### TYLER

Still frightened sometimes, but less dependent on pretending he is somebody else.

### MASON

Returns to prison routine, making Connor wonder whether Mason has watched hundreds of versions of this departure.

---

# END IMAGE / BOOK TWO HANDOFF

Two prison vehicles / two destinations / two futures.

Connor heads toward Category C sentenced life.

Craig heads toward the dispersal/high-security estate.

Book One closes the Lewes stage.

Book Two opens the new worlds.

For the first time in the series, Craig can receive the heading the reader has been denied throughout Book One:

# CRAIG

# Character arc checklist

## Connor — transformation

Connor Blake → frightened remand prisoner → Rush → enjoys borrowed status → gets used → becomes Craig-adjacent → learns the scale of Craig's world → damages friendships → overreaches → begins choosing rather than reacting → five years / Cat C.

## Craig — reputation

Security transfer into Lewes → threat closes around him → Connor backs him → mentor bond → court absences / myth grows → Leah compromised → helps remove Bennett pressure → major sentence → dispersal.

No POV.

## Mason — institutionalisation

Experienced guide → gives title advice → watches Connor become Rush → reveals cost of being too comfortable inside → remains long-view conscience.

## Jamo — adaptation / jealousy / justified resentment

Education → trusted-job hope → Connor's chaos damages opportunity → jealousy + legitimate anger → moves out → later partial reconciliation.

## Stuart — faith / injustice / contamination

Remand student → Connor's cellmate → moral influence → pushed out → deteriorates → acquitted → leaves changed.

## Darren — addiction / informing / guilt

Dependency → Grant pressure → minor informing → consequences → mentors Josh → normalises damaged world → Josh dies → Darren survives with responsibility → first meaningful refusal to pretend nothing matters.

## Josh — belonging

Energetic young vehicle offender → attaches to Darren → becomes loved by cast/reader → gradual deterioration → fatal medical collapse → remains emotionally present through Darren.

## Grant — punishment/control

Hard-line officer → exploits Darren as source → believes methods are justified → creates collateral damage → remains a morally serious institutional antagonist rather than cartoon villain.

## Leah — attachment / compromise

Professional distance → curiosity → attachment → boundary crossings → recognises Bennett → uses Craig to help him → remains compromised / unresolved.

## Bennett — fear

Capable officer → coercion → small compromises → trapped → Leah notices → immediate pressure reduced through Craig's influence → career/moral future unresolved.

## Malik — information

Knowing things feels powerful → knowledge becomes dangerous → spots leak pattern → learns not all information should move → deliberately keeps something back.

## Jinx — leadership

Outside-linked confidence → Craig pressure → Rico fallout → informer suspicion → cooperation with Deano → chooses people inside over commands from outside.

## Deano — politics

Wants separation → forced into shared problems → cooperates with Jinx when interests overlap → learns neutrality between groups is temporary and practical.

## Leon — routine

Gym / discipline → politics repeatedly interrupts control → neutrality becomes interpreted → chooses conscience over perfect routine.

## Macca — absence

Family calls / visits / missed life → prison disruptions repeatedly damage home contact → emotional payoff based on what cannot be recovered.

## Tyler — fear/performance

Mouthy first-timer → invents reputation → lie becomes expectation → persona collapses → admits fear → begins becoming more authentic.

## Yusuf — coexistence

Fun-loving bridge → liked across groups → conflicting loyalty demands → chooses not to be owned by either side.

## Bruce — absurdity / hidden usefulness

Appears from nowhere → names Rush → says only-Bruce things → knows unexpected people/details → remains in-and-out → final hint that his later road will intersect Craig's high-security world.

## Big Sharky — unfinished history

Voice / calls only → attaches threat to Craig → failed attempt does not resolve feud → Craig's dispersal destination brings future collision closer.

No interior POV.

5. Introduce Josh as joyful before any deterioration.
6. Let Jamo's trusted-job loss grow directly from the consequences around Connor rather than generic jealousy.
7. Move into Stuart only after the Jamo friendship breaks.
8. By November, ensure Craig can disappear for stretches while other characters carry whole chapters.
9. Make Christmas a substantial emotional ensemble movement.
10. Reach the double sentence / double departure in February 2020.
