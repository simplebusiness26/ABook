# TUESDAY 5 NOVEMBER 2019 — STUART

## CONNOR — C WING

Connor's single cell lasted one day and two nights.

He considered this a personal attack by the prison service.

"Blake."

Connor looked up from the bottom bunk.

Officer Mercer stood at the door.

Behind her was a man Connor had seen around but never spoken to properly.

Slim. Early twenties. Neatly folded property. Calm face that did not quite manage to hide how tired he looked.

"You've got a new cellmate," Mercer said.

Connor stared at her.

"No I haven't."

Mercer looked behind herself as if checking the man had not vanished.

"Interesting. Because he appears to be standing here."

Connor sighed.

"I just got the cell to myself."

"Congratulations on your historic reign. It's over."

The man behind her smiled.

Connor noticed and immediately disliked him for enjoying it.

"Stuart," the man said.

Connor moved his things off the spare bunk.

"Connor."

Mercer looked at him.

"See? Social skills. Knew you had some."

"Cheers, miss."

She left them to it.

Stuart stepped inside and looked around the cell carefully.

Not fearfully.

More like somebody taking inventory of a place he had not chosen.

"Which space is yours?" he asked.

Connor pointed vaguely.

"Most of it."

Stuart nodded.

"Then we will negotiate."

Connor looked at him.

"Negotiate?"

"Yes. Two men, one small room. It seems sensible."

Connor smiled despite himself.

"You sound like a solicitor."

"I am a student."

"What, law?"

"No."

"Shame. Could've done with one."

Stuart laughed.

The sound was warmer than Connor expected.

---

## STUART

Stuart had already learned one prison truth.

People asked what you were in for before asking almost anything else.

Connor lasted nineteen minutes.

That was better than average.

"So what happened with you?"

Stuart looked up from arranging his books.

"My case?"

"Yeah."

Stuart paused.

He had learned to be careful with this too.

Not because he was ashamed.

Because every retelling changed the listener before it changed the story.

"There was an incident where I lived," he said. "Student accommodation. Another man attacked me. There were scissors. I was injured in my hand. I defended myself."

Connor looked at him.

"And you're in here?"

"Yes."

"For defending yourself?"

"The prosecution does not describe it exactly that way."

Connor nodded slowly.

"Course they don't."

Stuart smiled faintly.

"You have great faith in the legal system."

Connor laughed.

"You ain't heard my case yet."

"Aggravated burglary."

Connor frowned.

"Who told you?"

"This is prison."

Connor smiled.

"Fair."

Stuart put a small Bible beside his bed.

Connor noticed.

"You religious?"

"Christian."

"Proper?"

Stuart looked amused.

"What is improper Christian?"

"Dunno. Christmas and emergencies."

Stuart laughed.

"Then yes. Proper."

Connor nodded at the Bible.

"You gonna start preaching at me?"

"Not unless you begin asking questions you do not want answered."

Connor stared at him.

"That's annoyingly prison-y."

"I have been learning."

---

## CONNOR

By afternoon Connor had discovered Stuart prayed before eating.

Quietly.

No performance.

No announcement.

The first time, Connor waited until he finished.

"You pray over prison food?"

Stuart looked at the tray.

"This food needs more prayer than most."

Connor laughed.

That helped.

Later Stuart asked:

"Why do they call you Rush?"

Connor groaned.

"Long story."

"It is prison. We have time."

Connor told the short version.

Craig.

The attack.

Connor moving before thinking.

Getting knocked away.

Going back beside him.

Bruce naming him afterwards.

Stuart listened without the reaction Connor had become used to.

No impressed whistle.

No grin.

No immediate acceptance of the legend.

"And you like the name?" Stuart asked.

Connor shrugged.

"It's all right."

"But your name is Connor."

"Obviously."

Stuart nodded.

"Then I will call you Connor."

Connor laughed.

"You and Craig."

"Craig calls you Connor?"

"Yeah."

Stuart considered that.

"Maybe he knows something."

Connor rolled his eyes.

"Don't start making him sound spiritual."

"I said nothing about him."

"That's even worse."

Stuart smiled and went back to reading.

Connor looked at him for a moment.

He had expected the new cellmate to irritate him.

Stuart had managed it, but not in the way Connor planned.

---

## JAMO

Jamo saw Stuart carrying property into Connor's cell and felt something stupid twist in his stomach.

It was his old cell for less than forty-eight hours.

Already somebody else was in his chair.

Not literally.

Jamo had checked.

Connor had kept his word and Bruce had not been allowed to claim it.

That somehow made it worse.

Connor spotted Jamo on the landing later.

For half a second both nearly looked away.

Then Connor nodded.

"All right?"

"Yeah."

"New cell all right?"

"Yeah. Yours?"

Connor glanced back.

"Got a preacher."

Jamo laughed before he could stop himself.

"What?"

"Christian student. Stuart."

"That'll do you good."

"Fuck off."

The old rhythm appeared for about three seconds.

Then both noticed it.

Jamo looked away first.

"See you later."

"Yeah."

They went in opposite directions.

Neither friendship nor argument had ended.

They had simply been given different rooms.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer passed Connor's cell near lock-up and heard laughter.

She slowed.

Stuart was saying something about forgiveness.

Connor replied:

"I forgive loads of people. Just not quickly."

"That may be the opposite of forgiveness."

"Then they should've behaved better."

Stuart laughed.

Mercer carried on before either saw her.

Cell allocations were administrative decisions.

Beds, spaces, risk, practicality.

Staff liked to believe the process ended there.

It did not.

Put two men in a box for enough hours and each became part of the other's sentence.

Sometimes that was disastrous.

Sometimes it saved them.

Usually it was both in smaller amounts.

---

## STUART — NIGHT

After the door shut, Stuart prayed quietly beside his bunk.

Connor lay above him.

"You do that every night?"

"Most nights."

"What do you ask for?"

Stuart looked up.

"Different things."

"Like getting out?"

"Sometimes."

"Winning your case?"

"Yes."

Connor waited.

"What else?"

Stuart thought for a moment.

"Not becoming someone I would not recognise because I am angry about being here."

Connor stared at the ceiling.

That answer irritated him for reasons he could not explain.

"Bit deep before bed."

"You asked."

Connor smiled.

"Fair."

Silence settled.

Then Stuart said:

"What do you ask for?"

Connor laughed softly.

"I don't pray."

"That was not my question."

Connor stopped smiling.

Mum.

Little brother.

Five years becoming less.

Girlfriend waiting.

Craig respecting him.

Rush becoming real without swallowing Connor completely.

There were plenty of things.

He said none of them.

"Sleep," Connor said.

Stuart smiled in the dark.

"Then I hope you get some."

Connor closed his eyes.

Annoying bloke, he thought.

He slept better than he had alone.
