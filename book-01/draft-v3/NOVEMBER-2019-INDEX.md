# November 2019 — Draft V3 Index

**Status: NOVEMBER COMPLETE.**

November is the transition into the full ensemble middle of Book One. Craig remains important, but the prison increasingly carries whole chapters through other people.

## Chapters drafted

- `27-01-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **THE PERSON YOU SAID YOU WERE**
  - Tyler's invented cousin reputation produces a real expectation.
  - Connor recognises Tyler making the same sort of ego-driven mistake Connor made with Kez.
  - Tyler's mum punctures the prison persona by calling him Ty and asking ordinary caring questions.
  - Josh's love of cars becomes part of his energetic identity.
  - Connor/Jamo tension continues rather than exploding immediately.
  - Officer Mercer notices newer prisoners beginning to look to Connor for guidance.

- `28-03-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **ROOM FOR TWO**
  - Connor/Jamo finally have the argument the whole of October has been building toward.
  - Jamo admits jealousy without allowing Connor to use it to erase his legitimate grievance.
  - Jamo reveals he applied to move before the argument.
  - The prison abruptly grants the move because a bed becomes available.
  - Connor gets the single cell he thinks he wants and discovers the quiet is not entirely a reward.
  - Darren/Josh continue to become an ordinary, affectionate pair in the background.

- `29-05-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **STUART**
  - Stuart is allocated into Connor's cell after Jamo leaves.
  - His self-defence account, university background and Christian faith are introduced without graphic detail.
  - Stuart immediately insists on calling him Connor rather than Rush.
  - Their humour begins alongside the moral/spiritual contrast.
  - Connor and Jamo have their first awkward post-cellmate interaction, showing the friendship is damaged rather than destroyed.
  - Officer Mercer observes how cell allocations quietly reshape sentences.

- `30-08-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **SMALL THINGS**
  - Officer Grant pressures Darren again.
  - Darren gives a small piece of information he immediately regrets.
  - A later staff intervention around Jinx's wider circle may or may not have resulted from Darren's words.
  - Darren is trapped by not knowing whether he caused it.
  - Malik notices the timing but deliberately resists immediately spreading an accusation.
  - Jinx and Deano recognise that suspicion of informing is becoming its own threat.
  - Josh/Darren/Bruce keep the chapter human and funny through Josh's car obsession.

- `31-10-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **LAYERS**
  - Connor casually mentions an outside name Craig recognises.
  - Craig reveals that Connor's old cannabis world sat inside a much larger network Connor could not see.
  - The key lesson is that Connor "ran the bit he could see" rather than genuinely understanding the whole system around him.
  - Craig refuses to let Connor romanticise the discovery into proof that he was secretly important.
  - Stuart reframes the revelation morally rather than criminally.
  - Malik notices Connor learning to keep information to himself.
  - Bruce explains organised crime through a damaged onion metaphor because Bruce remains Bruce.

- `32-12-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **WHAT PEOPLE KNOW**
  - Officer Bennett's coercion/fear becomes visibly harder to hide.
  - Senior Officer Patel and Officer Mercer both begin noticing behavioural changes.
  - Tyler's fake reputation tightens when the prisoner who believed him comes back expecting results.
  - Bruce gives Tyler bizarre but accurate advice.
  - Connor gives Tyler the lesson he learned from Kez: stop creating a new lie to protect the last one.
  - Tyler manages his first small honest admission to his mum: prison is not always easy.
  - Craig independently notices Bennett's fear.
  - Officer Mercer notices Craig noticing Bennett, deepening her dangerous curiosity about what Craig sees.

- `33-14-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **THE CHEAPER TRUTH**
  - Tyler finally admits he exaggerated his cousin's influence.
  - The feared confrontation becomes forty seconds of embarrassment rather than catastrophe.
  - Connor's lesson to Tyler lands: sometimes truth is cheaper than maintaining a lie.
  - Jamo discovers moving cells did not remove Connor from his sentence and privately admits he misses him.
  - Stuart and Connor deepen through a conversation about family, sentence fear and the time prison takes.
  - Macca learns his daughter is a star in her school Christmas play.
  - Officer Mercer's professional curiosity about Craig becomes increasingly hard to separate from personal curiosity.

- `34-16-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **THE WRONG HOUR**
  - A routine wing lockdown becomes the clearest "one event, many prisons" chapter yet.
  - Macca misses the exact phone call he was waiting for from his daughter.
  - Leon loses gym and feels his routine/control disrupted.
  - Jamo is trapped behind a door with his new cellmate and his own thoughts.
  - Darren gets through a difficult locked-down hour partly because Josh will not stop talking.
  - Malik hears multiple rumours but deliberately repeats none of them.
  - Connor/Stuart recognise that the same closed door creates completely different prisons for different men.
  - Officer Mercer later helps Macca get a delayed call, but his daughter is already asleep.

- `35-18-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **NO PROOF**
  - Rumours begin attaching Darren to Officer Grant because prisoners have noticed the office meetings.
  - Malik possesses enough fragments to sound convincing but not enough to know the truth.
  - Yusuf forces Malik to confront the difference between suspicion and proof.
  - Jinx and Deano deliberately refuse to accuse Darren without certainty.
  - Darren realises Josh is learning to read prison atmosphere as well as routine.
  - Grant frames information-giving as responsibility; Darren experiences the same pressure as entrapment.
  - Malik chooses not to turn Darren's name into wing truth.
  - The title line matures from passive silence into responsible restraint.

- `36-20-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **TWO NAMES**
  - Stuart notices Connor answers differently to Connor and Rush.
  - Their conversation asks whether Rush is simply a nickname or a version of Connor he is beginning to prefer.
  - Jamo watches Connor/Stuart and realises he misses Connor rather than the trouble that came with sharing a cell.
  - Connor and Jamo share biscuits and the first genuine bridge back toward friendship.
  - Officer Mercer notices that Craig and Stuart both use Connor's real name.
  - Mercer/Craig remain outwardly formal: **Officer Mercer** is still the only name Craig knows on page.
  - Connor ends the chapter choosing not to answer a distant shout of Rush because, for once, he is already where he needs to be.

- `37-23-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **SIDES**
  - Yusuf's social ease becomes a liability when Jinx and Deano both expect him to validate their version of a dispute.
  - Yusuf refuses to become an unpaid messenger/witness for everybody who likes him.
  - Bruce gives unexpectedly useful advice: become unreliable for politics without becoming dishonest.
  - Leon discovers even a gym list can become political.
  - Connor starts seeing the weaknesses and contradictions in people who looked completely established when he arrived.
  - Jinx and Deano finally speak directly and stop a fresh suspicion spiral rather than using Yusuf as a route between them.

- `38-26-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **THE GATE**
  - Officer Bennett's fear now visibly follows him through the gate and into the staff car park/home life.
  - Officer Mercer challenges him directly; he nearly talks but retreats into "I'm fine."
  - Senior Officer Patel confirms that Bennett's pattern has moved beyond ordinary tiredness.
  - Jamo receives another visits-orderly knock-back for a reason unrelated to Connor and is forced to reassess how much blame he placed on him.
  - Connor and Jamo finally say they are all right without pretending neither was at fault.
  - Tyler discovers his cousin embarrassment has already begun fading into old news.
  - Officer Mercer asks Craig what he has noticed about Bennett; Craig gives only the observation that Bennett looks frightened.
  - Mercer remains professionally inside the line while moving emotionally closer to it.

- `39-30-NOVEMBER-2019.md` — **BEFORE CHRISTMAS**
  - Christmas begins changing the emotional weather of the prison.
  - Connor speaks properly with home and realises money cannot replace presence with his little brother.
  - His girlfriend sounds slightly more distant, but Connor resists inventing an explanation.
  - Jamo and Connor's friendship becomes ordinary again without returning to the old cell.
  - Stuart receives uncertain university news, reinforcing that acquittal would not magically restore lost time.
  - Darren and Josh remain primarily warm and funny; Josh still sees prison as an interruption rather than a life identity.
  - Grant's silence itself becomes pressure on Darren.
  - Malik asks Mason what "say nothing" means when silence might also cause harm; Mason clarifies that the rule is about distinguishing information from noise, not permanent silence.
  - Yusuf's refusal to carry everybody's politics works.
  - Leon recognises routine as borrowed control rather than permanent control.
  - Macca receives his daughter's school-play drawing and the line: **"DAD I WILL DO IT LOUD SO YOU CAN HEAR."**
  - Officer Mercer admits to herself that her curiosity about Craig has a direction even though no explicit boundary has yet been crossed.
  - Connor closes November understanding that there is no single answer to "what is prison like?"

## November completion state

By 30 November:

- Connor is comfortable as Rush but increasingly aware that Connor and Rush are not automatically the same thing.
- Jamo has moved out, admitted jealousy, experienced disappointment independent of Connor, and begun rebuilding the friendship.
- Stuart is established as Connor's warm but morally challenging cellmate.
- Darren and Josh are firmly a genuine, affectionate pair before any later deterioration.
- Darren is frightened that Grant's use of information may have consequences and is becoming socially vulnerable to suspicion.
- Malik has made his first major choice to withhold potentially damaging information without proof.
- Jinx and Deano have moved from parallel suspicion toward practical cooperation.
- Yusuf has learned that being liked by everybody can become a demand to serve everybody.
- Tyler has punctured his own fake reputation and become more sympathetic and honest.
- Leon's routine/control worldview has been tested by regime disruption and petty politics.
- Macca's family story is positioned to make Christmas emotionally significant.
- Officer Bennett's coercion is visibly worsening.
- Officer Mercer is increasingly curious about Craig while remaining outwardly professional.
- Craig has been able to disappear for stretches without the book losing momentum, proving the ensemble can now carry itself.

6. Officer Bennett's outside pressure escalates toward a decision point.
