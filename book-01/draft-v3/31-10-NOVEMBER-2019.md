# SUNDAY 10 NOVEMBER 2019 — LAYERS

## CONNOR — C WING

Connor found out by accident.

That made it worse.

He had always imagined important truths arriving properly.

Somebody sits you down.

Somebody lowers their voice.

Somebody says *you need to know this*.

Instead Connor was eating cereal while Craig looked at a name on a scrap of paper Connor had been carrying around in his head for weeks.

They were talking about nothing important.

That was the beginning.

Connor had mentioned an older bloke from outside who used to occasionally sort problems around the cannabis line.

Not somebody Connor worked for directly.

Somebody above somebody.

The sort of name Connor had once used because it made him sound more connected than he really was.

Craig stopped eating.

"Say that again."

Connor looked at him.

"What?"

"The name."

Connor repeated it.

Craig leaned back slightly.

Not shocked.

Thinking.

Connor noticed.

"You know him?"

Craig looked at Connor for a second.

"I know of him."

Connor smiled.

"Knew it. Everyone knows everyone."

Craig did not smile back.

"Who did you think he worked for?"

Connor shrugged.

"Himself, I suppose."

"And the people above him?"

Connor frowned.

"What people above him?"

Craig looked at him.

That look did something unpleasant to Connor's confidence.

"Connor."

"What?"

"You thought your line started with you?"

Connor laughed defensively.

"No. Obviously not."

Craig waited.

Connor hated that wait.

"I knew people supplied it."

"Who supplied them?"

"Dunno."

"And them?"

Connor's smile faded.

Craig nodded once.

"Exactly."

Connor stared at him.

"What are you saying?"

Craig looked down the wing before answering.

"I'm saying you ran the bit you could see."

Connor said nothing.

The sentence irritated him immediately because it was true in a way he had never considered.

Outside, Connor had thought of himself as running something.

His phone.

His runners.

His money.

His problems.

His line.

The word *his* had done a lot of work.

"That name you just said," Craig continued, "he sits under people I know."

Connor's chest tightened.

"Under you?"

Craig looked at him.

"Don't make it into a film."

"I'm asking."

"And I'm answering enough."

Connor leaned forward.

"So the weed I was selling—"

"Don't know where every bag came from. Neither do you."

Craig's tone stayed calm.

"But the network you were touching was bigger than you realised. Much bigger."

Connor sat back.

For a moment he felt embarrassed.

Then impressed.

Then embarrassed that he felt impressed.

"So I was basically working for you?"

Craig frowned.

"No."

Connor laughed.

"Come on."

"You didn't know me. I didn't know you. Don't rewrite your life because you've learned one new thing."

That landed harder than Connor expected.

Craig continued:

"People love finding out there was a bigger machine above them because it makes their own little part feel important. Sometimes it just means they were further down than they thought."

Connor stared at him.

"You always know how to ruin things."

Craig smiled faintly.

"Useful skill."

---

## MALIK

Malik saw Connor leave Craig looking different.

Not frightened.

Not angry.

Smaller somehow.

That interested him.

"What'd he tell you?" Malik asked later.

Connor looked at him.

"Nothing."

Malik smiled.

"That's new."

"What is?"

"You keeping something."

Connor leaned on the rail.

"Maybe Mason's finally got through to me."

"Impossible."

Connor laughed.

Malik waited for the rest.

It did not come.

That interested him even more.

Information was usually Connor's favourite toy.

Today he was holding it differently.

Malik thought about the staff intervention two days earlier.

About suspicion.

About how quickly information changed shape once it left the person who first said it.

Maybe Connor was learning the same thing from a different direction.

---

## STUART

Stuart knew something had happened because Connor cleaned the same patch of table three times.

"You are thinking."

Connor did not look up.

"Dangerous, I know."

"Very. Should I call someone?"

Connor smiled.

Stuart waited.

He had learned Connor filled silence eventually.

"You ever realise you weren't as important as you thought?" Connor asked.

Stuart closed his book.

"Frequently."

Connor looked at him.

"That's depressing."

"It can also be peaceful."

"How?"

"Because then not everything has to be about you."

Connor rolled his eyes.

"There it is. Church answer."

"You asked a church man."

Connor sat opposite him.

"Turns out stuff I was involved in outside was bigger than I knew."

Stuart nodded.

"And this pleased you?"

Connor frowned.

"Why would it please me?"

Stuart smiled.

"Because your face did something strange when you said bigger."

Connor laughed.

"Everyone in this prison thinks they're a face expert."

"Maybe you have a very readable face."

Connor leaned back.

"Craig says I ran the bit I could see."

"That sounds wise."

"Don't encourage him."

"I have never met him properly."

"Still."

Stuart looked at Connor for a moment.

"Maybe prison is showing you how many things you believed you controlled because you only saw a small piece."

Connor stared at him.

"Do you practise being annoying?"

"No. Natural gift."

Connor laughed.

Stuart opened his book again.

Connor looked down at his hands.

He had spent years wanting to be bigger.

Now he had discovered he had always been part of something bigger.

Those were not the same thing.

---

## BRUCE

Bruce heard Connor had discovered "layers" by lunchtime despite nobody remembering telling him.

He found Connor near the servery.

"Congratulations."

Connor frowned.

"For what?"

"Finding out the world existed before you."

Connor stared at him.

"Who told you?"

Bruce ignored the question.

"It's like onions."

"What is?"

"Crime."

Connor closed his eyes.

"Please don't."

"Layers. Makes you cry. Bad breath."

"That doesn't even work."

"Works enough."

Connor laughed.

Bruce looked pleased.

"Also, if someone says they're the top layer of an onion, they're lying because onions don't have tops."

Connor stared at him.

Bruce nodded solemnly.

"Think about it."

"I absolutely won't."

Bruce walked away.

Connor watched him go.

There were days Bruce sounded like a complete idiot.

There were other days he sounded like a prophet whose messages had been damaged in transit.

---

## CONNOR — NIGHT

That night Connor thought about his old life differently.

Not morally.

Not yet.

Structurally.

He pictured the lads who ran for him.

Then himself.

Then the people he dealt with.

Then names above those names.

Then Craig somewhere far beyond the point Connor had ever seen.

The picture kept expanding until Connor stopped trying to finish it.

Stuart was reading below.

"You awake?" Connor asked.

"Yes."

"You reckon knowing you're small makes you smarter?"

Stuart thought.

"Only if you stop pretending immediately afterwards."

Connor smiled in the dark.

"You're annoying even when you're right."

"Thank you."

Connor closed his eyes.

He had entered prison believing he understood criminal life.

Every week Lewes found another way to show him the edge of what he had never even known to ask.
