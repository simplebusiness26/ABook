# WEDNESDAY 2 OCTOBER 2019 — USEFUL

## CONNOR — C WING

The first favour came disguised as a compliment.

Connor did not realise that until later.

At the time, all he heard was:

"You seem switched on."

He was standing near the phones waiting for Jamo, who had disappeared to chase an education officer about a form he had already submitted twice.

The man speaking to Connor was called Kez.

Connor knew that much.

Kez was one of those prisoners Jamo had described with a sentence that somehow explained nothing.

*He's all right, but just know what you're saying around him.*

Connor had asked what that meant.

Jamo had replied, *Exactly what I said.*

Now Kez leaned against the wall as if the conversation had happened accidentally.

"Heard about Sunday."

Connor sighed.

"Everyone has."

"You did well."

Connor shrugged.

"Just happened."

"That's what people say when they want you to know they didn't shit themselves."

Connor laughed.

Kez smiled.

"No, seriously. Most people would've gone the other way."

Connor heard Craig's voice in the sentence.

*Most people move the other way.*

He liked hearing it from somebody else more than he should have.

"Wasn't thinking."

"Sometimes that's better."

Kez looked down the landing.

"You got visits this week?"

Connor's smile faded slightly.

"Maybe. Why?"

"Just asking."

Connor waited.

Kez waited too.

Prison conversations had developed a new layer since Sunday.

People said ordinary sentences and left little doors open inside them.

Connor was starting to notice.

Not always quickly enough.

"What do you want?" he said.

Kez smiled again.

"See? Switched on."

There was the compliment again.

Connor felt himself stand a little straighter.

---

Kez did not explain everything there.

That should have been Connor's warning.

Instead it made him feel included.

There was a favour connected to an upcoming visit.

Something prohibited was expected to move through somebody else's arrangement, and Connor was being asked to play a small part because, as Kez put it, "No one's looking at you like that yet."

Connor heard the sentence as trust.

It wasn't.

It was arithmetic.

New prisoner.

Useful reputation.

Eager to prove he wasn't scared.

No established disciplinary history beyond Sunday's incident.

Connor did not think through any of those things.

He thought:

*They rate me.*

That was enough.

"What exactly am I doing?" he asked.

Kez lowered his voice.

Connor listened.

The details themselves mattered less than the feeling that followed them.

Connor's mouth went dry.

"That's it?"

"That's it."

"And if something goes wrong?"

Kez looked almost amused.

"Why would it?"

Connor hated that answer immediately.

People only said *why would it?* when they had already decided the consequences belonged to somebody else.

Still, Connor nodded.

"Yeah. All right."

Kez clapped him lightly on the shoulder.

"Knew you were sound."

Connor watched him walk off.

His stomach had already started disagreeing with his mouth.

## JAMO

Jamo came back carrying three pieces of paper and the expression of a man betrayed by government.

"They lost it."

Connor looked over.

"Lost what?"

"My visits orderly application."

"Again?"

"Apparently it 'isn't on the system.'"

Jamo made quotation marks with his fingers so aggressively Connor thought he might injure himself.

"I handed it to them."

"Maybe the system ate it."

"Don't."

"Hungry system."

"Connor."

Connor laughed.

Jamo dropped the papers onto the table.

"I've done another one. Education said they'll back me."

"You'll get it."

"You reckon?"

"Yeah."

Jamo looked suspicious.

"Why are you being nice?"

"Can't I be nice?"

"Not naturally."

Connor smiled.

Jamo sat opposite.

"You all right?"

"Yeah."

"You're doing that face."

"What face?"

"The one where you've agreed to something stupid."

Connor laughed too quickly.

"What are you on about?"

Jamo stared at him.

Connor looked away.

"Nothing."

"Who've you been talking to?"

"No one."

"Connor."

"Jamo."

"You're a shit liar."

Connor leaned back.

"Hear all. See all. Say nothing, remember?"

Jamo's face changed.

"That's not what Mason meant."

Connor smiled.

"You don't know that."

"I know he definitely didn't mean use it to hide stupid things from your padmate."

Connor got up.

"I'm getting tea."

"It's two in the afternoon."

"Early tea."

Jamo watched him walk away.

Then looked at the visits orderly application on the table.

For the first time, Connor's secrets were starting to feel as if they might become Jamo's problem too.

## DARREN

Darren avoided Grant for most of the day.

Not dramatically.

He did not duck behind doors or sprint in the opposite direction.

He simply developed an intense interest in being wherever Grant wasn't.

Laundry problem on the other side of the wing?

Darren was suddenly fascinated by laundry.

Someone arguing at the servery?

Darren needed to know every detail.

Macca complaining about a phone issue?

Darren became supportive in a way that confused Macca.

"You all right?" Macca asked.

"Yeah."

"You've been standing here five minutes while I moan about a phone."

"I'm listening."

"You never listen."

Darren shrugged.

"New me."

Macca looked past him.

Grant was walking onto the landing.

Macca looked back at Darren.

"Ah."

Darren frowned.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Why'd you say ah?"

"Didn't."

"You did."

"Must've been wind."

Darren stared at him.

"Jamo's saying that now."

"Popular line."

Grant called from farther down.

"Darren."

Darren's shoulders dropped.

Macca looked at him.

"You in trouble?"

"Apparently I exist. That's enough for him."

He walked toward Grant.

---

Grant did not ask about Sunday this time.

He asked about phones.

Not a specific hidden phone.

Who was always around the wing phones.

Who got unusual calls.

Who seemed to know things from outside before everybody else.

The questions were vague enough that Darren could answer them without feeling like he had informed on anybody.

That was what Darren told himself.

"Malik knows everything," he said with a shrug.

Grant gave nothing away.

"Everybody says that."

"Then why ask me?"

"Because everybody sees different things."

Darren looked at him.

For one strange second Mason's phrase appeared in his mind.

Hear all.

See all.

Say nothing.

Darren almost smiled.

Different teacher.

Different lesson.

Grant nodded toward the door.

"You can go."

Darren stood.

"That's it?"

"That's it."

Darren walked out feeling relieved.

Then less relieved.

He replayed what he had said.

Malik knows everything.

Everybody knew that.

That wasn't information.

Was it?

He decided it wasn't.

That decision made the rest of the afternoon easier.

## BRUCE

Bruce had acquired a spoon.

This should not have been interesting.

It became interesting because Bruce was walking around asking everyone whether the spoon looked "institutional".

"It's a spoon," Yusuf said.

"That's not what I asked."

"What does institutional mean for a spoon?"

Bruce held it up to the light.

"Look at it. It's got no hope."

Yusuf stared at him.

"You think spoons have hope?"

"Good ones do."

Connor walked past.

Bruce pointed the spoon at him.

"Rush. Opinion."

Connor stopped.

"On what?"

"This spoon."

"It's a spoon."

Bruce looked disappointed.

"Same answer as Yusuf. Groupthink."

Connor laughed and carried on.

Bruce watched him go.

Then looked at Yusuf.

"He's doing something."

Yusuf raised an eyebrow.

"Because of how he reviewed your spoon?"

"No. Because he's walking like his stomach's following him half a second later."

Yusuf looked after Connor.

"You are occasionally disturbingly observant."

Bruce put the spoon into his pocket.

"Ruins the brand if I do it too often."

## CONNOR

By evening Connor had convinced himself the favour was small.

He did this carefully.

First he compared it with Sunday.

Sunday had involved actual bodies moving at Craig.

This was just a favour.

Then he compared it with his case.

Aggravated burglary.

Blood in a C63.

Firearm allegation.

Compared with that, this felt tiny.

Then he compared himself with everyone else.

People in prison did things for each other constantly.

That was normal.

Then he stopped comparing altogether and just told himself:

*Don't be a pussy.*

That one worked best because it required no evidence.

Craig found him near the end of association.

"You look busy."

Connor's stomach tightened.

"Do I?"

"No."

Connor stared at him.

Craig smiled slightly.

"That's why I said it."

Connor laughed.

They stood for a moment watching the wing.

"People treating you different?" Craig asked.

Connor shrugged.

"Bit."

"You like it?"

Connor looked at him.

"What?"

"The name. People knowing you."

"It's funny."

"Didn't ask if it was funny."

Connor looked away.

Craig waited.

Connor hated that.

"Yeah," he said eventually. "Bit."

Craig nodded.

No lecture.

No judgement.

That was worse somehow.

"What?" Connor said.

"Nothing."

"You keep doing that."

"Doing what?"

"Looking like you know something."

Craig smiled.

"Maybe I do."

Then he walked away.

Connor watched him go.

For one second he considered telling Craig about Kez.

Then the thought embarrassed him.

What was he going to say?

*Someone asked me to do a favour and I'm nervous?*

Rush wasn't nervous.

Connor was.

That distinction was becoming useful.
