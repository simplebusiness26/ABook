# FRIDAY 8 NOVEMBER 2019 — SMALL THINGS

## DARREN — C WING

Darren had started measuring good days by how little happened.

No argument.

No debt conversation.

No officer calling his name.

No craving strong enough to rearrange the whole afternoon.

No one looking at him like they knew something.

Friday had almost managed it.

Then Officer Grant appeared.

"Darren."

Josh looked over immediately.

Darren hated that.

Not Josh looking.

What the look did to him.

Concern had become another thing Darren had to manage.

"What?" Darren said.

Grant's expression stayed flat.

"Office."

Josh frowned.

"You in trouble?"

Darren smiled.

"Always."

"Seriously."

"No. Go annoy Bruce."

Josh looked unconvinced.

Darren pointed across the wing.

"Go."

Josh went.

Slowly.

Looking back once.

Darren followed Grant.

---

## OFFICER GRANT

Grant did not think of Darren as an informant.

Not in the dramatic way prisoners did.

There were no films in Grant's head.

No secret identity.

No cloak-and-dagger nonsense.

There was information inside a prison and there were people willing to give pieces of it for different reasons.

Fear.

Anger.

Attention.

Self-interest.

Sometimes because they genuinely believed something bad was about to happen.

Grant cared about outcomes.

That was how he justified most things.

Darren sat opposite him.

"I said no last time."

"I remember."

"Then why am I here?"

Grant folded his arms.

"Because things change."

Darren laughed once.

"Not that quick."

Grant ignored it.

"There's tension around Rico's lot."

Darren looked away.

"There's always tension."

"One name keeps coming up."

Darren stayed quiet.

Grant waited.

Darren hated that part most.

Grant rarely needed to fill silence.

People did it for him.

"I'm not giving you names," Darren said.

"I didn't ask you for one."

"You were about to."

Grant almost smiled.

"All right. Don't."

Darren stared at him.

Too easy again.

Grant continued:

"Tell me if I'm wrong then. Is it getting worse?"

That felt smaller.

Safer.

Not a name.

Not an accusation.

Darren thought about what he had heard.

People angry about Rico.

People suspicious.

People blaming the wrong men for the right reasons.

"Yeah," Darren said finally. "Bit."

Grant nodded.

"Anything specific?"

Darren should have stopped.

He knew he should have stopped.

Instead he said one more thing.

A small thing.

A man had been mouthing off about somebody connected to Jinx.

That was all.

Grant asked nothing about methods, hiding places or prohibited items.

He did not need to.

He had a name attached to tension.

Darren saw the moment the information became useful.

He regretted it immediately.

"That's not—"

Grant looked at him.

"Not what?"

Darren stopped.

There was no sentence that made it smaller again.

---

## JOSH

Josh was trying to convince Bruce that a Ford Focus ST was underrated.

Bruce disagreed on philosophical grounds.

"Too sensible."

"It's quick."

"So is diarrhoea. Doesn't make it aspirational."

Josh stared at him.

Then burst out laughing.

"That's not even relevant."

"Speed alone is not enough."

"You don't even drive."

"Exactly. Pure observer. No bias."

Connor was nearby cleaning a table.

"Don't listen to him, Josh. He thinks a Vauxhall Zafira looks aggressive."

Bruce pointed at Connor.

"With the right driver, anything looks aggressive."

"A mobility scooter?"

Bruce thought.

"Especially a mobility scooter. No one expects it."

Josh laughed so hard he had to sit down.

Darren returned from Grant's office while they were still arguing.

Josh noticed immediately.

"You all right?"

Darren nodded.

"Yeah."

Josh accepted it less quickly than he used to.

That worried Darren.

Prison was teaching him to hear the gap between words and faces.

Darren sat down beside him.

"What are we arguing about?"

Josh recovered instantly.

"Bruce has no respect for engineering."

"I respect bridges," Bruce said.

"Cars."

"Some cars go over bridges."

Darren closed his eyes.

"I regret asking."

Josh smiled at him.

For a few minutes Darren managed to smile back.

## JINX — A WING

The officers came for Leon Carter's cell just before lunch.

Not Leon the gym orderly.

A different Leon.

That distinction became important because prison had an unreasonable number of men with the same first names.

Jinx watched from farther down as staff dealt with it.

No drama.

No shouting.

Just a routine intervention that felt too precisely timed to be random.

Deano came alongside him later.

"You see that?"

"Yeah."

"Bit convenient."

Jinx looked at him.

"What are you saying?"

"Nothing yet."

Jinx hated the word *yet*.

"Someone's talking," Deano said.

Jinx looked away.

"Everyone talks."

"Not like that."

"You don't know why staff moved on him."

"Neither do you."

That was true.

Jinx disliked true things delivered by Deano.

"Don't start accusing people," Jinx said.

"Didn't."

"You're thinking it loudly."

Deano smiled.

"You sound like Bruce."

"Take that back."

Deano laughed.

Jinx did not.

Rico's failed attack had already damaged trust.

Now every staff action threatened to turn suspicion into another kind of violence.

He had spent weeks trying to stop outside people's feud from swallowing his circle.

It was starting to happen anyway, only from the inside out.

---

## MALIK

Malik noticed timing.

That was his real talent.

Not knowing everything.

Knowing when two things happened too close together to ignore.

He had heard, two days earlier, that one prisoner had been loudly complaining about Jinx's people.

Then Friday staff took a sudden interest in the same man.

That could mean nothing.

Prison generated coincidences all day.

It could also mean somebody had supplied a piece of information.

Malik stood near the rail thinking about everyone who might know the same thing.

Too many.

That was the problem.

People imagined information as a line from one mouth to one ear.

In reality it was fog.

Everyone breathed some of it.

Yusuf appeared beside him.

"You've got your thinking face on."

Malik looked over.

"What's my thinking face?"

"Like normal, but less attractive."

Malik smiled.

"Someone's talking."

Yusuf's expression changed.

"About what?"

"Don't know."

"Then maybe don't say someone's talking."

Malik looked at him.

Yusuf shrugged.

"You of all people should know what happens when a sentence leaves your mouth."

Malik said nothing.

That annoyed him because it sounded like Mason's advice again.

Hear all.

See all.

Say nothing.

The rule was much easier when the information belonged to somebody else.

---

## DARREN — NIGHT

Darren heard about the staff intervention after bang-up.

A shout through doors.

Then another voice adding a detail.

Then the name.

The same name Darren had said to Grant.

His stomach dropped.

He sat on the bed staring at nothing.

He did not know whether what he had said caused anything.

That uncertainty was almost worse.

If Grant had acted on something else, Darren was innocent of the consequence.

If Grant had acted on Darren's words, he was not.

There was no way to ask without revealing the question.

From somewhere along the landing Josh shouted:

"Daz!"

Darren looked at the door.

"What?"

"BMW M3 or C63?"

Darren closed his eyes.

Of course.

The most serious moral question of Darren's evening interrupted by Josh's thirteenth car argument of the week.

"C63!" Darren shouted back.

Connor yelled from another cell:

"Wrong!"

Josh cheered.

Darren laughed despite himself.

Then the laughter disappeared.

The name he had given Grant was still in his head.

Small things did not stay small once somebody else decided what to do with them.
