# SATURDAY 5 OCTOBER 2019 — THE OTHER VISIT

## CONNOR

Connor had never met the woman before.

That was the first thing that made the whole idea feel ridiculous.

Not dangerous.

Ridiculous.

He was about to sit across from somebody he did not know, pretend there was a reason she had come to see him, take responsibility for something she had brought in and then somehow return to C Wing carrying the consequences if anything went wrong.

All because Kez had spent two days telling him he was switched on, that he could make some money and that this was how people started making a proper name for themselves.

Twenty percent.

Connor had liked the sound of that when Kez said it.

Sitting there waiting to be called, it suddenly occurred to him that twenty percent also meant somebody else was keeping eighty while Connor did the part making his palms sweat.

He tried not to think about that.

Jamo had watched him get ready without saying much.

That was worse than a lecture.

At the door Jamo finally said:

"You can still not do whatever stupid thing you're doing."

Connor looked back.

"I'm just going on a visit."

"With someone you don't know."

Connor said nothing.

Jamo nodded.

"Exactly."

Connor left before the conversation could become useful.

---

The woman was older than Connor expected.

Not old.

Just not the sort of person his imagination had supplied.

Normal coat. Normal hair. Tired face.

She smiled when she saw him, but there was no recognition in it.

Connor sat down opposite her.

"All right?"

"Yeah. You Connor?"

That was nearly funny.

"Yeah."

"Right."

Then they had to behave like two people with something to talk about.

The first few minutes were agony.

Weather.

Traffic.

A made-up reference to somebody Connor vaguely knew by name.

Neither of them was good enough at pretending for Connor to believe the room could not see straight through them.

Every officer looked too close.

Every pause felt suspicious.

Connor wondered what the woman had been told about him.

Probably not much.

Maybe just a name and a table.

That thought made him feel even smaller inside the arrangement.

Kez had made it sound like Connor was being brought into something.

Sitting opposite the stranger, Connor felt more like a replaceable part in it.

---

Eventually the moment came.

The woman passed responsibility for a prohibited parcel to Connor.

No ceremony.

No clever conversation.

No feeling of power.

Just a small thing that immediately seemed to weigh more than it should.

Connor knew what came after visits.

He knew there would be searching before he was allowed back onto the wing.

That was the part Kez had spoken about as if it were merely another stage.

Connor now understood that people were very relaxed about frightening stages they did not personally have to stand through.

The rest of the visit became almost impossible to follow.

The woman kept talking.

Connor answered.

He could not have repeated half of what either of them said afterwards.

He was listening instead for officers' footsteps.

Watching movement in the room.

Thinking about the search waiting after the visit.

Thinking about the fact that nobody who had asked him to do this would be standing beside him when it happened.

And, stupidly, thinking about twenty percent.

Twenty percent had sounded like money before.

Now it sounded like the price Kez had put on Connor being the one who could get caught.

---

When the visit ended, Connor felt briefly sick.

He followed instructions.

He went where he was told.

The post-visit search process included a strip search.

Connor had known that before agreeing.

Knowing it in a cell and standing there while it actually happened were completely different things.

He was frightened enough that ordinary instructions sounded far away.

For those minutes there was no Rush.

No reputation.

No Craig.

No Kez calling him switched on.

Just Connor Blake, twenty-one years old, standing through a prison search knowing he had accepted somebody else's risk and waiting for the moment everything might collapse.

It did not.

He got through the search without the prohibited parcel being detected.

The exact physical mechanics mattered far less to Connor than the fear. Afterwards he could mostly remember his heartbeat, the humiliation of the search and the certainty that if he was caught, Kez would not be appearing beside him to share the consequences.

When Connor was finally allowed onward, his legs felt strangely light.

Relief came first.

Then pride.

That was the dangerous part.

He had been terrified.

But he had done it.

And somewhere in Connor's head, surviving a stupid risk immediately began trying to rewrite the risk as evidence of courage.

---

## JAMO

Jamo knew the second Connor came back.

Connor was talking too much.

"Food on visits is a joke. You know that? Absolute robbery. And there was this officer who kept—"

"What did you do?"

Connor stopped.

"Nothing."

"You've got the face."

"What face?"

"The face where you've done something you want to brag about and hide at the same time."

Connor looked away.

Jamo's stomach dropped.

"Did you bring something back?"

Connor looked at him sharply.

Jamo swore under his breath.

"You're a fucking idiot."

"Keep your voice down."

"See? That's not a denial."

Connor moved closer.

"It's sorted."

"For who?"

Connor frowned.

"What?"

"For you? Or for the people who sent you?"

Connor did not answer.

Jamo looked at him properly.

"What are you even getting for it?"

Connor hesitated.

"Twenty percent."

Jamo stared.

"Twenty?"

"Yeah."

"And you did the visit?"

Connor's face hardened.

"What difference does that make?"

Jamo almost laughed, but there was nothing funny in his face.

"Mate, it makes all the difference."

Connor felt heat rise in his neck.

He already knew where the conversation was going and suddenly hated Jamo for getting there before him.

Jamo pointed around the cell.

"This is what I mean. I live here as well. If they spin this cell because of your bullshit, my name's standing next to yours on the door."

Connor felt defensive immediately.

"Nothing's in here."

"That is not the point."

Connor had heard those words a lot recently.

They were becoming annoying because people kept using them when they were right.

---

## KEZ

Kez found Connor before Connor found him.

"All good?"

Connor nodded.

"Yeah."

Kez smiled and held out a hand.

"Knew you'd be sound."

Connor shook it.

For a second he waited.

Kez had already started looking elsewhere.

"What about my twenty?" Connor said.

Kez looked back as if he had almost forgotten Connor existed.

"You'll get sorted."

"When?"

"Relax."

That word irritated Connor immediately.

Relax.

Kez had been relaxed the whole time.

Kez had not sat opposite a stranger.

Kez had not gone through the search afterwards.

Kez had not spent the visit wondering whether his life was about to get worse for somebody else's parcel.

Connor had done all of that for one fifth.

Kez smiled again.

"You did well. People know you're sound now. Told you. Proper name for yourself."

Two days earlier Connor would have swallowed every word.

Now the speech sounded rehearsed.

He looked at Kez and, for the first time, saw the deal without the compliments wrapped around it.

Kez had kept most of the reward.

Connor had carried nearly all of the immediate risk.

And Connor had been grateful for the opportunity.

He felt like a mug.

That feeling was worse than fear because at least fear had made sense while it was happening.

---

## CONNOR — LATER

Craig did not ask how the visit went.

He asked:

"Who asked you?"

Connor stopped.

"Asked me what?"

Craig looked at him.

Connor lasted two seconds.

"Kez."

Craig nodded once.

"And you did it."

"Yeah."

There was less pride in the answer now.

Craig noticed that too.

"What'd he give you?"

Connor looked away.

"Twenty percent."

Craig's expression barely changed.

Somehow that was worse than laughing.

"And who took the risk?"

Connor said nothing.

Craig waited.

Connor hated him for making him answer it in his own head.

"You think because people know your name now, they're inviting you into something," Craig said.

Connor looked at him.

"Some of them are just looking for someone else to carry the risk."

"I can handle myself."

Craig almost smiled.

"That's exactly why you're useful."

Connor's expression changed.

Craig let the sentence sit.

"Useful isn't the same as important, Connor."

The real name made it worse.

"If you want to be somebody, stop volunteering to be the person who gets caught doing somebody else's stupid job."

Connor looked away.

"So what, you get people to do everything for you?"

Craig's eyes stayed on him.

"I'm telling you to learn the difference between leading something and being used by it."

Connor remembered the stranger.

The search.

Kez saying *relax*.

Twenty percent.

Craig stepped away.

"You got away with being stupid. Don't confuse that with being clever."

Then he walked off.

Connor hated the lecture.

But now he hated something else more.

Kez had not tricked him with a complicated plan.

He had flattered him.

That had been enough.

---

## BRUCE

Bruce was sitting with Yusuf when Connor walked past.

He looked at Connor once.

"Successful crime face."

Connor stopped dead.

Yusuf closed his eyes.

"Bruce."

"What?"

Connor stared at him.

"What the fuck does that mean?"

Bruce shrugged.

"Relieved, annoyed and disappointed nobody gave you a certificate."

Yusuf started laughing.

Connor tried not to.

Failed.

Bruce pointed at him.

"There. Exactly that."

Connor shook his head and walked away.

Yusuf looked at Bruce.

"One day you're going to say the wrong thing to the wrong person."

Bruce nodded.

"Statistically inevitable."

Then he went back to eating biscuits.
