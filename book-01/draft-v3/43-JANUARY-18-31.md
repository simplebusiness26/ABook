# SATURDAY 18 JANUARY 2020 — SOMEBODY ELSE'S CELL

## CONNOR

Connor got his single cell.

Then discovered privacy could be noisy.

Nobody snored.

Nobody prayed below him.

Nobody asked what he was thinking when he cleaned the same thing twice.

Nobody told him he was becoming somebody he might not recognise.

Perfect.

Exactly what he had wanted.

He hated it by the third night.

Not enough to ask Stuart back.

That would have required admitting the move had been a mistake.

Connor had become better at admitting mistakes in public disputes than private ones.

He found Stuart on the landing after breakfast.

"New cell all right?"

Stuart looked at him.

"It is a cell."

"Helpful."

"You asked a broad question."

Connor smiled.

Stuart smiled too, but less.

He looked tired.

Connor noticed.

"You sleeping?"

"Some."

"Cellmate?"

Stuart glanced down the landing.

"He keeps different hours."

Connor understood the phrase only partly.

He had heard things about Stuart's new cellmate. A man whose days had narrowed around synthetic drugs and sleep and the uneven rhythms that followed.

Nothing Connor could prove.

Nothing he wanted to know in detail.

"You want me to say something?"

Stuart laughed softly.

"To whom?"

Connor did not know.

That was the problem with offering help after causing the situation.

He wanted a button marked UNDO.

Prison had plenty of buttons.

None did that.

"If you need anything," Connor said.

Stuart nodded.

"Thank you, Connor."

Still Connor.

That made it worse.

---

## STUART

Stuart had begun staying out of the cell whenever the regime allowed.

Library.

Association.

Quiet corners.

Anywhere that did not feel like the atmosphere inside his new room.

His cellmate was not cruel.

That would have made the situation easier to describe.

He was simply absorbed in a way of getting through prison Stuart had once watched from a distance.

Time disappeared around him.

Plans disappeared.

Whole afternoons became something to escape rather than use.

Stuart had arrived at Lewes convinced that if he kept his mind disciplined enough, prison could not take the person he was.

Remand had been testing that belief for months.

The new cell tested it differently.

One evening Stuart sat on his bunk listening to the man below talk about making the night pass faster.

Stuart thought of Connor's empty bottom bunk.

Then hated himself for wanting it.

Pride could survive even righteousness.

---

## JAMO

Jamo saw Connor watching Stuart.

"You regret it?"

Connor looked over.

"What?"

"Moving him."

"Didn't move him. Prison did."

Jamo laughed.

Connor's face tightened.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You love that word now."

Jamo shrugged.

"You put the app in."

Connor looked back toward Stuart.

"He'll be all right."

Jamo nodded.

"Probably."

Connor hated probably even more than nothing.


# TUESDAY 21 JANUARY 2020 — TY

## TYLER

Tyler's reputation finally met somebody who had known him before he invented it.

The new arrival was called Aaron.

Same wider area.

Same schools in overlapping years.

Enough shared people that Tyler's stories had nowhere to hide.

Aaron saw him during association and grinned.

"Ty?"

Tyler's whole body tightened.

Nobody on C Wing called him Ty except his mum through a telephone.

"All right, mate?"

Aaron laughed and hugged him briefly.

"Didn't know you were in here."

Tyler felt people looking.

Probably they were not.

That did not matter.

"Yeah. Been here a bit."

"How's your mum?"

"Good."

Aaron started talking about home.

Names.

Places.

People who were suddenly real rather than useful decorations for Tyler's prison persona.

Then Bruce appeared.

Of course he did.

"You know Tyler?"

Aaron looked at him.

"Ty? Yeah. Since school."

Bruce's eyes moved to Tyler.

Tyler wanted the floor to open.

"He always this loud?" Bruce asked.

Aaron laughed.

"He was worse at school. Funny little prick. Used to get sent out every lesson."

Funny little prick.

Not feared.

Not connected.

Not the hard young criminal Tyler had spent months presenting.

Just Ty.

A lad from school.

Tyler waited for the humiliation to become violent.

It did not.

It became laughter.

"Tell them about Lee," someone said later.

Aaron frowned.

"His cousin?"

Tyler closed his eyes.

"Yeah," Bruce said, enjoying himself only slightly too much. "International man of influence."

Aaron burst out laughing.

"Lee? He works on sites now."

The landing laughed too.

Tyler's face burned.

Months of construction collapsed in one sentence and a high-visibility vest.

---

## CONNOR

Connor found Tyler in the cell later.

"You all right?"

"No."

The answer came instantly.

Connor almost smiled at the progress.

Tyler saw it.

"Don't."

"I weren't."

"Everyone knows now."

"Knows what?"

"That I ain't..."

Tyler stopped.

Connor waited.

"That I ain't who I said."

Connor sat down.

"Who did you say you were?"

Tyler looked annoyed.

"You know."

"No, seriously. You acted like you knew more people. Made your cousin bigger. Talked shit. So what?"

"Easy for you. Rush is real."

Connor looked at him.

That sentence would have pleased him in October.

Now it felt more complicated.

"Rush is one thing I did in ten seconds."

Tyler laughed bitterly.

"And everyone rates you for it."

"Some do. Some think I'm an idiot. Both might be right."

Tyler looked away.

Connor continued:

"You know what everyone actually learned today?"

"What?"

"You were a loud funny kid at school."

"That's worse."

Connor laughed.

Tyler did too, reluctantly.

"Mate, being frightened isn't some secret offence. You were new."

Tyler stared at the floor.

"I've been scared since reception."

There it was.

Plain.

No performance.

Connor nodded.

"Yeah. Me too."

Tyler looked up sharply.

"You?"

"Especially me."

Connor thought of his bags on C Wing, the shower alarm, Jamo saying everyone could tell.

"I just got lucky and prison gave my fear a better story."

Tyler smiled.

For the first time since arriving, Tyler did not need Connor to be Rush in order to listen to him.

---

## BRUCE

Bruce found Tyler the next morning.

"Lee works on sites?"

Tyler stared at him.

"Please die."

"Honest employment. Respectable."

"Bruce."

"Does he have a van?"

Tyler started laughing despite himself.

Bruce nodded.

"Good. Shame metabolised."

"What?"

"Nothing."

Bruce walked away.

Tyler realised the story was already becoming funny.

That was how prison survived most embarrassments.

It ate them and asked for another one.


# FRIDAY 24 JANUARY 2020 — DIFFERENT JOSH

## DARREN

Darren noticed the change because Josh stopped finishing stories.

That was unnatural.

Josh normally finished one story, started another halfway through and returned to the first three hours later.

Now he lost the thread and shrugged.

"What were you saying?" Darren asked.

Josh frowned.

"Dunno."

They were sitting near the phones.

Josh looked tired again.

Not ordinary tired.

Flat in places where he used to be restless.

Then suddenly animated for ten minutes.

Then quiet.

Darren knew those patterns too well to pretend he did not recognise danger around them.

"You been staying up?"

Josh shrugged.

"Bit."

"Around who?"

Josh looked at him.

"Why?"

Darren heard the defensiveness and hated himself because some of it sounded learned.

"Just asking."

"You do the same stuff."

The sentence hit cleanly.

Darren looked away.

There it was.

The argument he had been avoiding since October.

Josh had not entered Darren's world through a single doorway.

He had entered by repetition.

Sit here.

Know him.

Ignore that.

This is normal.

Bad day.

Everyone makes choices.

Darren had been teaching prison without writing a syllabus.

"Doesn't mean you should," Darren said.

Josh laughed.

"Listen to you."

Darren's chest tightened.

"I'm serious."

"So am I. You can't tell me everything's normal for months then suddenly act like my dad."

Darren had no answer.

Josh stood.

"I'm fine."

He walked away.

Darren watched him go.

The phrase sounded worse from Josh than it ever had from Darren.

---

## CONNOR

Connor noticed too.

Not immediately.

He noticed when Josh did not argue with Bruce about cars.

Bruce said something deliberately wrong about an M3.

Josh barely reacted.

Connor looked at Darren.

Darren was already looking at Josh.

That was enough.

Later Connor asked:

"He all right?"

Darren's face closed.

"Yeah."

Connor waited.

Darren sighed.

"I don't know."

That answer frightened Connor more.

"You spoke to him?"

"Yeah."

"And?"

"Told me not to dad him."

Connor smiled faintly.

"Sounds like Josh."

"That's the problem."

Connor looked at him.

Darren rubbed both hands over his face.

"He's still him. Just..."

He stopped.

Connor understood enough not to finish the sentence for him.

---

## LEON

Leon removed Josh's name from one gym session because Josh did not turn up.

Routine consequence.

Nothing personal.

When Josh complained later, Leon did not argue.

"Turn up next time."

"I was asleep."

"Then you made a choice."

Josh rolled his eyes.

Leon watched him leave.

Months earlier Josh would have spent ten minutes arguing that sleep was not a choice.

The absence of argument bothered Leon more than the original absence.

Routine showed you people.

Especially when they stopped caring about it.


# MONDAY 27 JANUARY 2020 — LEAH

## OFFICER BENNETT

Bennett finally spoke because fear made a mistake for him.

He was on C Wing when his phone, secured away as required during duty, became the only thing he could think about.

He had seen a message before shift.

A demand had moved from vague pressure to a clear expectation that Bennett cross another professional line.

He did not intend to do it.

He also no longer trusted himself to simply refuse and go home safely.

Mercer found him in the staff area with both hands gripping the edge of a desk.

"Bennett."

He looked up.

"Yeah."

"What's happened?"

"Nothing."

Mercer's expression changed.

"Don't."

Bennett looked toward the door.

A prisoner passed beyond the glass.

Then another.

"I need a word," he said.

Mercer waited.

Bennett's voice dropped.

"Not here."

"Fine."

He looked at her properly.

"Leah, please."

The first name came out because Bennett had stopped performing for half a second.

Neither officer noticed who was near enough to hear it.

Craig did.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

In a private staff space, Bennett gave her enough truth to change everything.

Not every detail.

Not a clean confession.

Pressure outside.

People connected to prisoners.

Threats that had become personal.

Things already done that Bennett knew he should have reported.

A new demand he could not face.

Mercer listened without interrupting.

When he finished, Bennett looked physically smaller.

"I'm fucked," he said.

Mercer did not tell him he wasn't.

That would have been kind and useless.

"Patel needs to know."

Bennett closed his eyes.

"I know."

"Today."

"I know."

"And you don't do anything else for them."

He laughed once.

"You say that like I get to decide."

Mercer stared at him.

"You decide what you do. You may not decide what follows."

The sentence sounded like something Stuart might say.

Or Craig.

That irritated her.

Bennett nodded.

"All right."

Mercer believed him more than she had believed *I'm fine*.

That was not the same as believing the danger had ended.

---

## CRAIG AND OFFICER MERCER

Craig said nothing until later.

Mercer was near the rail when he approached.

"Officer Mercer."

"Craig."

He waited until nobody was close enough to make the conversation entertainment.

Then:

"Bennett told you."

Mercer's body went still.

"That's none of your business."

"Correct."

"Then don't ask."

"Didn't."

Mercer looked at him.

He knew something.

How much, she could not tell.

That uncertainty had become the dangerous attraction of talking to him.

"You said he looked frightened," Mercer said.

Craig nodded.

"He is."

"People connected to prisoners are putting pressure on him."

Craig's expression did not change.

Mercer heard herself continue and knew she had crossed into a conversation she could not later pretend had been casual.

"It needs to stop."

Craig looked at her for a long moment.

"That's a big thing to ask a prisoner, Officer Mercer."

"I'm not asking you to do anything unlawful."

"No."

The word contained enough scepticism to annoy her.

"I'm asking whether you can make it clear he's not available to be leaned on."

Craig remained unreadable.

Then he said quietly:

"Leah."

Mercer's face changed before she could control it.

Craig saw.

Of course he did.

"Don't call me that."

"Bennett did."

"You weren't meant to hear it."

"Still heard it."

Mercer felt the boundary shift in the space between one name and another.

Officer Mercer was public.

Leah was not.

"Craig."

"I'll see what I can do about Bennett's problem."

She should have stopped him there.

Asked what that meant.

Reported the conversation.

Refused the offer.

Instead she said:

"No one gets hurt."

Craig's face became colder.

"I didn't say anyone would."

Mercer nodded once.

Then walked away.

Her heart was beating too fast.

Not because Craig had threatened anything.

Because he had called her Leah and she had continued the conversation.

---

## CONNOR

Connor knew something had changed because Craig called Officer Mercer "Officer Mercer" more carefully afterwards.

That was suspicious in itself.

Connor looked at him.

Craig looked back.

"What?"

Connor smiled.

"Nothing."

"Good."

Connor remembered the title.

For once, he left the knowledge exactly where he found it.


# WEDNESDAY 29 JANUARY 2020 — NOT GUILTY

## STUART — COURT MORNING

Stuart left for court with his Bible and returned without knowing he would return until the words were spoken.

Not guilty.

Two words.

Months of remand did not reverse themselves when he heard them.

No door opened straight onto his old room at university.

No clock ran backwards.

No one returned the nights he had spent awake wondering whether the state would believe his account of defending himself.

But the words mattered.

Not guilty.

When the process finally released him back toward Lewes only long enough for discharge arrangements, Stuart felt almost numb.

Freedom had become too large to feel immediately.

---

## CONNOR

Connor heard before Stuart reached C Wing.

"Stuart's beat his case."

He turned so quickly Bruce laughed.

"Not guilty?"

"That's what they're saying."

Connor grinned.

A real grin.

Then the second feeling arrived.

Stuart was going home.

The empty bottom bunk Connor had wanted so badly suddenly felt like evidence.

When Stuart appeared to collect the last of his property, the wing greeted him with congratulations, jokes and the special envy reserved for a man whose door was about to open outward.

Connor waited until the noise thinned.

"You actually did it."

Stuart smiled.

"The court did it."

"Don't get humble now."

Stuart laughed.

Connor looked at him.

He seemed thinner than November.

More tired.

There were parts of his recent weeks Connor had not asked enough about.

"You all right?" Connor said.

Stuart understood the real question.

"I will be."

Not *I am*.

Connor nodded.

"About the cell..."

Stuart raised a hand.

"Connor."

"Let me say it."

Stuart waited.

Connor struggled.

"I was a dickhead."

Stuart smiled.

"This is a strong opening."

Connor laughed.

"I wanted space. I made it your problem. Then you ended up in that cell and... I saw you changing."

Stuart looked down.

"My choices remained mine."

"I know."

"You did not make every bad day I had."

"I know."

"But you were selfish."

Connor nodded.

"Yeah."

The agreement hurt and relieved him at the same time.

Stuart picked up his bag.

"Then learn from the correct amount of guilt. Not all of it. Not none of it."

Connor stared at him.

"You had to get one last sermon in."

"Absolutely."

They hugged briefly.

Not dramatic.

Enough.

At the door Stuart looked back.

"Connor."

"What?"

"Do not let Rush make all your decisions."

Connor smiled.

"Go home, man."

Stuart did.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer processed enough of the discharge to watch Stuart leave.

Prison was strange around acquittals.

One minute a man occupied a bed, a risk entry, a meal count, a place in a cell.

Then the legal reason for holding him vanished and the institution began removing him from itself.

Mercer watched Connor watching too.

Blake looked pleased and guilty.

Human combination.

Stuart passed through the final internal door.

Connor would probably never know exactly what happened to him next.

That was prison too.

People became central to your day and then disappeared through a gate.

---

## CONNOR — NIGHT

The cell was properly Connor's now.

No Jamo.

No Stuart.

No temporary expectation that someone would come back.

He sat on the bottom bunk because there was no reason not to.

Stuart's absence made the room larger and worse.

Connor found a tiny folded scrap of paper behind the bed.

Stuart's handwriting.

A verse reference Connor did not recognise and beneath it one line in plain English:

**YOU ARE RESPONSIBLE FOR THE MAN YOU PRACTISE BEING.**

Connor stared at it.

"Annoying prick," he said softly.

He put the paper in his property rather than throwing it away.


# FRIDAY 31 JANUARY 2020 — THE QUIETEST ALARM

## DARREN — MORNING

Josh was not at breakfast.

That was the first wrong thing.

Josh missed gym sometimes now.

Missed parts of association.

Slept at odd hours.

But breakfast involved food and people, two things Josh had always treated as appointments.

Darren looked toward Josh's cell.

"Seen him?" he asked Connor.

Connor shook his head.

"Thought he was with you."

The sentence landed badly.

Darren walked toward the cell.

He knocked.

No answer.

Called Josh's name.

Nothing.

For one second Darren stood still.

Then he got an officer.

He did not try to solve it himself.

That decision would return to him later as one of the few things he did not regret.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

The response moved quickly once staff understood Josh was seriously unwell.

An alarm.

Officers clearing space.

Healthcare called.

Orders becoming sharp and simple.

The wing noise changed.

Prisoners knew alarms.

They knew the difference between an ordinary incident and the atmosphere around something worse.

Mercer kept people back and let healthcare work.

She did not need the whole wing watching a young man's worst morning.

Darren stood farther away, pale and completely still.

Connor was beside him.

Josh was moved for emergency treatment and then out for hospital care.

No one on C Wing knew the outcome when the doors shut afterwards.

That uncertainty filled every cell.

---

## CONNOR

The rumours began before the alarm had properly ended.

He'd died.

No, he was alive.

He was breathing.

He wasn't.

Hospital.

Healthcare.

Someone had seen something.

Nobody had seen anything.

Connor listened to three versions and repeated none.

Malik did the same.

That frightened Connor almost as much as the alarm.

When Malik refused information, it meant he understood the price of being wrong.

Darren sat on the edge of Connor's bunk because Connor had pulled him into the cell without thinking.

"He's gonna be all right," Connor said.

Darren looked at him.

Connor immediately regretted pretending to know.

"Sorry."

Darren stared at the floor.

"I taught him all this."

"No."

"Don't."

Connor stopped.

Darren's voice cracked.

"Don't make me feel better with something that ain't true."

Connor sat beside him.

"You didn't choose for him."

"I made it normal."

Connor had no answer that could remove that sentence.

So he stayed.

---

## MALIK

By evening Malik knew one ugly version of the story that would spread fast if he repeated it.

He did not know whether it was true.

Earlier Malik would have traded the detail for status.

Now he imagined Josh's mum hearing strangers' invented certainty before she heard facts.

He shut his mouth.

Hear all.

See all.

Say nothing.

Tonight the phrase was not clever.

It was mercy.

---

## DARREN — NIGHT

No news was worse than bad news until bad news arrived.

That night it had not arrived yet.

Darren lay awake behind his door listening for footsteps.

Josh's voice existed everywhere because Darren expected it everywhere.

A shout about cars.

A question through the wall.

A complaint about food.

Nothing came.

For the first time since Josh arrived, Darren understood how much noise one person had added to the prison.

The wing had never sounded quieter.
