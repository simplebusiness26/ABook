# TUESDAY 17 DECEMBER 2019 — WHAT FOLLOWS YOU HOME

## OFFICER BENNETT

Bennett's problem finally used his home as punctuation.

The message arrived while he was standing in the kitchen making toast for his child.

Nothing explicit.

Nothing that could be shown to somebody without a longer explanation Bennett was still terrified to give.

Just a reference to the street.

His street.

Not the prison.

Not work.

Home.

The toast popped.

Bennett jumped badly enough that his child laughed.

"Daddy!"

"Yeah. Sorry."

He smiled because children deserved smiles that did not belong to adult problems.

Then he turned the phone face-down on the counter.

For the rest of breakfast he could feel it there.

Prison had followed him through the gate, into the car, through the front door and now onto the kitchen worktop.

He had spent months telling himself he was protecting his family by keeping the problem quiet.

For the first time the logic looked completely backwards.

---

## SENIOR OFFICER PATEL

Patel asked him into the office after handover.

Bennett's pulse jumped immediately.

"Sit down."

Bennett sat.

Patel stayed standing for a moment, then shut the door.

"This isn't disciplinary."

That sentence did not calm Bennett.

"All right."

"You're not right."

Bennett laughed once.

"Christmas with a kid. No one's right."

Patel did not smile.

"You've been jumpy for weeks. You're checking doors. You disappear with your phone. Mercer says you've nearly walked into people because you're looking behind you."

Bennett felt heat rise into his face.

"Mercer's been talking about me?"

"I'm talking about you."

Patel sat opposite.

"If something outside work is affecting you inside work, I need to know enough to manage risk."

There it was.

A door.

Bennett could walk through it.

He imagined the relief of saying the first sentence.

Someone is pressuring me.

Then the second sentence appeared.

I should have told you months ago.

Then the third.

I have already crossed lines.

The relief disappeared.

"Family stuff," Bennett said.

Patel watched him.

"That's all?"

"Yeah."

Patel leaned back.

Bennett knew he had failed the conversation.

The strange thing was that Patel looked disappointed rather than convinced.

"All right," Patel said. "But hear me properly. If that changes, you come to me before it becomes something I have to come to you about."

Bennett nodded.

He left the office feeling worse.

The door had been open.

He had chosen the corridor again.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer did not ask Bennett how the meeting had gone.

She could see.

He emerged with the expression of a man who had been offered help and found a reason not to take it.

She knew that expression more intimately than she wanted to admit.

Not because her situation was the same.

It was not.

Bennett looked frightened.

Mercer's problem was curiosity becoming attachment.

Different roads.

Same bad habit: telling yourself the line only mattered after you crossed it completely.

Craig was returning from a legal visit later that morning.

Mercer checked the paperwork.

Normal.

Necessary.

She did not need to look up when he approached.

She did anyway.

"Officer Mercer."

"Craig."

His eyes moved briefly toward Bennett.

Mercer caught it.

"Don't."

Craig looked back at her.

"Didn't say anything."

"Good."

A pause.

"He looks worse," Craig said.

Mercer should have ended the conversation.

Instead she asked:

"Than when?"

Craig's face barely changed.

That was enough for Mercer to realise what she had done.

She had asked.

Not about Craig's case.

Not about his behaviour.

About her colleague.

Craig answered quietly.

"Than last month."

Mercer nodded once and walked away.

The conversation was not misconduct.

It was also not nothing.

---

## CONNOR

Connor saw Mercer walk away from Craig and immediately smiled.

Craig looked over.

"Don't."

Connor raised both hands.

"I haven't said anything."

"Your face has."

"Everyone's a face expert."

Craig almost smiled.

Connor fell into step beside him.

"Court?"

"Lawyers."

"Good?"

"Lawyers rarely come with adjectives that simple."

Connor laughed.

Craig's case had started becoming a physical absence in the wing.

Appointments.

Court days.

Meetings.

People guessing what each one meant.

Connor had learned enough not to ask for every detail.

He had not learned enough not to wonder.


# SATURDAY 21 DECEMBER 2019 — THE OUTSIDE MOVES

## CONNOR — PHONE

Connor's girlfriend answered on the fifth ring.

He counted.

He wished he had not counted.

"Hello?"

"You all right?"

"Yeah. Sorry. Was getting ready."

"Going out?"

"Yeah."

Connor looked at the wall in front of him.

"Where?"

She told him.

Same place she had mentioned on the visit.

Friends.

Christmas drinks.

Nothing suspicious in the words.

Connor's brain immediately began applying for the job.

"Who's going?"

A pause.

Small.

Probably meaningless.

Connor heard it like a siren.

She listed names.

One of them was a man Connor knew loosely.

Not a friend.

Not an enemy.

Exactly the sort of name prison could turn into a whole story if Connor let it.

"He's going?"

"Yeah. He's with everyone."

Connor's chest tightened.

He could hear his old self assembling questions.

Why didn't you tell me?

Have you been speaking to him?

How many times?

Who invited him?

He looked across the wing.

Stuart was reading nearby.

Mason's line sat somewhere behind that.

Hear all.

See all.

Say nothing.

Not forever.

Just long enough to know what you actually had.

"All right," Connor said.

His girlfriend sounded almost suspicious of the lack of suspicion.

"You all right?"

"Yeah."

"Connor."

He smiled without much humour.

"I don't love it. But you're allowed to go out."

Silence.

Then:

"Thank you."

Connor hated that she sounded relieved.

It told him his behaviour outside had not always been as reasonable as he remembered it.

"Ring me tomorrow," he said.

"I will."

They said goodbye.

Connor put the phone down.

He did not feel calm.

He had simply behaved calmly.

Prison was teaching him the difference.

---

## STUART

"You are angry," Stuart said.

Connor looked over.

"You heard all that?"

"No. I have eyes."

Connor laughed once.

"Her mate's going out. Bloke I know."

"Her mate or your mate?"

"Neither."

"Then why is he responsible for your evening?"

Connor stared at him.

"You know what, sometimes Christianity should come with an off switch."

Stuart smiled.

"This is not Christianity. This is basic peace."

Connor sat down.

"What if something happens?"

"Then something happens."

"Brilliant."

"And if nothing happens?"

Connor did not answer.

Stuart closed his book.

"You cannot serve a sentence here and make another person serve one outside."

That landed harder than anything else.

Connor looked at him.

"I ain't making her do nothing."

"Good."

"I said she could go out."

Stuart's eyebrows rose.

"How generous."

Connor threw a rolled sock at him.

Stuart ducked, laughing.

---

## JAMO

Jamo found Connor later pretending not to care.

"What's wrong?"

"Nothing."

Jamo stared.

Connor sighed.

"Girlfriend's out."

"Ah."

"Don't ah me."

"Wasn't an insulting ah."

"Sounded like one."

Jamo smiled.

"You trust her?"

Connor opened his mouth.

Closed it.

"Mostly."

"Then that's your answer tonight."

Connor looked at him.

"When did you become Stuart?"

"I haven't. I swear more."

Connor laughed.

It helped.

Not enough.

Enough to get through Saturday without turning one man's name into evidence.

---

## GIRLFRIEND — OUTSIDE, THROUGH WHAT CONNOR HEARS

Connor did not see the night.

The reader did not need to either.

That was the point.

For Connor, it existed only as a blank space beyond the prison wall.

Music he could not hear.

People he could not see.

A phone he could not check.

Prison punished imagination by giving it no competition.


# TUESDAY 24 DECEMBER 2019 — CHRISTMAS EVE

## BRUCE

Bruce had acquired a paper crown four hours before Christmas dinner existed.

Nobody knew where from.

He wore it with the seriousness of a constitutional monarch.

"Take that off," Connor said.

"Why?"

"Because you look like a prick."

"Kings hear that a lot."

Josh appeared wearing a second paper crown.

Connor stared.

"Oh, for fuck's sake."

"Royal family," Josh said.

Darren followed behind them.

"I tried."

Bruce pointed at Darren.

"Minister for Decline."

"What does that even mean?"

"Cabinet role."

Josh laughed.

For ten minutes Christmas Eve felt almost like a badly organised youth club.

Then the phones opened and everybody remembered why the date mattered.

---

## CONNOR — PHONE

His little brother answered shouting.

"It's Christmas Eve!"

Connor laughed.

"I know."

"Mum said I can open one tonight."

"One."

"Maybe two."

"One."

"You ain't here."

The words were innocent.

Connor felt them anyway.

"Still one."

His little brother giggled.

They talked about presents.

Food.

A film they were going to watch.

Connor pictured the front room in detail.

Where the tree would be.

Which chair his mum used.

His trainers on the floor in September returned for no useful reason.

For weeks his memory of home had been frozen on the night police arrived.

Now Christmas decorated over it.

Home had continued changing without him.

His mum came on.

"You got everything you need?"

Connor looked around prison.

"Depends how philosophical we're being."

"Don't start."

He smiled.

"Yeah. I'm all right."

She told him everyone loved him.

That his little brother had put a present under the tree with Connor's name on it anyway.

Connor looked down.

"What's in it?"

"Not telling you."

"I'm literally in prison."

"Still Christmas."

Connor laughed.

His girlfriend was not there.

He noticed.

He did not ask for the first minute.

Then he did.

"She about?"

His mum paused.

"Not here."

"Where is she?"

"Connor."

That single word told him not to turn Christmas Eve into an interview.

He stopped.

"All right."

"She'll speak to you."

Connor nodded even though his mum could not see him.

The empty space grew.

He left it empty.

For now.

---

## MACCA

Macca's daughter sang a Christmas song into the phone.

Not well.

Perfectly.

He sat with one hand over his other ear while the wing carried on around him.

When she finished, he clapped against the receiver.

"That's better than the play."

"No it's not."

"Course it is."

"The play had costumes."

"Fair point."

His partner came on afterwards.

Money was tight.

One of the kids had been ill.

Christmas had cost too much.

Normal life.

Messy life.

Macca wanted it so badly he could have cried.

"Next year," he said.

His partner went quiet.

Macca regretted it immediately.

Promises about prison time were dangerous.

"We'll see," she said.

"Yeah."

They both knew love did not control release dates.

---

## STUART

Stuart spent part of Christmas Eve reading the nativity story quietly.

Josh noticed.

"You actually read the Christmas bit at Christmas?"

Stuart looked up.

"Do you object?"

"No. Just organised."

"Thank you."

Josh sat beside him.

"Was Jesus born in December?"

Stuart smiled.

"That is a longer conversation than you think."

Josh's eyes lit up.

"Go on then."

Connor passed five minutes later and found Josh asking theological questions at machine-gun speed while Stuart tried to answer one at a time.

Darren stood nearby laughing.

"You've lost him," Connor said.

"He asked one question," Stuart said.

"That's how it starts."

Josh pointed at Connor.

"Did you know—"

"No. And I'm protecting that."

Connor walked off.

For Stuart, Christmas faith did not remove prison.

It gave the day another language.

Connor could see that without needing to borrow it.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer was working Christmas Eve.

Half the staff wanted to be somewhere else.

Most of the prisoners did too.

That created a strange equality nobody mentioned.

She passed Craig near lock-up.

"Merry Christmas, Officer Mercer."

The words sounded almost formal enough to be funny.

"You too."

"You working tomorrow?"

She should have heard it as a routine question.

She did not.

"Part of it."

Craig nodded.

"Unlucky."

"You too."

This time he smiled.

Mercer walked away annoyed at herself for carrying that smile into the staff office.


# WEDNESDAY 25 DECEMBER 2019 — CHRISTMAS DAY

## CONNOR — MORNING

Christmas morning in prison was still prison.

That was the first disappointment.

Connor had not expected magic.

He had apparently expected something, because waking to the same door and same ceiling felt offensively normal.

Stuart was already awake.

"Merry Christmas."

Connor pulled the blanket over his face.

"Too early."

"It is eight."

"Christmas eight is six."

Stuart laughed.

A voice shouted from farther down the landing:

"MERRY CHRISTMAS, RUSH!"

Connor shouted back:

"FUCK OFF!"

Bruce's voice answered:

"SEASONAL HOSTILITY!"

Stuart laughed harder.

Connor sat up.

"Merry Christmas."

"Thank you."

That was how the day began.

Not with freedom.

With Bruce shouting through architecture.

---

## JOSH AND DARREN

Josh had been awake before everybody.

Darren suspected this because Josh arrived during morning movement vibrating with enough energy to power the decorations.

"It's Christmas."

"I've heard."

"Dinner later."

"Apparently."

"Canteen."

"You bought canteen four days ago."

"Christmas canteen has ceremonial value."

Darren stared at him.

"Stuart teaching you words?"

"Ceremonial? I knew that."

Bruce appeared wearing yesterday's crown.

"He didn't."

Josh ignored him.

The three of them shared snacks on a table with Connor, Jamo and Yusuf drifting in and out.

For a while Darren forgot to monitor himself.

That was rare.

He laughed.

Ate.

Listened to Josh explain why one chocolate bar was objectively superior to another with the seriousness of a barrister.

He felt almost normal.

The danger was that normal was exactly what Darren had started making his whole world look like to Josh.

Today he refused to think about that.

One day.

He wanted one day where being Josh's mate was only good.

---

## MACCA

Macca got a call from home before lunch.

His daughter told him what Santa had brought.

His son argued in the background.

His partner sounded exhausted.

At one point the phone was passed around so quickly Macca stopped knowing who he was speaking to.

He loved every second.

Then the call ended.

That was Christmas in prison.

Ten minutes could be perfect and the eleventh could remind you where you were.

Macca sat alone afterwards.

Connor found him.

"Good?"

"Yeah."

"Actually?"

Macca smiled.

"Actually. That's why it hurts."

Connor understood.

---

## TYLER

Tyler's mum cried.

He tried to make her laugh.

It worked twice.

Then she said she had set a place at the table for him by accident.

Tyler stopped talking.

"Ty?"

"Yeah."

"Sorry."

"Don't be stupid."

"Language."

Tyler laughed with wet eyes.

His mum had a supernatural ability to parent him through any setting.

"Next Christmas," she said.

Tyler did not promise.

That was progress too.

"Love you, Mum."

He had not said that loudly on the wing before.

Today he did.

Nobody laughed.

That taught him something about the men he had been frightened of.

---

## STUART

Stuart prayed before Christmas dinner.

Connor waited.

Jamo, sitting nearby, waited too.

Josh tried to start eating, saw everyone else waiting and froze with a fork halfway up.

"Oh. Sorry."

Stuart finished.

"Amen."

Josh immediately ate.

Connor laughed.

The dinner was better than ordinary prison food and worse than almost every Christmas dinner Connor remembered.

That somehow made it meaningful.

They argued about potatoes.

Bruce attempted to trade one item for another under a system only Bruce understood.

Yusuf accused him of creating a Christmas black market in gravy.

Bruce replied that gravy was a human right.

For twenty minutes the table sounded like family without anybody saying so.

Stuart noticed.

He kept that observation to himself.

---

## OFFICER BENNETT

Bennett was not working the whole day.

He should have been relieved.

Instead he spent the morning checking his phone every time it moved.

Nothing came.

By lunch the silence was worse than a message.

His family thought he was distracted because of work.

Work thought he was distracted because of family.

Bennett was running out of places to hide the truth between them.

At 14:17 a message arrived.

Two words.

**Merry Christmas.**

No threat.

No demand.

Bennett went cold anyway.

He deleted it.

Then, for the first time, wrote the words down on paper before he could pretend later that it had not happened.

He folded the paper and put it in a drawer.

It was not confession.

It was the first thing he had preserved instead of erased.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer worked part of Christmas Day.

She watched prisoners make the best of a place designed without celebration in mind.

Connor and Jamo laughing again.

Macca quiet after a phone call.

Josh overexcited.

Stuart saying grace.

Darren looking better for a few hours than Mercer had seen him look in months.

Craig sat with Connor later, talking quietly.

Mercer passed.

Craig looked up.

"Merry Christmas, Officer Mercer."

"You've already said that."

"Yesterday. Different day."

Connor grinned.

Mercer looked at him.

"Blake."

"I didn't do nothing."

"That's rarely reassuring."

She moved on.

Behind her Connor laughed.

Mercer smiled before she could stop herself.

Nobody saw.

She hoped.

---

## CONNOR — NIGHT

After lock-up, Christmas became quiet.

Stuart read.

Connor lay above him thinking about home.

His little brother opening presents.

His mum cooking.

His girlfriend somewhere else.

The present with Connor's name under the tree.

"You all right?" Stuart asked.

"Yeah."

Connor paused.

"No. Bit."

Stuart smiled without looking up.

"That is a better answer."

Connor stared at the ceiling.

"You think people can come out the same?"

"Same as when?"

"Before."

Stuart closed the book.

"No."

Connor turned his head.

"Cheers."

"Nobody comes out of five years of life the same even if they are not in prison."

Connor considered that.

"So what matters?"

"Who you become."

Connor groaned.

"Church answer."

"Christmas. You asked for it today."

Connor laughed.

Outside the cell someone shouted Merry Christmas again.

This time Connor did not tell them to fuck off.


# SATURDAY 28 DECEMBER 2019 — AFTER

## CONNOR

The prison decorations looked sadder after Christmas.

Same tinsel.

Same tree.

Different meaning.

Connor had never noticed how quickly celebration turned into rubbish.

He spent the morning sweeping fragments of paper crown from the floor.

Bruce watched.

"Fall of the monarchy."

"You wore it for three days."

"Heavy is the head."

"It was paper."

"Symbolism has weight."

Connor shook his head.

Christmas was over.

Court was coming.

That was how quickly prison changed emotional weather.

---

## DARREN

Darren noticed Josh was tired.

Not dramatically.

Josh was still Josh.

Still talked too much.

Still argued about cars.

Still asked questions nobody else would think worth asking.

But he had spent more time around Darren's wider circle over Christmas.

Men Darren knew from bad days.

Men who made hours disappear in ways Darren understood too well.

No one had forced Josh anywhere.

That mattered to Darren because he kept repeating it.

Josh was an adult.

Josh chose who he sat with.

Josh chose what sort of prison he wanted.

Darren knew every sentence was technically true.

He also knew why he kept needing them.

"You all right?" Darren asked.

Josh yawned.

"Yeah. Just tired."

"Go sleep then."

"Can't. Gym later."

"You love gym now?"

"No. Leon's gonna call me lazy if I miss again."

Again.

Darren heard the word.

"You missed one?"

Josh shrugged.

"Before Christmas."

Darren looked at him.

Josh smiled.

"Don't dad me."

The line was a joke.

Darren did not laugh immediately.

That frightened him more than Josh being tired.

---

## LEON

Josh arrived late to gym and got the exact look he expected.

"You're late."

"But present."

"That's not how time works."

Josh grinned.

Leon pointed him where he needed to go.

Nothing serious.

Nothing worth recording.

Leon still noticed.

Routine changes showed before people admitted changes.

He knew that from himself.

Josh had once arrived early enough to annoy everyone.

Now he was late twice.

Leon filed it away.

Not as gossip.

As concern.

---

## GIRLFRIEND — PHONE

Connor finally spoke to his girlfriend after Christmas.

The conversation was fine.

That was the word both of them would have used.

Fine.

She had enjoyed the night out.

Yes, the man Connor knew had been there.

No, nothing had happened.

Connor believed her because he had no reason not to.

He also heard distance.

"You sound different," he said.

She sighed.

"Connor, everything is different."

There it was.

Not accusation.

Not betrayal.

Truth.

"I know."

"Do you?"

Connor stared at the wall.

"I'm trying."

She went quiet.

"I know."

The words were kind.

They did not reassure him.

---

## MASON

Mason watched Connor after the call.

"Bad?"

Connor shrugged.

"Fine."

Mason nodded.

"Terrible word, fine. Covers everything from mild inconvenience to divorce."

Connor laughed despite himself.

"She says everything's different."

"It is."

"You're all so helpful."

Mason smiled.

"You want comforting lies, ask Bruce."

Bruce called from farther away:

"I heard my department!"

Connor laughed.

Mason's face softened.

"Your outside life isn't betraying you by moving. It's moving because life does."

Connor looked at him.

"Same thing Stuart said."

"Annoying when the Christians and repeat offenders agree."

"Very."

Mason walked away.

Connor stood beneath dying Christmas tinsel and understood that the hardest part of remand was not simply waiting for court.

It was watching every other clock continue.


# TUESDAY 31 DECEMBER 2019 — MIDNIGHT

## CONNOR

New Year's Eve made everybody count things.

Months left.

Court dates.

Birthdays missed.

Years already served.

Years expected.

Connor had been inside one hundred and two days.

He knew because Josh had worked it out and announced it as if presenting a certificate.

"Hundred and two."

Connor looked at him.

"Why do you know that?"

"Dates."

"That's not an answer."

"I counted."

"That's worse."

Josh grinned.

Connor had entered Lewes in September believing his life had stopped.

Now the year itself was ending without permission.

---

## CONNOR AND SOLICITOR — LEGAL VISIT

His solicitor had given him something close to a sentencing window.

January.

Not guaranteed.

Nothing in court was guaranteed except waiting.

Connor had already indicated his intention to plead guilty. The case was now moving toward the part he had feared since police custody: somebody saying a number that belonged to him.

Five years had been discussed as possible.

Not promised.

Connor carried the figure carefully.

Five.

Back in September he had imagined fifteen until it became a private sentence.

Five should have sounded horrific.

Instead part of him heard relief.

That disturbed him.

He told Craig later.

"Five might be where it lands."

Craig nodded.

"Might."

"That's good, innit?"

Craig looked at him.

"You want me to tell you five years is good?"

Connor frowned.

"Compared to fifteen."

"Compared to fifteen, yes. Compared to being home tonight, no."

Connor looked away.

Craig had a talent for removing the comparison Connor was using to feel better.

"Still," Connor said. "I'll take five."

"You haven't been given it yet."

"I know."

"Then don't serve the sentence twice."

Connor smiled.

"That's almost positive from you."

"Don't get used to it."

---

## CRAIG — SEEN THROUGH MALIK

Craig's own court world had become harder to read.

Malik heard three versions of it on New Year's Eve.

Trial soon.

Plea negotiations.

More charges.

Charges dropped.

Massive sentence.

Not as bad as people thought.

Malik believed none completely.

Craig did not explain himself.

That made information breed around him.

The less a man said, the more prison said for him.

Malik had once loved that kind of vacuum.

Now he treated it with caution.

He knew too much about what stories could do after they left the mouth.

---

## PHONE CALL

Somewhere outside Lewes, a man said Craig's case was moving.

The response came through another voice, calm enough to make the message feel heavier.

"New year changes nothing."

Nobody in Lewes heard Big Sharky say it directly.

By the time the sentence reached the wing days later, it would have acquired threats, dates and promises that had never been spoken.

The original was simpler.

New year changes nothing.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer worked late enough to hear prisoners shouting the countdown through doors.

Ten.

Nine.

Eight.

Some voices enthusiastic.

Some mocking.

Some absent.

Bennett was not on shift.

Mercer found herself relieved.

Then worried about why.

Craig was behind his door somewhere on C.

Connor too.

For once the whole prison counted the same seconds.

Three.

Two.

One.

Noise burst through the building.

Happy New Year.

Mercer stood in a corridor built long before any of them were born and thought how little the prison cared what year people called it.

---

## CONNOR — CELL

Josh shouted Happy New Year through the wall.

Bruce shouted something about tax years that nobody understood.

Jamo yelled abuse from another cell.

Stuart laughed below Connor.

Connor lay on the bunk listening.

"New year," he said.

"Yes," Stuart replied.

"You reckon it'll be better?"

Stuart considered it.

"Different."

Connor groaned.

"You can never just say yes."

"You would not believe me."

Fair.

Connor closed his eyes.

2020.

The year he would be sentenced.

The year Craig's case would end.

The year Stuart hoped to go home.

The year Macca's children would get older without waiting.

The year Tyler might stop pretending.

The year Josh was certain prison would become a story he told later.

The year Darren did not dare imagine.

Connor listened to the noise fade.

Midnight had changed the number on the calendar.

Nothing else had moved yet.
