# TUESDAY 12 NOVEMBER 2019 — WHAT PEOPLE KNOW

## OFFICER BENNETT

Officer Bennett had started checking his phone before he reached the staff car park.

That was new.

He used to leave the prison, get into his car and feel the shift begin to fall off him.

Not disappear.

Nothing disappeared that neatly.

But the gate used to mean something.

Now the gate only changed which side of the problem he was standing on.

The message was waiting.

Not long.

Not dramatic.

A reminder dressed as a question.

Bennett read it once.

Then again.

His stomach tightened.

He put the phone away and immediately took it back out.

Deleted the message.

Regretted deleting it.

Same routine as before.

He had become a man performing the same panic with minor variations.

"You all right?"

Bennett looked up sharply.

Senior Officer Patel was walking toward him.

"Yeah."

Patel stopped.

"You jumped."

"Didn't hear you."

"Clearly."

Bennett forced a smile.

"Long day."

Patel looked at him for a second too long.

"Go home."

Bennett nodded.

"Planning to."

He walked to the car.

Home no longer felt like the opposite of prison.

That frightened him more than the messages did.

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer had stopped believing Bennett was merely tired.

Tired people complained.

Bennett monitored.

Doors.

Phones.

Who was behind him.

Who had entered a staff room.

Which prisoner had looked at him for half a second too long.

He was trying too hard to seem normal.

Mercer recognised the pattern without yet understanding the cause.

She saw him the next morning at handover.

"Bennett."

He looked over.

"Yeah?"

"Coffee?"

"Already had one."

"That wasn't really the question."

He gave her a tired look.

Mercer lowered her voice.

"Something going on?"

"No."

"Outside?"

His face changed.

Barely.

Enough.

"Just family stuff," he said.

Mercer nodded.

She did not believe him.

More importantly, Bennett knew she did not believe him.

That made the conversation end.

"All right," Mercer said.

He looked relieved too quickly.

She watched him leave.

There were professional ways to handle concerns.

There were also human ways.

Mercer had not yet decided which Bennett needed.

## TYLER — C WING

The prisoner came back for Tyler after lunch.

Of course he did.

Tyler had spent eleven days hoping the problem might become bored and choose somebody else.

Problems rarely had that level of consideration.

"You sorted that thing?"

Tyler nodded too quickly.

"Yeah, nearly."

"Nearly?"

"My cousin's been busy."

The man looked at him.

Tyler kept his face still.

"Thought you said one phone call."

There it was.

A sentence Tyler himself had invented returning like evidence.

"It is. He just hasn't picked up yet."

"So it ain't one phone call then, is it?"

Tyler smiled as if they were joking.

The man did not.

"Tomorrow," he said. "Let me know."

Then he walked away.

Tyler's stomach twisted.

He turned and found Bruce standing three feet away eating crisps.

"How long have you been there?"

Bruce looked around.

"Existentially? Thirty-two years. Here? Bit less."

Tyler stared at him.

"You hear that?"

"Enough."

"Don't say nothing."

Bruce looked offended.

"I'm famous for discretion."

Tyler actually laughed.

Bruce chewed.

Then said:

"You should probably tell him your cousin isn't Batman."

Tyler's smile disappeared.

"He does know people."

"Probably. Most people know people."

"You don't get it."

Bruce shrugged.

"I get you've built a shed and now you're upset someone wants to live in it."

Tyler stared at him.

"What?"

"Metaphor."

"It's shit."

"Still accurate."

Bruce walked away.

Tyler hated him for being right in a language nobody else would have chosen.

## CONNOR

Connor found Tyler sitting alone later.

That alone was unusual.

Tyler normally sat where the most people could hear him.

Connor leaned against the rail.

"You sort your cousin thing?"

Tyler looked up.

"Yeah."

Connor waited.

Tyler sighed.

"No."

Connor nodded.

"Better answer."

"He came back today."

"Who?"

"The bloke asking."

Connor sat beside him.

"And?"

"I said tomorrow."

Connor laughed softly.

Tyler looked annoyed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Don't do that."

Connor smiled.

"Sorry. It's just every time you're scared of admitting the last thing, you create a new thing."

Tyler looked away.

Connor recognised the face.

Not because Tyler looked like Connor.

Because pride did.

"Tell him straight," Connor said.

Tyler shook his head.

"Can't now."

"Why?"

"Because I've been saying it for weeks."

"Exactly."

"People will think I'm a mug."

Connor looked down the wing.

Kez came to mind again.

Twenty percent.

Twenty-five after the argument.

Craig asking who carried the risk and who understood the deal.

Connor had felt like a mug too.

He had nearly made the feeling cost him more.

"Sometimes you are a mug," Connor said.

Tyler looked at him.

"Cheers."

"I'm serious. Better to be a mug for one conversation than keep paying for it."

Tyler stared ahead.

"Easy for you to say. Everyone rates you."

Connor nearly laughed.

"You think that fixes anything?"

Tyler shrugged.

"Must help."

Connor looked at him.

There it was again.

Somebody newer seeing Rush instead of Connor.

He wondered how many of his own mistakes Tyler could not see because Connor's nickname stood in front of them.

"Mate," Connor said, "half the reason people know me is because I did stupid shit in public."

Tyler smiled despite himself.

"Fair."

"Tell him."

Tyler did not promise.

Connor respected that more than he would have yesterday.

## OFFICER MERCER — C WING

Mercer noticed Bennett looking toward one particular cluster of prisoners and then deliberately looking away.

The action was small.

Small things were what worried her lately.

She looked across the wing herself.

Nothing obvious.

Connor talking to Tyler.

Darren with Josh.

Malik pretending not to watch everybody.

Craig farther back, reading something.

Ordinary.

Then Craig looked up.

Not at Mercer.

At Bennett.

Bennett moved away a few seconds later.

Craig returned to reading.

Mercer felt something cold settle in her stomach.

It did not mean Craig knew anything.

It meant he had noticed.

Those were different things.

She found herself wanting to know which.

That curiosity bothered her more than it should have.

## CONNOR AND CRAIG

Craig caught Connor later.

"Your officer looks nervous."

Connor frowned.

"My officer?"

"Bennett."

"Why's he mine?"

Craig ignored that.

"You've noticed?"

Connor thought.

He had.

Not enough to name it.

Bennett always looked tired lately.

"Bit jumpy," Connor said.

Craig nodded.

"Don't ask him why."

Connor laughed.

"Why would I?"

Craig looked at him.

Connor sighed.

"All right, fair."

"Some things become your problem because you go looking for the answer."

Connor smiled.

"Mason's got you saying his shit now."

"Mason says plenty of sensible things."

"Don't tell him. He'll be unbearable."

Craig almost smiled.

Officer Mercer passed at the far end of the landing.

Craig's eyes moved toward her for a fraction too long.

Connor noticed.

Craig noticed Connor noticing.

"Say nothing," Craig said.

Connor grinned.

"Didn't say a word."

Craig shook his head.

For once Connor obeyed the title before somebody had to explain it to him.

## TYLER — PHONE

Tyler rang his mum that evening.

"Ty?"

"Yeah."

"You sound tired."

"I'm fine."

"Mm."

That noise annoyed him more than a question.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You're doing a noise."

"I'm allowed noises."

Tyler smiled.

His mum asked about food.

Laundry.

Court.

Whether anyone had written.

Normal questions.

Tyler answered them.

Then she said:

"You don't have to make it sound easy for me, you know."

Tyler went quiet.

The wing moved behind him.

Voices.

Doors.

Somebody laughing.

His whole prison audience.

"I know," he said.

"Do you?"

Tyler looked down.

For the second time in two weeks, the truth reached the edge of his mouth.

*I'm scared.*

It did not come out.

But this time he did not replace it with a boast.

"It's not always easy," he said.

His mum was quiet.

"I know, Ty."

That was all.

It was the smallest honest thing Tyler had said since arriving.

He felt lighter afterwards.

Not much.

Enough to notice.
