# WEDNESDAY 20 NOVEMBER 2019 — TWO NAMES

## STUART — C WING

Stuart had noticed that Connor answered faster to Rush.

Not every time.

Enough.

If somebody called "Connor" from down the landing, Connor sometimes looked around as if checking whether there was another Connor nearby.

If somebody shouted "Rush", his head turned immediately.

Stuart found this fascinating.

Connor found Stuart finding anything fascinating deeply irritating.

"It's habit," Connor said.

"Of course."

"Don't say it like that."

"Like what?"

"Like you're about to turn it into a lesson."

Stuart smiled.

They were cleaning the cell.

Connor claimed he did enough cleaning at work and therefore should not have to clean his own cell.

Stuart had rejected the argument on the grounds that it was stupid.

Connor was wiping the table badly in protest.

"You like Rush," Stuart said.

Connor stopped.

"We've done this."

"No. We discussed the name. I am asking if you like the person."

Connor looked at him.

"What person?"

"Rush."

"It's me."

Stuart nodded.

"Then easy question."

Connor laughed.

"You don't half chat shit for a Christian."

"Christianity has no ban on conversation."

"Should consider one."

Stuart smiled and folded his towel.

Connor went back to the table.

After a while he said:

"People respect Rush."

Stuart did not answer immediately.

"And Connor?"

Connor looked at him.

There it was.

The annoying bit.

"People respect me."

"I hope so."

"What's that mean?"

"Nothing bad."

Connor stared.

Stuart smiled.

"You are learning suspicion very quickly."

Connor laughed despite himself.

"Everyone sees the story first," Connor said. "The attack. Craig. Whatever. Rush comes with a reason. Connor's just... Connor."

Stuart nodded.

"Maybe that is valuable."

"Being boring?"

"Being a person before being a story."

Connor looked away.

He hated when Stuart said something useful without making enough fuss for Connor to dismiss it.

## JAMO

Jamo heard somebody call Rush from below and watched Connor answer.

Then Stuart called:

"Connor, you left your mug."

Connor turned again.

Jamo smiled before he could stop himself.

Two names.

Two versions.

He remembered Connor arriving with bags and no idea where the showers were.

Back then Jamo had been the experienced one.

That version of Connor had annoyed him less because he had needed Jamo.

Jamo recognised the selfishness in that now.

He did not like it.

His new cellmate appeared beside him.

"You waiting for someone?"

"No."

The man followed his eyes.

"Blake?"

Jamo looked away.

"Used to cell with him."

"Rush?"

"Connor."

The correction came out too fast.

The man shrugged.

"Same bloke."

Jamo watched Connor take the mug from Stuart and say something that made them both laugh.

"Yeah," Jamo said.

"Suppose."

---

## CONNOR AND JAMO

They met properly near the servery.

Not passing.

Not nodding.

Stopped.

Jamo was holding a packet of biscuits.

Connor looked at them.

"You got the good ones."

"Bought them. That's generally how canteen works."

Connor smiled.

"Thought you might've stolen them off your snoring boyfriend."

Jamo stared.

"Who told you he snores?"

Connor grinned.

Jamo closed his eyes.

"Bruce."

"Obviously."

"I'm gonna kill that ginger prick."

"Get in queue."

For a second it was normal again.

Connor looked at the biscuits.

Jamo sighed and held the packet out.

"One."

Connor took two.

"Prick."

"You offered."

"One."

"Didn't specify size."

Jamo laughed.

Then both went quiet.

The argument was still there.

Not in the way.

Nearby.

Connor broke first.

"Cell all right?"

Jamo looked at him.

"Yeah. Apart from the snoring."

"You wanted the move."

"I know."

Connor nodded.

There were several possible apologies available.

Neither chose one.

Jamo said:

"Stuart all right?"

Connor glanced back.

"Annoying."

"You like him then."

"Fuck off."

Jamo smiled.

"Thought so."

An officer called for movement.

Jamo stepped away.

"See you, Conn."

Connor looked at him.

Jamo had not called him that since the argument.

"Yeah," Connor said. "See you."

It was not reconciliation.

It was the first plank of a bridge neither man admitted they were building.

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer had started noticing the difference between Connor Blake and Rush as well.

Rush was what the wing shouted.

Blake was what paperwork said.

Connor was what Craig called him.

And now Stuart.

Mercer disliked how much she had noticed that.

She passed Craig during association.

"Everything all right?"

"Yes, Officer Mercer."

The formality should have reassured her.

Instead it reminded her there was a line between them.

Lines only became noticeable when somebody was standing near one.

Craig glanced toward Connor and Stuart.

"New cellmate seems good for him."

Mercer looked over.

Connor was laughing.

"Maybe."

Craig looked at her.

"You don't sound convinced."

"I'm paid to be suspicious."

"Must be exhausting."

"It has moments."

Craig almost smiled.

Mercer should have moved on immediately.

She did move on.

Just not immediately enough for her own liking.

Later, in the staff room, another officer called:

"Mercer, you done with that report?"

She looked up.

"Nearly."

Mercer.

Surname.

Work identity.

Normal.

She thought about Craig saying **Officer Mercer** and felt an absurd flicker of relief that he knew nothing more personal than that.

Then wondered why the thought had occurred to her at all.

## STUART — NIGHT

Connor lay on the top bunk.

Stuart was below, reading.

"You spoke to your old cellmate today," Stuart said.

Connor opened one eye.

"You watching me now?"

"Small prison."

"Apparently everyone's a detective."

"You seemed happy."

Connor stared at the ceiling.

"He's all right."

"I did not ask if he was all right."

Connor smiled.

"You really need new material."

Stuart closed the book.

"You miss him?"

Connor thought about the first nights on C Wing.

Jamo explaining everything.

Wet-towel arguments.

Talking through the dark.

The visit.

The spin.

The jealousy.

The chair.

"Bit," Connor said.

Stuart nodded.

No sermon.

No lesson.

That was almost disappointing.

Connor looked down.

"That's it?"

"What else?"

"Thought you'd say forgiveness or pride or Jesus."

Stuart smiled.

"You already know those words."

Connor laughed softly.

"Night, mate."

"Good night, Connor."

Connor closed his eyes.

Somewhere outside the cell somebody shouted:

"Rush!"

Connor did not answer.

Not because he rejected the name.

Because for once, he was already where he needed to be.
