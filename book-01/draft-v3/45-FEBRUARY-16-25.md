# SUNDAY 16 FEBRUARY 2020 — HOME

## MASON — M WING / INDUCTION

Mason had a resettlement appointment on Monday.

He mentioned it as if somebody had booked him for a dental check.

Connor heard from Jamo.

"Mason might be getting close to his door."

Connor looked over.

"Release?"

"Maybe. Stuff moving."

Connor found Mason later doing the same induction work he had been doing when Connor arrived in September.

New prisoner.

Property.

Questions.

The face Connor now recognised instantly.

*I am absolutely fine and would like everybody to stop noticing how absolutely fine I am.*

Mason caught Connor watching.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You've learned that word dangerously well."

Connor smiled.

The new prisoner moved on.

"Jamo says you're getting out."

Mason groaned.

"Jamo says many things. It's one of his defects."

"Resettlement though?"

"Meeting."

"You happy?"

Mason looked at him.

The pause answered before he did.

"Course."

Connor stared.

"You're lying."

Mason smiled.

"Look at you. Four months and suddenly Sherlock Holmes with a mop."

Connor did not let him escape into the joke.

"You're nervous."

Mason looked toward the wing.

He knew where everything was.

Who to ask.

What every noise meant.

How to fill a day.

Which doors opened when.

How to make prison small enough to survive.

"Outside's changed," he said eventually.

Connor understood in a way September Connor could not have.

"So have you."

Mason nodded.

"That's the worry."

Connor had once envied Mason for being comfortable in prison.

Now, for the first time, he saw the cost clearly.

A man could become so good at surviving somewhere that leaving became the new frightening thing.

"You'll be all right," Connor said.

Mason raised an eyebrow.

"You don't know that."

Connor laughed.

"No."

They both smiled.

The sentence had travelled through half the cast now.

Maybe that was what prison really taught: stop promising outcomes and start staying beside people while they found out.

---

## CONNOR

His own new sentencing date was Tuesday.

Two days.

This time Connor refused to treat it as real until somebody put him in a van.

Still, he packed mentally.

Five years sat in his head again.

Not safe.

Not guaranteed.

Possible.

He looked around C Wing.

If sentence came, the next question would be allocation.

Category C.

Another prison.

Another wing where nobody knew Rush.

The thought surprised him.

Four months earlier anonymity would have felt safe.

Now part of him feared losing the identity he had built.

Stuart's note was in his property.

**YOU ARE RESPONSIBLE FOR THE MAN YOU PRACTISE BEING.**

Connor read it again.

Maybe a new prison would answer a question Lewes could not.

Who was he when nobody had seen the ten seconds that made him Rush?


# TUESDAY 18 FEBRUARY 2020 — FIVE YEARS

## CONNOR — COURT

This time the hearing happened.

Connor almost did not believe it while it was happening.

He sat listening to professionals describe his offence in sentences cleaner than the memory.

Aggravated burglary.

Stolen watches.

The Mercedes.

The blood evidence.

The allegation of a firearm present during the offence.

His guilty plea.

His age.

His record and circumstances.

Things said in mitigation.

Things said against him.

Connor heard all of it through the noise of one question.

*How long?*

The judge spoke about seriousness and consequence.

Connor looked straight ahead.

Then the number arrived.

Five years' imprisonment.

For half a second Connor felt nothing.

Then relief hit him so hard he nearly hated himself for it.

Five years.

Five.

Not fifteen.

Not twelve.

Not ten.

Five.

He knew how insane that relief was.

Five years was birthdays.

Christmases.

His little brother growing.

His mum getting older.

Five years was not good news.

Yet Connor's imagination had been sentencing him to worse since September, and the real number felt almost merciful by comparison.

He looked toward his barrister.

The man gave him a small nod.

Connor exhaled.

The waiting was over.

Another kind of time had begun.

---

## CONNOR — HOLDING CELL

His solicitor came down afterwards.

"You understand the sentence?"

Connor nodded.

"Five."

There would be paperwork, calculation of time already served and decisions about where he went next.

Connor listened without trying to turn any of it into an exact release date in his head.

He had learned what dates did when you loved them too early.

"How do you feel?" the solicitor asked.

Connor laughed.

"Happy. Which is fucked."

The solicitor understood.

"Relieved."

"Yeah."

Connor sat back.

"Five years, man."

The number sounded different now it belonged to a judge rather than fear.

---

## CONNOR — RETURN TO LEWES

The wing knew before he reached the cell.

"Five!"

Someone shouted it like a football score.

Connor laughed despite himself.

Jamo reached him first.

"Five?"

"Yeah."

Jamo hugged him briefly.

"That's decent."

Connor pulled back.

"Everyone needs to stop calling five years decent."

"You started it."

"Fair."

Macca shook his hand.

Tyler slapped his shoulder.

Bruce looked disappointed.

"Was hoping for a more mathematically interesting number."

"Cheers."

"Seven has character."

"Fuck off."

Darren came last.

He looked worse since Josh.

Still standing.

Still Darren.

"Five," he said.

Connor nodded.

Darren smiled faintly.

"Josh would've said you can buy the M3 in five years with future money."

Connor laughed, then covered his mouth.

The laughter hurt.

"Yeah," he said. "He would've."

They stood quietly for a moment.

Josh remained present in the space between jokes.

---

## CRAIG

Craig found Connor later.

"Five."

"Yeah."

"How you feeling?"

Connor thought about lying.

"Relieved."

Craig nodded.

No judgement.

"Feels wrong."

"Doesn't mean you wanted prison. Means you feared worse."

Connor looked at him.

"Stuart said relief can be strange."

"Stuart sounds irritating."

Connor laughed.

"You would've liked him."

"Probably not."

Craig's own case was days from its end.

Connor knew it.

Neither mentioned it yet.


# THURSDAY 20 FEBRUARY 2020 — THE NUMBER

## MALIK

Craig's sentence reached C Wing as a number before it reached as meaning.

Twenty-two years.

Someone said twenty-four first.

Someone else said twenty.

Then the reliable version settled.

Twenty-two.

Malik did something unusual.

He waited.

Confirmed.

Then still did not announce it.

Everybody knew anyway.

The wing changed around the number.

Men who had spent months treating Craig as permanent suddenly remembered he was passing through.

Twenty-two years meant a different estate.

Different security.

Different prisons.

The word dispersal started appearing again.

So did Big Sharky.

Somebody said the two men would end up in the same place.

Somebody said never.

Somebody claimed it was already arranged.

Malik believed the sentence.

He treated everything after it as weather.

That was growth too.

---

## CONNOR

Connor found Craig sitting as calmly as if somebody had given him a delayed train time.

"Twenty-two?"

Craig looked up.

"That's what the judge said."

Connor sat opposite.

For once he had no joke.

"Fuck."

"Yeah."

The simple agreement made the number feel heavier.

Connor had spent months watching people mythologise Craig into something beyond ordinary fear.

Twenty-two years reduced everything to time.

No reputation could make twenty-two small.

"You knew it could be that?"

"I knew it could be bad."

"You all right?"

Craig looked at him.

Connor almost laughed at himself.

The same useless question everybody used.

Craig saved him.

"No."

Connor went still.

It was the first time he remembered Craig answering that question plainly.

Then Craig added:

"I'll deal with it."

There was the man everyone knew again.

But Connor had heard the no first.

That mattered.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer knew the sentence from official channels before the wing had finished inventing it.

Twenty-two years.

She read the entry twice.

Her professional brain moved first.

Security review.

Allocation.

Transfer.

Court outcome.

The personal reaction arrived underneath it.

Craig was leaving Lewes.

Not being released.

Moving deeper into prison.

She hated the relief and loss tangled together.

The boundary problem would physically leave her workplace.

That should have solved something.

It did not feel solved.

She saw Craig later.

"Officer Mercer."

He had gone back to the public name.

For a moment she almost wished he hadn't.

That thought was evidence enough of how far she had moved.

"Twenty-two," she said.

"Apparently."

"I'm sorry."

Craig looked at her.

"You're not meant to say that."

"Probably not."

He smiled faintly.

No one was close.

"Thank you, Leah."

Mercer held his eyes.

This time she did not tell him not to call her that.

That was a boundary crossed entirely in silence.

---

## PHONE CALL

Word of Craig's sentence travelled farther than Lewes.

Eventually a voice reported the number to people connected with Big Sharky.

The reply that came back was short.

"So he's coming deeper."

No celebration.

No threat.

Just recognition.

The prison system was moving two old histories closer together.

Book One still did not explain why.


# SATURDAY 22 FEBRUARY 2020 — ALLOCATIONS

## CONNOR

The word Category C stopped being theoretical.

Connor received notice that his next stage would be in the Category C estate.

The exact prison was less important to him in the first minute than the fact of leaving Lewes.

Leaving.

He had spent months wanting out.

Now out meant another prison rather than home.

"You know what's mad?" he told Jamo.

"Many things."

"I'm nervous."

Jamo laughed.

"Freshie again."

Connor stared at him.

"Don't call me that."

"Four months later and we've come full circle."

Connor smiled.

Jamo was right.

At Lewes Connor knew the showers.

Knew the jobs.

Knew the faces.

Knew which officer would answer and which would tell you to put an app in.

Knew where the floor stayed damp longest after mopping.

At the next prison he would be new again.

But not the same new.

He would arrive with a sentence and a story.

The question was which parts he brought with him.

---

## JAMO

Jamo had imagined Connor leaving during the worst part of their argument and enjoyed it.

Now it was happening and he hated it.

"You better ring when you land," he said.

Connor looked at him.

"How?"

"You know what I mean. Get word back."

"You gonna miss me?"

"No."

Connor smiled.

"Face."

"Fuck off."

They both laughed.

Jamo looked toward Connor's old cell.

"First day you didn't know where the showers were."

"Everyone keeps bringing that up."

"Because it was funny."

"You were six months in acting like governor."

"I knew loads."

"You knew laundry day."

"Exactly. Power."

The old rhythm was back without the old room.

That was enough.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Craig's allocation was moving toward the high-security/dispersal system.

No surprise.

Still, seeing it written made his departure real.

Mercer processed the information professionally.

She did not ask where exactly until the system required her to know.

She had learned something from Connor too.

Do not create extra information simply because uncertainty makes you uncomfortable.

Bennett was now dealing with formal consequences and support after finally reporting the coercion.

His immediate outside pressure had receded.

Mercer's own compromise remained invisible to most people.

That did not make it harmless.

Craig would leave soon.

She wondered whether distance repaired boundaries.

Experience told her distance mostly revealed whether the boundary had existed inside you in the first place.

---

## BRUCE

Bruce heard Craig was going high security and nodded as if receiving a weather forecast.

Connor stared at him.

"That's it?"

"What?"

"Everyone's talking about it."

"Everyone talks about rain too."

"You don't care?"

Bruce looked at Craig farther down the wing.

"Course I care."

Then he smiled.

"If you see him before I do, tell him he owes me a tea."

Connor frowned.

"Why would you see him?"

Bruce considered the question.

"Life's long. Prisons are nosy."

"That means nothing."

"Most prophecies do at first."

Bruce walked away.

Connor watched him.

Somehow that felt less like a joke than it should have.


# SUNDAY 23 FEBRUARY 2020 — THE CHOICE AGAIN

## CONNOR — C WING

The final test did not arrive looking important.

They rarely did.

Tyler was arguing with another young prisoner near the tables.

Nothing serious at first.

A stupid comment.

A reply.

People noticing.

The audience making the argument heavier simply by existing.

Connor was several feet away with Jamo.

"Tyler," Connor called.

Tyler ignored him.

The other lad stepped closer.

Tyler did too.

A shoulder bumped.

Someone in the crowd made the noise crowds make when they want a moment to become something.

Connor's body moved.

One step.

The same instinct as September.

Trouble.

Move toward it.

Rush.

Then Mason's voice appeared with irritating clarity.

Hear all.

See all.

Say nothing.

Not literally nothing.

Understand before you volunteer.

Connor stopped for half a second.

Saw Tyler's face.

Fear performing anger.

Saw the other lad glancing at the crowd.

Saw officers not far away.

Saw that nobody needed Connor to prove he was brave.

He chose.

Connor stepped in from Tyler's side, took hold of Tyler's sleeve and pulled him backward rather than driving himself into the middle.

"Come on."

Tyler resisted for one second.

"He—"

"I know. Walk."

"I'm not—"

"Ty."

The name stopped him.

Connor had never called him that before.

Tyler looked at him.

Connor shook his head once.

Tyler came away.

Staff moved toward the remaining argument and broke the atmosphere before it grew.

No alarm.

No segregation.

No new nickname.

Nothing anybody would talk about tomorrow.

Connor's heart was still racing.

He looked at his hands.

In September the choice had happened after his body moved.

Today the pause had arrived first.

That felt bigger than Rush.

---

## TYLER

"You called me Ty."

Connor looked at him.

"Worked."

"Only my mum calls me that."

"And Aaron."

"Fuck Aaron."

Connor laughed.

Tyler's anger was already leaking away.

"I would've hit him," Tyler said.

"Maybe."

"You think I would've lost?"

Connor sighed.

"You're missing the whole lesson."

Tyler smiled.

"Worth asking."

Connor shook his head.

Four months earlier Connor would have answered with status.

Who was tougher.

Who knew who.

Who the wing rated.

Today he said:

"Who cares?"

Tyler looked surprised.

Connor was too.

---

## MASON

Mason had seen the whole thing from farther down.

Later he found Connor.

"Very disappointing."

Connor frowned.

"What?"

"No alarm. No block. No legendary comeback."

Connor smiled.

"Growing up."

"Terrible."

Mason looked at him for a moment.

"You paused."

Connor nodded.

"Yeah."

"Why?"

Connor grinned.

"Hear all. See all. Say nothing."

Mason laughed.

"You finally understood it and still quoted it wrong for the situation."

"I didn't say nothing."

"Exactly."

Connor's smile faded into something more thoughtful.

"It ain't about saying nothing."

Mason waited.

"It's about not needing to be in everything. Knowing what you're actually looking at first."

Mason nodded.

"There you go. Took five months."

"Four."

"Felt longer."

Connor told him to fuck off.

Mason walked away smiling.

The frightened boy from induction had learned prison far too well.

At least he had learned one lesson that might help him leave it again.


# MONDAY 24 FEBRUARY 2020 — BECAUSE I KNOW YOUR NAME

## CONNOR AND CRAIG — C WING

They both knew the conversation was probably the last one at Lewes.

That made them avoid saying so for the first five minutes.

Connor sat beside Craig.

"Twenty-two's mad."

"You've mentioned."

"Five feels stupid next to it."

Craig looked at him.

"Don't compare sentences like cars."

Connor smiled.

"Josh would've."

The smile went from both of them.

Craig nodded once.

"Yeah."

They sat in silence.

Connor had spent months wanting to ask Craig about Big Sharky properly.

Now there was almost no time left and the question felt childish.

He asked anyway.

"Sharky."

Craig looked over.

"What about him?"

"You two actually know each other from before?"

Craig considered him.

"Long before."

"He thinks you did him dirty."

"He does."

"Did you?"

Craig looked away toward the landing.

For several seconds Connor thought that was the answer he would get.

Then Craig said:

"He was older. I looked up to him once. Things happened while he was inside. Things changed while I was outside. He thinks I chose something that happened to him."

Connor waited.

"Did you?"

"Not the way he thinks."

"That's not no."

Craig looked at Connor and smiled faintly.

"You've learned."

"So what's the truth?"

"Book Two."

Connor laughed.

Craig had no idea why the phrase sounded like a joke from somewhere else.

Then his face settled.

"Truth is longer than the version people use. That's all you're getting."

Connor accepted it.

For once.

"You gonna see him where you're going?"

"Maybe."

"You worried?"

Craig looked at him.

"Yes."

Again.

A plain answer Connor would never have expected in September.

Craig continued:

"Worry isn't the same as fear running you."

Connor nodded.

"Stuart would've loved that."

"I'm starting to dislike Stuart and I've never met him."

Connor laughed.

Then Craig's expression changed.

"Do your time."

Connor looked at him.

"Obviously."

"No. Listen. Do your five. Go home. Don't turn a sentence into a career because prison finally made you feel important."

Connor stopped smiling.

Craig rarely spoke about Connor's future directly.

"You saying don't end up like you?"

Craig looked at him.

"I'm saying you got enough information now to make your own mistake if that's what you want."

Connor nodded slowly.

"You gonna call me Rush before I go?"

Craig smiled.

"No."

"Still?"

"Still."

Connor already knew the answer.

He wanted to hear it once more.

"Why?"

Craig looked at him.

"Because I know your name."

Connor smiled.

This time the sentence did not feel like Craig refusing the prison identity Connor wanted.

It felt like Craig returning something Connor had nearly given away.

"Yeah," Connor said. "Me too."

Craig nodded.

"Good."

That was their goodbye.

Neither called it one.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer saw the two men talking and knew better than to interrupt without reason.

Craig would leave for the dispersal estate.

Blake for Category C.

Two men who had arrived at Lewes in the same week now leaving into completely different prison worlds.

Craig looked toward her after Connor walked away.

For a second Mercer considered going over.

She did not.

Professional boundary or cowardice.

She could decide later.

Craig gave the smallest nod.

Mercer returned it.

No first name.

No promise.

Nothing that could be written down.

That was sometimes how the most dangerous things survived.


# TUESDAY 25 FEBRUARY 2020 — TWO VANS

## CONNOR — MORNING

Connor packed his property differently from how he had unpacked it.

In September every item had felt like proof he did not belong.

Now everything had a place.

Cards.

Letters.

His little brother's drawings.

Stuart's note.

A few photographs.

The ordinary rubbish that became precious because prison controlled how much of home could fit in a box.

Jamo appeared at the cell door.

"You ready?"

"No."

Jamo smiled.

"Good."

Connor zipped the bag.

"Remember first night?"

"You threw a T-shirt at me."

"You called me freshie."

"You were."

"You were a prick."

"Still am."

They hugged.

Longer than either planned.

Jamo pulled away first.

"You were a nightmare cellmate."

"You moved out."

"Best decision I made."

"You cried."

"Didn't."

"Face."

Jamo laughed.

"Ring word back when you land."

"Yeah."

This time the promise meant something realistic.

Not everything will stay the same.

Just: I won't vanish without telling you.

---

## DARREN

Darren came to say goodbye holding Josh's car drawing folded in his pocket.

He had been rough since the death.

Some days better.

Some worse.

No miraculous transformation.

But he had stopped pretending nothing mattered.

That alone changed his face.

"Cat C," Darren said.

Connor nodded.

"Fancy."

"Luxury."

Darren smiled.

"Josh would've researched it in six minutes and told you where every car magazine was."

Connor laughed softly.

"He'd have wanted me to get an M3."

"C63."

"Wrong."

Darren's smile became real for one second.

"Take care, Connor."

Not Rush.

Connor noticed.

"You too, Daz."

Darren nodded.

He had no idea whether he could.

He knew he had to try.

---

## TYLER

Tyler stood with his hands in his pockets.

"So that's it?"

"That's prison. People move."

Tyler nodded.

"I'm gonna be bored."

"You've got Bruce."

"Exactly."

Connor laughed.

Tyler looked at him.

"You reckon I'll be all right?"

Connor thought about giving the old answer.

Definitely.

Instead:

"I reckon you'll be better if you stay Ty sometimes."

Tyler smiled.

"Don't ever call me that again."

"Your mum will."

"That's different."

"Exactly."

Tyler understood after a second.

He nodded.

---

## BRUCE

Bruce arrived late enough to make his entrance feel deliberate.

"Rush."

Connor turned.

Bruce pointed triumphantly.

"Still works."

Connor laughed.

"You stuck me with that."

"Gift."

"Disease."

"Most gifts become diseases if you keep them long enough."

Connor stared.

"That might be the worst thing you've ever said."

"Competitive category."

Bruce held out a biscuit.

Connor took it.

"Craig's going today too?"

Bruce nodded.

"Different transport. Different kingdom."

"You and kingdoms."

Bruce looked toward the gate direction.

"Tell him about the tea if you see him."

"You're definitely never seeing him."

Bruce smiled.

"We'll see."

Then, because Bruce could not leave a moment alone, he added:

"Don't become respectable. It'll confuse your face."

Connor laughed.

"See you, Bruce."

"Statistically likely."

Bruce walked away.

Connor had no idea how right he might be.

---

## MASON

Mason met Connor near movement.

Exactly where older prisoners had met thousands of younger ones leaving one stage for another.

"So," Mason said. "Hear all. See all. Say nothing."

Connor smiled.

"Not nothing."

Mason raised an eyebrow.

Connor continued:

"Just know when your mouth helps and when you're talking because you want to be part of it. Know what you're actually seeing before you move."

Mason nodded.

"Ruined my slogan."

"Improved it."

"Too long for T-shirts."

Connor laughed.

Then looked at Mason properly.

"You gonna be all right if you get out?"

Mason smiled.

"You don't know that."

Connor laughed again.

"Nah. I don't."

They shook hands.

Mason pulled him briefly into a hug.

"Don't get too good at this place," he said.

Connor nodded.

He understood now.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Two transfers on the same morning did not use the same vehicle or timetable.

That would have been too neat for real prison logistics.

Blake moved first through one chain of doors toward transport for the Category C estate.

Craig's high-security transfer was handled separately.

Mercer saw both processes from the staff side.

Connor carried his property with the confidence of someone who knew how reception worked now.

That made her remember the pale young man from September who could not look at one officer without looking at everything behind her.

"Blake."

Connor turned.

"Miss."

"Good luck."

He smiled.

"You too."

Mercer laughed once.

"I'm not going anywhere."

Connor's eyes flicked, just briefly, toward where Craig would later be moved.

Mercer knew he knew more than he said.

For once she appreciated it.

"See you, Officer Mercer."

"Take care, Connor."

She used his first name deliberately.

Not Rush.

He noticed.

Then he was moved on.

---

## CONNOR — TRANSPORT

The van had tiny windows.

Same cruelty as September.

This time Connor knew what to do with them.

He watched ordinary life appear in fragments.

A petrol station.

Houses.

Traffic.

Someone carrying shopping.

A dog walker.

The world had not become more meaningful.

Connor had become more aware of what it meant to be separated from it.

He thought about his mum.

His little brother.

His girlfriend, whatever happened next.

Jamo.

Stuart home somewhere outside prison.

Josh not going home at all.

Darren carrying him.

Mason frightened of freedom.

Tyler learning to be Ty.

Macca's silver star.

Leon and his broken routine.

Malik learning that information could stay still.

Yusuf refusing to belong to everybody.

Jinx choosing the men beside him over voices outside.

Bruce, inexplicably Bruce.

And Craig.

Connor had arrived at Lewes believing prison was one thing he needed to survive.

It had turned out to be dozens of different prisons occupying the same building.

His had changed every month.

The van turned.

Lewes disappeared from the little window.

Someone in the transport asked his name.

Connor looked over.

For half a second he considered the answer the wing had given him.

Rush.

Then Craig's voice came back.

*Because I know your name.*

"Connor," he said.

"Connor Blake."

The other prisoner nodded.

That was all.

No story.

No reputation.

Just his name arriving somewhere new before anything else could write on it.

---

## CRAIG — SEEN FROM OUTSIDE

Later, a different vehicle carried Craig away from Lewes under a different security arrangement toward a different prison world.

He did not receive a POV.

Not yet.

Behind him, the wing began telling stories before the gates had fully closed.

Twenty-two years.

Dispersal.

Big Sharky.

Who Craig knew.

What Craig would do next.

Most of it was guesswork.

Some of it would become true.

---

## MASON — LEWES

By afternoon both men were gone.

Mason was back at induction.

Another new arrival stood holding property too tightly, trying not to look frightened.

"First time?" Mason asked.

The lad considered lying.

Mason almost smiled.

Lewes had already begun again.
