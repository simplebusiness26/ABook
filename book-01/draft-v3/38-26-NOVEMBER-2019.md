# TUESDAY 26 NOVEMBER 2019 — THE GATE

## OFFICER BENNETT

Officer Bennett used to think the gate meant the end of a shift.

Now it meant the beginning of a different kind of waiting.

He reached the car park and saw the message before he opened the car.

Another reminder.

Another expectation.

Nothing detailed.

Nothing he could point at and say *there, that is the exact moment everything became impossible*.

That was what frightened him.

The pressure had grown by degrees until ordinary life had started carrying it.

Shopping.

School run.

His phone vibrating while his child was talking.

His partner asking why he kept checking the window.

The prison had followed him home without ever leaving the building.

He put the phone away.

Then heard footsteps.

"Bennett."

He turned too fast.

Officer Mercer stopped a few feet away.

"You keep doing that."

"Doing what?"

"Looking like someone's fired a gun every time someone says your name."

Bennett forced a smile.

"Tired."

Mercer looked at him.

"No."

The word landed harder than a question.

Bennett frowned.

"No what?"

"No, you're not just tired."

He looked toward his car.

"I've got stuff going on."

"I know."

"Then leave it."

Mercer's face changed slightly.

Not offended.

Concerned.

That was worse.

"If it's affecting work, it isn't only outside anymore."

Bennett felt anger rise because anger was easier than fear.

"I'm doing my job."

"I didn't say you weren't."

"Sounds like it."

Mercer lowered her voice.

"I'm saying you don't look safe in your own head at the minute."

Bennett stared at her.

For one second he nearly told her.

Not everything.

Enough.

Then the imagined consequences arrived all at once.

Career.

Family.

Questions.

Investigations.

People outside learning he had spoken.

He shook his head.

"I'm fine."

Mercer closed her eyes briefly.

"Right."

He hated the disappointment in that one word.

He got into his car.

The gate was behind him.

Nothing felt left behind.

## SENIOR OFFICER PATEL

Patel had worked with enough officers to know when somebody was having a bad month.

Bennett was having something else.

He made small mistakes and noticed them immediately.

Jumped at routine conversations.

Watched prisoners who were not watching him.

Asked questions twice.

Then worked too hard to make up for all of it.

That last part concerned Patel most.

People hiding deterioration often became model employees for short bursts.

It gave them something measurable to control.

Patel caught Mercer after handover.

"You spoken to Bennett?"

Mercer looked at him.

"A bit."

"And?"

"Says family stuff."

Patel nodded.

"You believe him?"

Mercer did not answer immediately.

"I believe there's stuff."

Patel almost smiled.

"Very precise."

"I don't know what it is."

"Keep an eye."

Mercer nodded.

Then said:

"At what point does keeping an eye become doing something?"

Patel looked at her.

"When you've got something more than a feeling. Or when the feeling gets bad enough you can't ignore it."

Mercer nodded.

That answer applied to more than Bennett.

She wished it did not.

## JAMO

Jamo got another knock-back about visits orderly.

This time Connor had nothing to do with it.

No cell search.

No security issue attached to Rush.

No shared-cell problem.

Just no vacancy.

Maybe next month.

Keep doing education.

Keep your record clean.

Wait.

Jamo stood with the piece of paper in his hand and felt strangely empty.

For weeks he had blamed Connor because blame gave disappointment a face.

Now the system had disappointed him completely on its own.

That was harder to argue with.

Mason found him later.

"Good news?"

Jamo held up the paper.

"Apparently I'm excellent at waiting."

Mason nodded.

"Valuable prison qualification."

Jamo smiled.

Then looked across the wing.

Connor was cleaning near the stairs.

"I was harsh on him," Jamo said.

Mason followed his eyes.

"A bit."

"Cheers."

"You asked nobody, but you said it out loud. Dangerous."

Jamo laughed.

"He did bring trouble to the cell."

"Also true."

"And I was jealous."

"Also true."

Jamo frowned.

"Can two things not cancel each other out?"

"Rarely. Makes life inconvenient."

Jamo folded the paper.

He was beginning to understand that being right about one thing did not make you right about everything around it.

Connor had been learning the same lesson through Kez.

Prison liked repetition.

## CONNOR

Jamo showed him the letter without ceremony.

"Still no visits job."

Connor read it.

"That's shit."

"Yeah."

Connor looked at him.

"Nothing to do with me this time."

Jamo gave him a look.

Connor immediately regretted saying it.

"That came out wrong."

"Bit."

Connor handed the paper back.

"I mean—"

Jamo interrupted.

"I know what you mean."

A pause.

Then Jamo said:

"I was blaming you for too much."

Connor stared at him.

It was so close to an apology that both men became uncomfortable.

"You were being a prick," Connor said.

Jamo laughed.

"And you were being a bigger one."

"There it is."

"Not taking all the blame."

Connor smiled.

"Didn't ask you to."

Jamo looked at him.

Same words from their argument.

Different meaning now.

"We're all right, yeah?" Jamo asked.

Connor nodded.

"Yeah."

No hug.

No speech.

Jamo stole one of Connor's biscuits.

Connor told him to get fucked.

That was enough.

## TYLER AND JOSH

Tyler's social disaster had lasted less time than his fear of it.

A couple of people took the piss.

Bruce called Lee "Commissioner Gordon" for three days.

Then prison found newer entertainment.

Josh had never cared much either way.

"Your cousin actually called Lee?"

"Yeah."

"Normal name."

"What's wrong with Lee?"

"Nothing. Just expected something dramatic."

"Why?"

"Because you made him sound like the Avengers."

Tyler laughed.

"Fair."

Josh grinned.

They were looking at a car magazine.

Tyler pointed at one.

"That."

Josh shook his head immediately.

"No chance."

"Why?"

"Ugly interior."

"You can't even see the interior."

"I can sense it."

Tyler stared.

"You're an idiot."

"Experienced idiot."

Darren appeared behind them.

"Three weeks and he's experienced now."

Josh nodded.

"Relative experience."

Darren looked at Tyler.

"He's been saying that all month."

"Because it's true."

Tyler smiled.

For once he did not need to be the loudest person in the conversation.

That was progress nobody on the wing would have bothered recording.

## OFFICER MERCER — C WING

Mercer saw Craig watching Bennett again.

This time she went to him.

That decision bothered her before she even arrived.

"Something interesting?"

Craig looked at her.

"Depends."

"On?"

"Why you're asking."

Mercer folded her arms.

"You keep looking at Officer Bennett."

Craig's expression stayed neutral.

"So do you."

The answer irritated her because it was true.

"I'm his colleague."

"I know."

"What have you noticed?"

Craig looked toward the wing.

For a moment Mercer thought he would refuse.

Then:

"He looks frightened."

"Of who?"

Craig looked back at her.

"That's the question you're asking too early."

Mercer felt her jaw tighten.

"If you know something relevant to staff safety—"

"I said he looks frightened."

"You notice a lot."

Craig almost smiled.

"Occupational hazard."

Mercer should have left.

Instead she said:

"And Blake?"

Craig glanced toward Connor and Jamo laughing over something near the stairs.

"Learning."

"From you?"

Craig looked at her.

"From everyone."

That answer stayed with Mercer.

Prison changed people collectively.

No single mentor.

No single officer.

No single incident.

Everyone left marks.

She walked away.

Craig had told her almost nothing.

It still felt more personal than the conversation should have.

## OFFICER BENNETT — NIGHT

Bennett sat in his car outside home for six minutes before going inside.

The house lights were on.

His child was in there.

His partner was in there.

Normal life waiting on the other side of the front door.

His phone vibrated.

He did not look.

For ten seconds he imagined walking into work tomorrow and telling Patel everything.

Then he imagined every consequence after that.

He stayed in the car.

The phone vibrated again.

The prison gate was miles behind him.

He had never felt more inside it.
