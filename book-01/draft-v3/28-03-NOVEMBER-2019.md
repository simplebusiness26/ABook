# SUNDAY 3 NOVEMBER 2019 — ROOM FOR TWO

## CONNOR — C WING

The argument started over a chair.

That was what Connor remembered afterwards, although the chair had almost nothing to do with it.

There were two chairs in the cell.

One had become Connor's by repetition.

The other had become Jamo's by exactly the same process.

That morning Connor came back from cleaning and found Malik sitting in Jamo's chair while Jamo stood by the door.

"You all right?" Connor said.

Malik nodded.

"Need a word."

Jamo looked at Connor.

Connor did not notice.

Malik kept his voice low.

"People are still chatting about Kez."

Connor's face changed.

"About what?"

"Nothing major. Just saying he's been telling people you nearly lost your head over money."

Connor laughed once.

"Course he has."

"I'm telling you because it'll come back round."

"Let it."

Malik nodded.

"Fair."

Then he stood.

"Cheers for the office."

Connor smiled.

Jamo did not.

Malik left.

Connor turned.

"What?"

Jamo looked at the chair.

"Nothing."

Connor closed his eyes.

"Please don't say nothing."

"Why?"

"Because it never means nothing."

Jamo laughed without humour.

"You want the truth?"

"Yeah."

"I'm sick of people coming in here looking for you."

Connor stared at him.

"Malik sat down for two minutes."

"It's not Malik."

"Then who?"

"Everyone."

Connor looked around the tiny cell as if everyone might be hiding behind the toilet.

"No one is in here."

Jamo pointed toward the door.

"That's because they just left."

Connor laughed.

Wrong choice.

Jamo's face hardened.

"See?"

"See what?"

"You think everything's funny now."

"What are you talking about?"

Jamo stepped farther into the cell.

"I'm talking about five weeks ago you didn't know where the fucking showers were. Now people come to our cell asking what Rush thinks."

Connor's smile disappeared.

"That's not my fault."

"Didn't say it was."

"Sounds like you are."

"Because every time anything happens around you, I get part of it as well."

Connor knew where this was going before Jamo said it.

Visits orderly.

The search.

Association concerns.

The lost opportunity.

Jamo said all of it anyway.

"I did six months right," he said. "Education. No stupid shit. Kept my head down. Tried for something decent. Then you come in, jump into Craig's thing, people start turning up, cell gets spun, my name gets attached to yours and suddenly I'm a concern."

Connor felt the familiar defensiveness rise.

"I didn't tell them to knock you back."

"I know."

"Then stop blaming me."

"I'm not blaming you for the decision. I'm blaming you for acting like none of it touches anyone else."

That landed.

Connor disliked it enough to attack somewhere else.

"You're jealous."

Silence.

Jamo stared at him.

Connor knew immediately he had found the ugliest true part and used it as if it cancelled everything else.

Jamo nodded slowly.

"Yeah."

Connor had expected denial.

It wrong-footed him.

"What?"

"Yeah. I am a bit."

Jamo's eyes were bright with anger now.

"You got here and everyone knows you. Craig likes you. People call for you. You got a story. I spent six months being nobody. So yeah, sometimes that pisses me off."

Connor said nothing.

"But that don't make the rest untrue."

Connor folded his arms.

"So what do you want me to do?"

"I don't know."

"Exactly."

Jamo laughed bitterly.

"There you go again."

"What?"

"Winning arguments instead of listening."

Connor felt heat in his face.

"I'm not changing who I talk to because you didn't get a job."

Jamo looked at him for a long second.

"I didn't ask you to."

"Then what are we even arguing about?"

Jamo looked around the cell.

"This."

"What about it?"

"It doesn't feel like mine anymore."

Connor said nothing.

The chair finally made sense.

## JAMO

Jamo left before he said something he could not take back.

That was the mature version.

The less flattering version was that he left because his throat had tightened and he refused to stand in front of Connor looking like he might cry over a prison cell.

He walked the landing with nowhere specific to go.

That was one of prison's crueller jokes.

You could storm out of a room and still be inside the same building.

Mason found him near movement later.

"You look delighted."

"Great day."

"Fallen out with Blake?"

Jamo looked at him.

"How does everyone know everything?"

"Your face."

"Apparently everyone's a fucking face expert in here."

Mason smiled.

"You want advice?"

"No."

"Good. Saves time."

Jamo almost laughed.

Then he looked away.

"I want out the cell."

Mason's expression changed.

"Because you're angry today or because you've wanted out for a while?"

Jamo did not answer immediately.

That was answer enough.

"Put the application in," Mason said.

"Already did Friday."

Mason raised his eyebrows.

Jamo shrugged.

"Told you. Wanted out for a while."

"Then today's just the day you stopped pretending you didn't."

Jamo hated how accurately older prisoners could summarize things they had not lived through.

"Still don't want advice."

"Wouldn't dream of it."

Mason walked on.

Jamo watched him go.

Then laughed despite himself.

## OFFICER MERCER

Officer Mercer had seen cell arguments over almost everything.

Television volume.

Cleaning.

Food.

Windows.

Phone credit.

Someone breathing too loudly at three in the morning.

The real argument was rarely the thing named first.

Jamo approached her during association.

"Miss, I put an app in about moving cell."

"I know."

He looked surprised.

"You read it?"

"Occasionally we perform miracles."

Jamo almost smiled.

"Anything available?"

Mercer checked what she already knew.

There was a space elsewhere on the wing after another move.

Not a reward.

Not a punishment.

A bed needing a body.

Prison logistics often solved emotional problems by accident.

"There might be," she said. "No promises."

Jamo nodded.

"All right."

"Problem with Blake?"

Jamo looked away.

"Nah."

Mercer waited.

Jamo sighed.

"Just need a change."

She believed that more than the first answer.

"Fine. I'll see what's possible."

He walked off.

Mercer looked toward Connor's cell.

Blake was inside alone, moving things around with more force than necessary.

Young prisoners often believed the important part of an argument was who had been right.

Prison had plenty of time to teach them otherwise.

## DARREN AND JOSH

Josh found Darren eating biscuits and immediately took one.

Darren stared at the packet.

Then at Josh.

"Did I offer you that?"

"You were about to."

"Was I?"

"Yeah. I know your patterns."

Darren shook his head.

"You've known me three weeks."

"Long time in jail."

"Don't start talking like Connor."

Josh smiled and ate the biscuit.

Across the landing they could hear Connor and Jamo's raised voices through the open cell.

Josh glanced over.

"They falling out?"

"Sounds like it."

"Think they'll scrap?"

Darren looked at him.

"Why's that the first place your mind goes?"

"Because we're in prison."

"People argue outside prison."

"Yeah, but less dramatic doors."

Darren laughed.

Josh pointed at the biscuits.

"Can I have another?"

"No."

Josh took one.

"You're a terrible father."

Darren snatched the packet away.

"You're going up for adoption."

Josh laughed loudly enough that Connor looked out of his cell in irritation.

For a second Connor saw them as a pair.

Darren and Josh.

Not Darren the wreck and Josh the new kid.

Just mates.

He wondered when that had happened.

Probably the same way most things happened in prison.

While everybody was paying attention to something else.

## CONNOR — EVENING

At six twenty an officer appeared at the cell door.

"Mills. Get your kit together."

Jamo looked up from the bottom bunk.

Connor did too.

"Why?" Jamo asked.

"Cell move."

The answer was so ordinary it took the drama out of the argument completely.

Jamo stood.

Connor stared at him.

"You actually put in to move?"

Jamo did not look at him while he gathered his things.

"Yeah."

"When?"

"Friday."

That hurt more than Connor expected.

It meant the argument had not caused the decision.

It had revealed it.

Connor sat on the top bunk while Jamo packed.

Neither apologised.

Neither wanted to be first.

At the door Jamo finally looked back.

"I'll see you on the wing."

Connor nodded.

"Yeah."

Jamo hesitated.

"Don't let Bruce have my chair."

Connor laughed once.

"Fuck off."

Jamo smiled.

Then he left.

The door stayed open another few seconds.

Connor looked at the bottom bunk.

Empty.

For the first time since arriving on C Wing, the cell belonged only to him.

He should have enjoyed it.

He did enjoy it.

For about eleven minutes.

Then the room became too quiet.
