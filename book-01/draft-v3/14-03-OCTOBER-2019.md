# THURSDAY 3 OCTOBER 2019 — THE VISIT

## CONNOR

Connor woke up already nervous.

That annoyed him.

He had spent half the night telling himself there was nothing to be nervous about, which meant his first thought in the morning was naturally the thing he had been trying not to think about.

The visit.

Not the person coming to see him.

The other thing attached to it.

He sat up on the top bunk and looked down at Jamo, who was asleep with one arm hanging off the bed.

For a second Connor considered waking him and telling him everything.

Then he imagined the look.

*You agreed to what?*

No.

He got dressed quietly.

---

Visits made the prison feel briefly like the outside world had been brought indoors and told to behave itself.

Men who spent all week pretending not to care suddenly checked their hair in bits of reflective metal and asked each other whether their shirts looked all right.

Some talked too much beforehand.

Some went silent.

Macca had once told Connor there were only two kinds of prisoners on visit mornings: men pretending they weren't excited and liars.

Connor understood that now.

He should have been thinking about seeing someone from home.

Instead he was watching officers.

Every movement looked meaningful.

Every glance felt aimed at him.

By the time his name was called, his mouth was dry.

"Blake."

Connor stood.

Bruce, somehow nearby, looked him up and down.

"You look like you're going to court for a murder you forgot about."

Connor frowned.

"Cheers."

"Relax your shoulders."

"Why?"

"They're trying to leave without you."

Connor laughed despite himself.

Bruce nodded, satisfied.

"Better."

Connor walked away before Bruce could become more helpful.

---

The visit itself passed in fragments.

Home talk.

His mum asking whether he was eating.

His girlfriend asking when he thought he might be home.

Connor lying gently because he had no answer.

He told them prison was boring.

He did not tell them about Craig.

He did not tell them about the fight.

He definitely did not tell them why his heart kept kicking whenever an officer moved nearby.

The prohibited favour happened around him rather than as something he wanted to understand.

Connor did the small part he had agreed to do.

No cleverness.

No grand moment.

Just fear, a decision already made and the horrible awareness that if anything went wrong, the men who had called him switched on would not be sitting beside him explaining why.

At one point an officer paused close enough that Connor forgot what his girlfriend had just said.

"Connor?"

He looked at her.

"What?"

"I said your mum's been crying loads."

The sentence landed harder than the risk around him.

For a second he was twenty-one again rather than Rush.

"Tell her I'm all right."

His girlfriend looked at him.

"Are you?"

Connor smiled.

"Yeah."

He had become very good at that answer.

---

## JAMO

Jamo knew something had happened because Connor came back from visits talking too much.

That was Connor's tell.

When he was genuinely relaxed, he could sit quietly.

When he was pretending, he narrated his own life.

"Visit was all right. Mum's stressed. Girlfriend looked good. Food in visits is robbery. You know how much they charge for—"

"What did you do?"

Connor stopped.

"What?"

"You heard me."

"Nothing."

Jamo stared at him.

Connor looked offended.

That confirmed it.

"Mate," Jamo said, "you've got the face."

"What face?"

"The stupid face."

"You've made that up."

"I've lived with you a week. I've got categories."

Connor laughed.

Jamo didn't.

"Don't bring anything back to this cell," he said.

Connor's smile faded.

"I haven't."

"I'm serious."

"So am I."

Jamo held his gaze.

"I've got my visits thing going through. Education are backing me. I don't need people spinning this cell because you're enjoying being Rush."

Connor felt something defensive rise immediately.

"Nothing's happening."

"Good. Keep it that way."

Jamo turned back to his book.

Connor stared at him.

It was the first time Jamo had spoken to him like a problem instead of a mate.

Connor did not like it.

He liked even less that Jamo had a point.

---

## CONNOR — LATER

Craig did not ask Connor how the visit went.

He asked:

"Who asked you?"

Connor stopped walking.

"Asked me what?"

Craig looked at him.

Connor lasted about two seconds.

"How do you know?"

"Because you're twenty-one and you've spent all day looking like someone gave you a secret too heavy for your pocket."

Connor glanced around.

"Kez."

Craig nodded once.

"And you said yes."

It wasn't a question.

Connor shrugged.

"Wasn't a big thing."

Craig's expression changed slightly.

"That's what they told you?"

Connor felt irritated.

"Nothing happened."

"That isn't the point."

"Then what is?"

Craig moved closer, voice quiet.

"You think because people know your name now, they're inviting you into something."

Connor said nothing.

"Some of them are just looking for someone else to carry the risk."

"I can handle myself."

Craig almost smiled.

"That's exactly why you're useful."

Connor frowned.

Craig let that sit.

"Useful isn't the same as important, Connor."

The use of his real name made the sentence heavier.

"If you want to be somebody, stop volunteering to be the person who gets caught doing somebody else's stupid job."

Connor looked away.

"So what, you get other people to do everything?"

Craig's eyes stayed on him.

"I'm telling you to learn the difference between leading something and being used by it."

Connor wanted to argue.

Instead he thought about the officer pausing near him on visits.

About Kez saying *why would anything go wrong?*

About how quickly Kez would probably have disappeared if it had.

Craig stepped away.

"You got away with being stupid. Don't confuse that with being clever."

Then he left Connor standing there.

Connor hated the lecture.

Which was how he knew he would remember it.

---

## BRUCE

Bruce found Connor later with a mop bucket nearby.

"Craig tell you off?"

Connor looked up sharply.

"How do you know everything?"

Bruce shrugged.

"I don't. I know twelve percent of everything and present it aggressively."

Connor laughed.

"He didn't tell me off."

"Right."

"He didn't."

"You keep saying it and eventually one of us will believe you."

Connor shook his head.

Bruce looked at the bucket.

"That yours?"

"No."

"Good. Doesn't suit you."

Connor looked at him.

"Why?"

Bruce thought for a second.

"Too much future in it."

"What does that mean?"

Bruce walked away.

"You'll see."

Connor watched him go.

He had begun to hate that phrase almost as much as he hated Bruce being right by accident.
