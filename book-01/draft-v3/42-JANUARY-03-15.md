# FRIDAY 3 JANUARY 2020 — DATES

## CONNOR — C WING

The first useful thing 2020 gave Connor was a date.

He did not trust it.

Prison had cured him of trusting dates.

A date could be written on paper, repeated by three professionals and still turn into another date while you were getting dressed for court.

Even so, when his solicitor told him sentencing was listed for the following Tuesday, Connor felt his whole body change.

Tuesday 7 January.

Four days.

Four nights.

Then a number.

"You all right?" Jamo asked when Connor came back from the legal visit.

"Tuesday."

Jamo understood immediately.

"Sentence?"

Connor nodded.

"Looks like it."

"You know what you're getting?"

"No."

"Thought you said five."

"Said five might be where it lands."

Jamo looked at him.

"You're shitting yourself."

Connor smiled.

"Little bit."

That admission would have been impossible in October.

Rush did not shit himself.

Connor did, occasionally.

Stuart had made that distinction less embarrassing.

"You'll be all right," Jamo said.

Connor looked at him.

"You don't know that."

"No. But you told Macca the same thing once. Felt like my turn."

Connor laughed.

---

## STUART

Connor became impossible to share a cell with when he had a court date.

He cleaned things that were already clean.

Read the same letter repeatedly.

Asked questions with no answer.

"Five years," he said for perhaps the ninth time.

Stuart looked up.

"Yes."

"If it's five, that's decent."

"You have changed the definition of decent."

"You know what I mean."

"Compared with fifteen imaginary years."

Connor frowned.

"They weren't imaginary. Could've got something serious."

"The fear was real. The number was invented."

Connor sat down.

"You reckon I'll feel better after?"

"When you know? Probably."

"Even if it's five?"

"Certainty has value."

Connor nodded.

Waiting had become its own sentence.

Police station taught him that in three days.

Remand had spent three months proving it.

---

## CRAIG

Craig had court the following week too.

Connor found that out because Craig disappeared into the machinery more often now.

Legal visits.

Calls.

Papers.

People whispering versions of what was happening.

"You in next week?" Connor asked.

Craig looked at him.

"Maybe."

"That's not an answer."

"You've been here long enough to know better than to expect one."

Connor smiled.

"Tuesday for me."

Craig nodded.

"I heard."

"Course you did."

"How you feeling?"

Connor considered lying.

Then didn't.

"Sick."

Craig smiled faintly.

"Good. Means you understand it matters."

Connor stared at him.

"That's your comfort?"

"You want a hug?"

"Not from you."

"Then we're both relieved."

Connor laughed.

Craig's calm irritated him less than it used to.

Connor had started understanding that calm was not the absence of consequences.

Sometimes it was what people built around them.


# TUESDAY 7 JANUARY 2020 — NOT TODAY

## CONNOR — COURT

Connor woke before anyone called him.

He had slept in fragments.

Five years.

Six.

Seven.

Four.

The old fifteen returned at three in the morning just to prove it still knew where he lived.

By the time he was taken for court, he felt hollow.

The journey was familiar enough now to be worse.

He knew what waiting cells smelled like.

Knew how barristers said *we'll see* when they meant *I cannot promise you anything*.

Knew the order in which doors opened.

He had stopped being frightened by the process.

He was frightened by the result.

Then his barrister came to see him.

Connor knew from the face that something had gone wrong.

"What?"

"Sentencing isn't going ahead today."

Connor stared.

The sentence made no sense at first.

"What do you mean?"

"A report the court requires isn't ready and the matter's being put back."

Connor's jaw tightened.

"Put back to when?"

"We should have another date shortly."

"No. When?"

"Connor, I don't have it yet."

He stood up.

"I've been waiting since September."

"I know."

"No, you don't. You go home."

The barrister accepted that without defending himself.

That made Connor angrier.

"So I come all the way here to be told not today?"

"Essentially, yes."

Connor laughed once.

There was nothing funny in it.

Not today.

The same two words after his bail application.

Not today.

Court's favourite way of taking a whole future and moving it out of reach.

---

## CONNOR — RETURN TO LEWES

Jamo saw him first.

No kit.

No sentence sheet.

Face like murder.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

Jamo winced.

"Bad nothing?"

"Put back."

Connor kept walking.

People asked.

He stopped answering.

By the time he reached the cell Stuart already knew.

Of course he did.

Prison could not process an application in a week but could distribute disappointment before you got back from reception.

"Do not say anything spiritual," Connor said.

Stuart closed his mouth.

Connor threw himself onto the bunk.

"Five would've been fine."

Stuart looked up.

Connor corrected himself.

"Not fine. You know what I mean. I was ready."

"For certainty."

"Yeah."

Connor stared at the ceiling.

"Now it's back."

"What is?"

"All the numbers."

He hated admitting that.

Five had stopped being a possible sentence and become safety.

If it was not five yet, maybe it could still be everything else.

Ten.

Twelve.

Fifteen.

"Connor," Stuart said quietly, "a delay does not change the evidence or suddenly create a different offence."

Connor knew that.

Fear did not care.

"I know."

"Then be angry about the delay. Do not invent another case."

Connor looked at him.

"Mason been giving you lessons?"

"No. I simply also dislike nonsense."

Connor laughed despite himself.

The laugh helped less than usual.

Still helped.

---

## MASON

Mason found Connor later beating a mop against a stubborn patch of floor as if cleanliness had personally adjourned him.

"Floor guilty?"

"Fuck off."

"Strong defence."

Connor sighed.

"They put it back."

"Heard."

"Course you did."

Mason leaned on the rail.

"Waiting's worse once you think it's finished."

Connor stopped mopping.

That was exactly it.

"Yeah."

Mason nodded.

"Nothing wise to add."

Connor looked at him suspiciously.

"Seriously?"

"Sometimes things are just shit."

Connor laughed.

It was the most comforting thing Mason had said all week.


# FRIDAY 10 JANUARY 2020 — BORROWED WEIGHT

## TYLER — C WING

Tyler's problem was small enough that Connor should have left it alone.

That was how Connor knew later that the mistake belonged to him.

Tyler had argued with Ellis over canteen.

Not a criminal empire.

Not Big Sharky.

Not Craig.

Two young men, one disputed debt and enough pride to make three pounds feel like a matter of constitutional law.

"I don't owe him," Tyler told Connor.

"Then tell him."

"I did."

"Good."

"He's saying I do."

Connor looked at him.

"Then you're at the complicated stage."

Tyler did not laugh.

"He's making it a thing."

Connor saw Ellis farther down.

Older than Tyler by a few years. Not someone Connor feared. Not someone Connor knew well either.

Rush supplied the next thought.

Connor barely noticed it arrive.

He walked over.

"Leave Tyler alone."

Ellis looked at him.

"Nothing to do with you."

"I'm making it something to do with me."

That sentence felt good for approximately two seconds.

Ellis smiled.

"Craig know you're sorting his wing now?"

Connor's pride moved faster than judgement.

"Craig don't want stupid shit kicking off over pennies."

Silence.

Tiny.

Enough.

Ellis's expression changed.

Not frightened.

Interested.

"Craig said that?"

Connor should have stopped.

Instead:

"You heard me."

He walked away.

Tyler stared at him like he had just witnessed expertise.

Connor enjoyed that too.

That was the second mistake.

---

## MALIK

By lunch Malik had heard Craig had banned arguments over canteen.

That was impressive because Craig had done no such thing.

The story had grown from one sentence in under an hour.

Malik found Connor.

"You using Craig's name now?"

Connor's stomach tightened.

"What?"

"Ellis says you told him Craig's involved in Tyler's thing."

"I didn't say involved."

Malik smiled.

"Excellent. We're doing legal definitions now."

Connor looked around.

"It was nothing."

"Then why'd you need Craig?"

That annoyed him because it sounded like something Craig would ask.

"I was shutting it down."

"Maybe."

Malik walked off.

Connor stood still.

The sentence he had used came back differently.

*Craig don't want...*

Borrowed authority sounded heavier after you put it down.

---

## CRAIG

Craig found Connor before Connor found him.

That was never a good sign.

"What did I say about Ellis?"

Connor considered pretending not to understand.

Craig's face made it pointless.

"Nothing."

"Good."

"I was just stopping Tyler getting pressured."

"Using my name."

"I didn't threaten him."

"Didn't say you did."

Connor folded his arms.

"It's sorted."

Craig looked at him.

"Is it?"

Connor felt eighteen again.

"Yeah."

"Then go tell Ellis I never said a word about it."

Connor stared.

"Why?"

"Because I never said a word about it."

"He'll think I'm a mug."

Craig nodded.

"Maybe."

Connor waited for rescue.

None came.

Craig continued:

"You stood beside me once. That doesn't make my name yours."

Connor's face heated.

"I know that."

"Do you?"

The question hurt because Connor had known it intellectually and ignored it emotionally.

Craig pointed down the landing with his chin.

"Fix your own mess."

Then walked away.

Connor watched him go.

For the first time, being close to Craig felt less like protection and more like having somebody close enough to catch him pretending.


# SUNDAY 12 JANUARY 2020 — YOUR MESS

## CONNOR AND ELLIS

Connor spent most of Saturday avoiding a forty-second conversation.

That was embarrassing in itself.

On Sunday he found Ellis near association.

"Oi."

Ellis looked over.

"What? Craig got a new policy?"

A couple of men nearby heard.

Connor felt the old instinct rise.

Say something sharp.

Win the room.

Make Ellis regret the joke.

Instead Connor said:

"Nah. That's why I'm here."

Ellis looked interested.

"Go on."

"Craig never said nothing about you or Tyler. That was me."

The words hurt exactly as much as Connor expected.

Ellis smiled.

"So you were chatting shit."

"I was speaking for myself and made it sound like I weren't."

There was a difference.

Connor needed there to be.

Ellis considered him.

"Why tell me?"

Connor looked toward Craig across the wing, then deliberately looked back.

"Because it's my mess."

Ellis laughed once.

"Fair enough."

The surrounding audience lost interest faster than Connor expected.

No humiliation ceremony.

No collapse of Rush.

Two men went back to their conversation.

Someone asked about tea.

The world survived Connor admitting he had been wrong.

That was becoming an irritating pattern.

"Tyler still says he don't owe you," Connor added.

"He does."

"Then sort it with Tyler. Not me."

Ellis nodded.

"Fine."

Connor walked away feeling lighter and slightly stupid.

Craig had made him give back the weight he borrowed.

It turned out he could still stand without it.

---

## TYLER

"You told him?"

Connor nodded.

Tyler looked horrified.

"Why?"

"Because Craig never said it."

"But Ellis backed off."

"Not the point."

Tyler stared.

Connor recognised the expression.

Tyler had spent months believing strength meant maintaining the story once people believed it.

"What happens now?" Tyler asked.

"You sort your own canteen thing."

"Brilliant."

"Welcome to adulthood."

Tyler told him to fuck off.

Connor smiled.

There was a strange satisfaction in refusing to become Tyler's Craig.

---

## MASON

Mason heard about Connor correcting himself.

"Learning?"

Connor looked suspicious.

"Don't ruin it."

"Wouldn't dream of it."

"Craig made me."

Mason nodded.

"Still counts if you actually did it."

Connor leaned on the rail.

"I thought everyone would think I was weak."

"Did they?"

"No one cared."

Mason smiled.

"One of prison's greatest disappointments is discovering you're not always the main event."

Connor laughed.

The lesson about importance kept arriving in different clothes.

---

## CRAIG

Craig did not congratulate him.

Of course he didn't.

Connor found him later.

"Sorted it."

Craig nodded.

"Good."

"That's it?"

"What do you want? Certificate?"

Connor laughed.

"Could've said well done."

Craig looked at him.

"For correcting a problem you created?"

"When you say it like that..."

"That's how it happened."

Connor smiled.

Then Craig added:

"You did it though."

Tiny sentence.

Connor felt it anyway.

That was the problem with Craig's approval.

He gave so little that Connor noticed every gram.


# WEDNESDAY 15 JANUARY 2020 — SPACE

## CONNOR AND STUART

The argument did not begin as an argument.

It began with three people in the cell.

Connor.

Stuart.

Malik sitting in the spare chair talking quietly about something Connor had no business turning into a meeting.

Then Tyler appeared at the door.

Then Jamo shouted something from outside.

Stuart closed his book.

Connor noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Stuart was learning prison sarcasm.

Connor hated that he had taught him.

Malik left.

Tyler disappeared after asking a question.

The cell went quiet.

Stuart looked around.

"This is becoming an office."

Connor laughed.

"Malik sat down."

"Yesterday Bruce sat on my bed."

"That's Bruce."

"Exactly."

Connor smiled.

Stuart did not.

"I need somewhere quiet sometimes."

"It's a prison cell, mate."

"It is also where I live."

The sentence sounded familiar.

Jamo.

*It doesn't feel like mine anymore.*

Connor heard the echo and immediately resented it.

"So what do you want me to do?"

"Tell people not to come in constantly."

"They don't."

Stuart looked at him.

Connor knew he was lying to both of them.

"Fine. I'll tell Bruce not to sit on your bed."

"This is not about Bruce."

"Everything becomes about me with cellmates."

Stuart's expression changed.

"That is an interesting sentence."

Connor felt attacked by the calmness.

"What?"

"You said it, not me."

Connor stood.

"Maybe we shouldn't share then."

Silence.

There it was.

A sentence said to win five seconds that suddenly had administrative potential.

Stuart looked at him.

"Do you mean that?"

Connor should have backed down.

Rush hated backing down in rooms where nobody else could hear.

"Maybe."

Stuart nodded slowly.

"Then say what you mean."

Connor folded his arms.

"I want more space."

"A single cell?"

"Yeah."

Stuart looked at the tiny room.

"At least that is honest."

Connor expected anger.

Stuart simply picked up his book.

That made Connor feel worse.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

The application surprised Mercer.

Not because prisoners wanted cell moves.

That happened constantly.

Because Blake had written it.

**Compatibility / need for space.**

Nothing dramatic.

No allegation against Stuart.

No invented threat.

Just a request.

Mercer found Connor later.

"Problem with Stuart?"

Connor shrugged.

"Not really."

"Helpful."

"Just think we'd be better separate."

"He agree?"

Connor hesitated.

Mercer noticed.

"Blake."

"He knows."

"That wasn't the question."

Connor looked away.

Mercer had seen the friendship between them.

She had also seen Connor's cell becoming a social hub again.

Rush was taking up more space than Connor realised.

"If a bed opens, a move may happen," she said. "Don't assume that means you'll stay single."

Connor nodded.

"Fine."

Mercer almost told him to think about whether he wanted the consequence or simply the feeling of control.

Then decided prison would teach that efficiently without her.

---

## JAMO

Jamo heard before the move happened.

"You're moving Stuart out?"

Connor frowned.

"Who told you?"

"This is prison."

"Everyone needs a new line."

Jamo looked at him.

"Why?"

"Need space."

Jamo laughed once.

Connor's face hardened.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Say it."

Jamo shook his head.

"Nah. You'll hear it eventually."

Connor knew exactly what Jamo meant.

He had heard it once already.

A cell could stop feeling like yours when somebody else's life grew too large inside it.

Connor told himself this was different.

The fact he needed the sentence worried him.

---

## STUART — EVENING

The move happened quickly because prison was cruel like that.

Weeks for an application you desperately wanted.

Hours for a decision you suddenly wished had taken longer.

An officer appeared.

"Stuart. Get your property together."

Connor looked up.

Stuart nodded as if he had expected it.

He packed carefully.

Books.

Clothes.

Bible.

The ordinary pieces of a life reduced to what fitted in prison property.

Connor sat on the bunk.

"You know it's not personal."

Stuart looked at him.

"Most personal things are described that way when people want to feel less responsible."

Connor flinched.

"I didn't say I don't like you."

"I know."

"We'll still talk."

"Probably."

Connor hated probably.

At the door Stuart stopped.

"Connor."

Not Rush.

Connor looked up.

"What?"

"Enjoy the space."

No sarcasm.

That was worse.

Then he left.

Connor looked at the empty bottom bunk.

He had wanted a single cell since Jamo moved out.

Now he had one again.

This time it took less than eleven minutes to feel wrong.
