# THURSDAY 14 NOVEMBER 2019 — THE CHEAPER TRUTH

## TYLER — C WING

Tyler had spent two weeks discovering that lies had admin.

You had to remember them.

Update them.

Make sure new versions matched old versions.

Answer questions you had never expected anyone to ask.

By Thursday morning his cousin Lee had apparently spoken to three people, missed two calls, been away for work and had a problem with his phone.

None of those things were true.

Lee was probably at home eating cereal.

That thought finally annoyed Tyler enough to do something sensible.

The prisoner who had been asking him for help found him near association.

"You got that sorted?"

Tyler felt the old answer waiting.

*Yeah. Nearly.*

He swallowed it.

"Nah."

The man frowned.

"What?"

"I made my cousin sound bigger than he is."

There.

The sentence was out.

Tyler waited for the wing to collapse.

It did not.

The man stared at him.

"You what?"

Tyler forced himself to keep looking at him.

"He knows people. Just not like I made out. I was chatting shit."

A pause.

Then the man laughed.

Not kindly.

Not cruelly either.

Just because the truth was ridiculous.

"So I've been waiting two weeks for your cousin who's basically some bloke called Lee?"

Tyler's face burned.

"Pretty much."

The man shook his head.

"You're a dickhead."

"Yeah."

That answer surprised both of them.

The man looked at Tyler for another second.

"Why didn't you just say that at the start?"

Tyler almost laughed.

Because at the start he had wanted to be somebody else.

Because admitting fear had felt worse than creating a reputation.

Because prison had been full of men who seemed to know who they were and Tyler had arrived with nothing except volume.

He said:

"Dunno."

The man shrugged.

"Forget it. I'll sort something else."

Then he walked away.

Tyler stayed still.

That was it.

No fight.

No great humiliation.

No mysterious consequence waiting behind the next door.

He had spent fourteen days protecting himself from a conversation that lasted forty seconds.

Connor passed a minute later.

Tyler looked at him.

"Told him."

Connor stopped.

"And?"

"Called me a dickhead."

Connor nodded.

"Reasonable."

Tyler laughed.

"Then he walked off."

Connor smiled.

"Expensive, weren't it?"

"What?"

"All that lying for a forty-second conversation."

Tyler thought about it.

"Yeah."

Connor started walking again.

"Truth's cheaper sometimes."

Tyler stared after him.

"You been hanging around Stuart too much!"

Connor turned without stopping.

"Don't ever say that again."

Tyler laughed.

For the first time since arriving, being laughed at felt better than being believed.

## JAMO

Jamo's new cellmate snored.

This had not featured on the application form.

The first night Jamo told himself it was temporary.

The second night he considered whether suffocation with a prison pillow counted as a proportional response.

By the third he had learned the rhythm.

Three normal breaths.

One enormous noise that sounded like furniture being dragged across concrete.

Pause.

Repeat.

Connor had talked too much.

Connor left wet towels in stupid places.

Connor borrowed things and forgot they had once belonged to someone else.

Connor attracted people to the cell at exactly the wrong time.

Connor did not snore.

Jamo hated that this had become one of his strongest memories of him.

He saw Connor and Stuart laughing near lunch.

Stuart said something Jamo could not hear.

Connor shook his head and laughed again.

Jamo looked away.

He had wanted the move.

Still did, mostly.

The problem was that leaving a room did not remove the person from your sentence.

Connor was still on the wing.

Still Rush.

Still talking to Craig.

Still the person Jamo automatically looked for when something stupid happened.

Bruce appeared beside him.

"Regret migration?"

Jamo looked at him.

"What?"

Bruce nodded towards Connor's cell.

"New habitat. Poor sleep quality. Familiar nesting site occupied by Christian."

Jamo stared.

"How do you know my cellmate snores?"

Bruce shrugged.

"Walls."

"Walls told you?"

"Mostly him. Through the walls."

Jamo laughed despite himself.

Bruce nodded as if his work was complete.

"You miss him."

Jamo's smile disappeared.

"Fuck off."

"Very emotional answer."

Bruce walked away before Jamo could throw anything.

Jamo watched Connor again.

He did not miss the trouble.

He did not miss people appearing in the doorway asking for Rush.

He did not miss feeling like a passenger in his own cell.

He missed Connor.

That was unfortunately different.

## STUART AND CONNOR

Stuart had developed a theory that Connor used jokes to move away from questions.

Connor had developed a theory that Stuart needed a hobby.

"You are doing it now," Stuart said.

Connor looked up from his tea.

"Doing what?"

"Moving."

"I'm sitting down."

"With words."

Connor sighed.

"Here we go."

Stuart smiled.

They were talking about home.

Stuart had asked Connor what he missed most.

Connor had said decent food.

Stuart had called him a liar.

"Little brother," Connor admitted eventually.

Stuart nodded.

No smugness.

That made it easier.

"How old?"

Connor told him.

Then immediately regretted it because the number dragged fifteen imaginary years back into the room.

He looked away.

Stuart noticed.

"You think about sentence?"

"Everyone thinks about sentence."

"That was not my question."

Connor smiled without humour.

"You do that too much."

"And you avoid answers too much. We balance."

Connor laughed.

"When I first got nicked I convinced myself I was getting fifteen years."

Stuart's face changed.

"Do you still believe that?"

"No. Not really."

"But?"

Connor stared into the mug.

"Still don't like doing the maths with everyone outside."

Stuart was quiet.

Then:

"Prison makes time feel like property somebody took from you."

Connor looked at him.

"That's actually decent."

"Thank you."

"Don't ruin it."

Stuart smiled.

Connor thought about his mum.

His girlfriend.

His little brother sleeping in one of his T-shirts.

For all the attention Rush had brought him, none of it could touch those things.

That was becoming harder to ignore.

## MACCA

Macca's partner told him their daughter had been given a part in the school Christmas play.

Not the main part.

Macca did not care.

As far as he was concerned she had just been cast in the West End.

"What is she?"

"A star."

Macca laughed.

"Course she is."

"No, literally. One of the stars."

"Still counts."

His daughter came on the phone and shouted the information as if her mother had not already told him.

"I'M A STAR."

Macca smiled so hard his face hurt.

"I know, baby."

"We've got silver things."

"What silver things?"

"For our heads."

"Crown?"

"No, Dad. Stars."

She said it with the exhausted patience of a child forced to explain basic theatre to an adult.

Macca laughed.

The performance was in December.

He could not be there.

That fact arrived quietly beneath the happiness.

He kept it there.

"You better know all your words."

"I don't have words."

"Even easier. Smash it."

She giggled.

After the call Macca sat still for a while.

Connor passed and noticed his face.

"Good news?"

"Kid's in her school play."

Connor smiled.

"Nice."

"She's a star."

Connor nodded seriously.

"Obviously."

Macca looked at him.

Connor did not ask whether he would be there.

That was another thing the lad was learning.

Some questions already had answers nobody wanted spoken aloud.

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer saw Tyler looking lighter that afternoon.

She did not know why.

That was fine.

Staff did not need to know every private victory a prisoner had.

The impulse to know everything could become its own kind of problem.

She thought of Craig immediately and disliked the connection.

Lately curiosity around him had started arriving after shift as well as during it.

What did he notice?

Who did he know?

Why did men twice Connor's age change tone when Craig entered a conversation?

Why had he noticed Bennett was frightened before Mercer had properly admitted it herself?

She told herself these were professional questions.

Some were.

That was what made the others harder to separate.

Across the wing Craig looked up from where he was sitting.

Their eyes met.

Mercer looked away first because she had work to do.

That was the reason.

She used it twice in her own head.

The second time sounded less convincing.
