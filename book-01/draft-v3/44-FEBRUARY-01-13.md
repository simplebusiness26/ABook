# SATURDAY 1 FEBRUARY 2020 — EMPTY SPACE

## OFFICER MERCER

The confirmation came in the morning.

Josh had died in hospital.

Mercer had received bad news professionally before.

There was always a process around it.

Managers.

Healthcare.

Notifications.

Information controlled until family had been told.

Instructions about what staff could say and when.

The process mattered.

It also felt indecently tidy compared with the fact itself.

A young man who had been arguing about cars on the wing days earlier was dead.

Mercer looked through the office glass toward C Wing.

Darren was waiting for news without knowing she already had it.

Connor too.

Macca.

Jamo.

All the people who had absorbed Josh into ordinary prison life.

The institution now had to tell them the ordinary had ended.

---

## DARREN

Darren knew before anyone said the words.

Officer Mercer came toward him with Senior Officer Patel.

Two staff.

Serious faces.

Nobody brought two people to tell you good news.

"Darren," Mercer said.

He stood.

His legs felt wrong.

"Josh died at the hospital."

That was all he heard properly.

The rest became fragments.

Family informed.

Support available.

Healthcare if needed.

Darren stared at Mercer.

"No."

Not an argument.

A reflex.

Mercer did not correct him.

Darren looked past her at the wing.

Josh should have been somewhere in it.

Complaining.

Moving.

Talking.

He had never understood how much space noise occupied until Josh's noise disappeared.

"Can I go cell?" Darren asked.

Mercer nodded.

He walked away without looking at anyone.

---

## CONNOR

Connor heard the truth from Darren's face before Malik whispered it.

"Josh died."

Connor looked at him.

Malik did not add a version.

No rumour.

No cause beyond what staff had confirmed at a high level.

For once information arrived and stayed still.

Connor sat down.

He pictured Josh holding a magazine upside down while arguing about engines.

Josh shouting adaptation.

Josh stealing Darren's biscuits.

Josh asking whether prison Christmas dinner was research.

Josh saying he would buy an M3 with future money.

The future money line hurt most.

Because it had contained a future.

Connor looked toward Darren's cell.

"He on his own?"

Malik nodded.

"For now."

Connor stood.

Then stopped.

Old Connor would have decided immediately what Darren needed and gone in.

Prison had taught him people sometimes needed a private minute more than they needed you proving you cared.

He waited.

Ten minutes later he knocked.

"Daz?"

No answer.

Connor sat outside the cell rather than entering.

After a while Darren said from inside:

"He was nineteen."

Connor closed his eyes.

"Yeah."

"He was meant to go home."

"Yeah."

Nothing else was useful.

---

## MACCA

Macca thought about Josh's mum.

He did not know her.

Had seen her across visits once perhaps.

A woman waving at a boy who talked too fast.

Now some official person had made a phone call or stood at a door and changed her life.

Macca looked at his daughter's drawing beside his bed.

**DAD WHEN UR HOME.**

He had spent months complaining about missed calls, school plays, birthdays and all the pieces prison took.

Today every one of those complaints felt like a privilege.

His daughter was outside living badly timed life.

Josh's family had lost the chance to complain about time.

Macca called home later just to hear his children.

He did not tell them why.

---

## BRUCE

Bruce said almost nothing all morning.

That frightened people more than any joke would have.

At lunch Connor found him folding a small piece of paper.

"What you doing?"

Bruce held it up.

A crude car.

Four wheels, one enormous spoiler.

"M3," Bruce said.

Connor stared.

"Looks shit."

"He'd have argued."

Connor smiled and then stopped smiling.

Bruce put the paper car near a place on the landing where people had started leaving small notes until staff told them what was allowed.

He walked away without a joke.

That was how Connor knew Bruce had liked Josh more than Bruce had ever said.

---

## DARREN — NIGHT

Darren did not become sober because Josh died.

He did not become wise.

He did not wake with all cravings removed by grief.

The opposite, if anything.

Pain made every old escape route louder.

That was the cruelty.

The consequence that should have made Darren want to feel everything also made him want to feel nothing.

He sat on the bunk holding the picture Josh's sister had drawn of the car.

Josh had left it with him weeks earlier because Darren "had more wall space."

A rectangle with circles.

Darren had called it excellent.

He pressed the paper flat with both hands.

"I'm sorry," he said.

Josh was not there to tell him to stop being dramatic.


# TUESDAY 4 FEBRUARY 2020 — WHAT DARREN CARRIES

## OFFICER GRANT

Grant waited three days before calling Darren in.

In his mind, that was restraint.

He knew what prisoners said about him.

Hard.

Punishment first.

Uses people.

Some of it was unfair.

Some of it was not.

Grant believed information prevented harm.

Josh's death made that belief stronger, not weaker.

People inside knew what was changing before staff did.

They knew who was vulnerable, who was being pressured, which atmosphere had shifted.

Grant saw silence as part of the problem.

Darren saw Grant as part of a different one.

They sat opposite each other.

Grant began carefully.

"How are you?"

Darren laughed.

"Don't."

Grant's jaw tightened.

"I'm asking."

"You don't care."

"You don't know that."

Darren looked at him.

For once Grant seemed offended rather than powerful.

That made him more human and somehow harder to hate cleanly.

"What do you want?" Darren asked.

Grant paused.

"I want to know if there are other young prisoners at risk."

There it was.

A question Grant could defend morally.

Maybe even a good question.

Darren felt the old machinery start.

If he said one name, one group, one worry, what would happen next?

Who would get searched?

Who would get labelled?

Who would know Darren had been in the office?

Josh's death did not make Grant's methods simpler.

"I'm not doing this today," Darren said.

Grant leaned back.

"Today?"

"Don't twist it. I'm not giving you names."

"Even if somebody else could end up dead?"

The sentence hit hard enough that Darren stood.

"Don't use him."

Grant remained seated.

"I'm not using anyone."

"You are. You're using Josh to make me talk."

Grant's face changed.

Perhaps because Darren was right.

Perhaps because Grant believed the distinction mattered.

"Sit down."

"No."

Darren moved toward the door.

Grant said:

"If you believe somebody is in immediate danger, you tell staff. That's not informing."

Darren stopped.

That sentence was fair.

He hated it for being fair.

"If I know that," Darren said, "I will."

He looked back.

"But I'm not giving you people so you can decide what my words mean afterwards."

Then he left.

It was not recovery.

It was not courage in a clean form.

It was one line Darren had finally drawn.

---

## MALIK

Malik knew Darren had been called in again.

He also knew enough now to construct a devastating version of Darren's whole story.

Meetings with Grant.

A staff intervention after Darren knew something.

Rumours.

Josh.

Darren's fear.

Pieces.

Malik could arrange them into certainty and make the wing believe it before dinner.

He did not.

Yusuf found him staring over the rail.

"Thinking face."

"Yeah."

"Dangerous?"

"Could be."

Yusuf waited.

Malik smiled faintly.

"Not telling you."

Yusuf nodded.

"Look at you. Growth."

"Fuck off."

They laughed.

Malik thought of Josh.

Information could be responsibility.

Sometimes the most powerful thing Malik knew was where a story should stop.

---

## OFFICER BENNETT

The pressure around Bennett changed after Mercer spoke to Craig.

It did not end with an explanation.

That would have been easier.

A message came telling him to forget the outstanding demand.

Then silence.

No apology.

No threat.

No reason.

Bennett read the message four times.

His relief was so intense it made him sick with shame.

Because he knew Mercer had done something.

And he knew, without knowing how, Craig was involved.

The prison had solved a problem created by prisoner-linked pressure through the influence of another prisoner.

Bennett sat with Senior Officer Patel later and finally gave a fuller account of what had happened.

Not every consequence was fixed by the pressure receding.

There would be investigation.

Professional consequences.

Questions.

But for the first time in months Bennett told the truth faster than he deleted it.

That mattered.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Bennett told her the new messages had stopped.

Mercer felt relief.

Then fear.

Craig had done something without telling her what.

No visible retaliation.

No new incident she could connect.

Simply a change outside the prison.

That should have reassured her.

Instead it proved the thing everybody said about Craig that Mercer had resisted turning into mythology.

Reach.

She found him later.

"It's stopped."

Craig looked up.

"Good."

"What did you do?"

"Nothing you need to know."

Mercer's jaw tightened.

"That's not reassuring."

"No one got hurt."

He repeated her own condition back to her.

Mercer stared.

Craig's expression softened by a fraction.

"Bennett should tell the truth now."

"He is."

"Then maybe he gets a chance."

Mercer nodded.

She turned to leave.

"Leah."

She stopped.

He had done it again.

Quietly.

Only when nobody else was close.

Mercer looked back.

"I told you not to call me that."

"You did."

"Then why are you?"

Craig's face remained unreadable.

"Because you keep answering."

Mercer walked away before she gave him another one.


# FRIDAY 7 FEBRUARY 2020 — INSIDE PEOPLE

## JINX — A WING

The instruction from outside arrived disguised as certainty.

Somebody was talking.

Somebody inside needed to be taught that it had consequences.

No proof accompanied the confidence.

Jinx read the message twice and felt tired rather than frightened.

Months earlier he might have interpreted the demand as loyalty.

Now he saw the shape more clearly.

People outside naming a problem.

People inside expected to carry whatever followed.

Rico's failed move on Craig had started exactly there.

Different details.

Same arrogance.

Jinx found Deano during movement.

"They're saying someone's talking."

Deano looked at him.

"They always are."

"They've got a name."

"Proof?"

"No."

Deano shrugged.

"Then they've got a hobby."

Jinx smiled despite himself.

"That's my line."

"You can invoice me."

Jinx's face settled.

"They want something done about it."

Deano understood.

He did not ask what.

"And?"

Jinx looked across the prison.

Men he actually saw every day.

Men who would serve the consequences.

"No."

One word.

It felt larger than any order he had repeated.

---

## YUSUF

Yusuf was asked to carry the reply.

He laughed.

Jinx stared at him.

"What's funny?"

"Months of me telling everyone I'm not the postman and somehow we're back here."

Deano smiled.

Jinx did not.

"I need it passed."

Yusuf shook his head.

"Then pass it."

"You speak to—"

"No."

Yusuf's voice stayed friendly.

That was his talent.

"Your no means more if it comes from you."

Jinx looked annoyed.

Then thoughtful.

Yusuf continued:

"If you're choosing the people here, choose them with your own mouth."

Deano laughed.

"He's got you."

Jinx told both of them to fuck off.

Then made the call through appropriate ordinary channels himself when he could.

The answer outside was not happy.

Jinx survived the unhappiness.

That taught him something.

---

## MALIK

Malik heard later that Jinx had refused an outside demand.

He did not ask for details.

That surprised everyone including Malik.

He had begun treating knowledge like hot food.

Useful.

Sometimes necessary.

Still capable of burning the person holding it.

"You're getting boring," Bruce told him.

"Thank you."

"Wasn't a compliment."

"Still taking it."

Bruce looked disappointed.

"Personal growth ruins everyone eventually."

Malik laughed.

---

## DARREN

Nobody came for Darren.

Nobody accused him.

Nobody turned rumour into certainty.

He knew enough prison to understand that several people had probably made choices he would never hear about.

That frightened and humbled him.

He had spent months thinking everybody saw him only as Darren the wreck.

Maybe some people had also chosen not to destroy him when they could have tried.

Josh would have turned that into a speech about friendship.

Darren could almost hear it.

The imagined voice hurt.

He kept walking.


# MONDAY 10 FEBRUARY 2020 — NEUTRAL

## LEON — GYM

Leon lost his perfect routine because he finally chose a side.

Not a crew.

A side.

The side where a frightened young prisoner did not need three older men making his gym session into punishment for an argument elsewhere.

Leon saw the atmosphere before anything happened.

Three men watching Aaron.

Aaron pretending not to notice.

A dispute Leon had repeatedly said had nothing to do with gym.

"Leave it outside," Leon told them.

One man laughed.

"It is outside."

"You know what I mean."

"You're choosing him?"

There it was.

The question Leon had spent months avoiding.

If he said no, the pressure stayed.

If he did nothing, neutrality became permission.

Leon looked at Aaron.

Nineteen or twenty.

Trying hard not to look young.

Connor had looked similar in September.

Tyler too.

Josh.

The thought of Josh made Leon's answer easier.

"I'm choosing gym," Leon said. "And this ain't gym."

He stopped the session and called staff before it became anything more.

Men complained.

The session was cancelled.

Staff questioned why Leon had not simply kept everybody moving.

His orderly position was put under review for a few days while accounts were taken.

Leon went back to his cell furious.

Routine broken.

Job threatened.

Exactly the consequences he had spent months avoiding.

He still knew he would make the same choice again.

That was new.

---

## CONNOR

Connor found Leon after.

"Heard you shut gym."

Leon looked at him.

"Don't start."

"Was gonna say good on you."

Leon frowned.

"You?"

"What?"

"Rush complimenting someone for not fighting."

Connor laughed.

"Character development."

Leon shook his head.

"Cost me the job maybe."

"You regret it?"

Leon thought.

"No."

Connor nodded.

He understood the answer more than he would have months ago.

You could lose something and still have made the right choice.

Prison was full of costs.

Not all of them proved the decision was wrong.

---

## BRUCE

Bruce heard Leon's gym job was under review and said:

"Congratulations on discovering neutrality has paperwork."

Leon stared at him.

"Go away."

"Supportive visit complete."

Bruce left.

Leon laughed despite himself.


# THURSDAY 13 FEBRUARY 2020 — THE STAR

## MACCA — VISITS

Macca's daughter arrived wearing a coat too big for her and carrying the silver paper star from the school play.

Not the costume.

A piece of it.

A badly cut star with creases through the middle.

"They let you bring that?" Macca asked.

His partner smiled.

"Eventually."

His daughter held it up.

"This is from the play."

Macca took it carefully.

"I know."

"You heard me."

Months earlier he had lied automatically.

Course I did.

Today he looked at her.

"I didn't hear it from here."

Her face changed slightly.

Macca continued quickly:

"But Mum told me everything. You told me everything. And I heard you sing on Christmas Eve. So I know exactly how loud you are."

His daughter considered this.

"Very loud."

"Too loud."

"Mum says that."

His partner rolled her eyes.

Macca laughed.

The truth did not break the moment.

That surprised him.

His daughter pushed the star across the table.

"You keep it."

Macca swallowed.

"Yeah?"

"Till you're home."

He nodded.

"Deal."

---

## CONNOR — VISITS HALL

Connor saw Macca with his daughter from across the room and looked away before Macca caught him watching.

His own family visit was shorter and more complicated.

His mum came.

His girlfriend did not.

Work, apparently.

Connor heard the explanation and believed perhaps eighty percent of it.

That was not enough to start a fight from prison.

His mum watched him carefully.

"You're handling that better than I expected."

"Cheers for the confidence."

"You know what I mean."

Connor did.

His little brother had sent another drawing.

This one showed Connor behind a steering wheel with **NO POLICE** written above the car.

Connor laughed so loudly people looked over.

"What?" his mum asked.

He showed her.

She covered her face.

"That child."

Connor kept the drawing.

He had started collecting proof that home still existed rather than proof he could pay for it.

That felt healthier.

---

## MACCA — AFTER

Back on the wing Macca taped the silver star beside the drawings.

Bruce inspected it.

"Professionally superior to mine."

"She's seven."

"Strong competitor."

Macca smiled.

"You want yours back?"

Bruce looked offended.

"Gifts are legally binding."

"That's not true."

"Tell the courts."

Macca laughed.

His sentence had cost him the school hall.

It had not cost him the relationship.

Not yet.

He understood the distinction now and treated it like something fragile.
