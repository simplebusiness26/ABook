# SATURDAY 16 NOVEMBER 2019 — THE WRONG HOUR

## MACCA — C WING

Macca knew the exact time his daughter was supposed to ring.

Six thirty.

Not around six thirty.

Not after tea.

Six thirty.

She had been practising how she was going to describe the school play costume to him and had made her mum promise she could call first.

At six twenty-two the wing locked down.

No warning.

No explanation worth having.

An argument elsewhere had turned into a staff response, then movement stopped, doors closed and the prison changed shape around a problem Macca had nothing to do with.

He stood at his cell door.

"Boss."

The officer kept moving.

"Boss, phone call. Six thirty. My little girl."

"Not now, Macca."

"It'll take two minutes."

"Not now."

The door shut fully.

Macca stared at it.

He checked the time.

Six twenty-six.

Four minutes later he imagined the phone ringing at home.

His daughter waiting.

Then asking why Dad had not called.

Then somebody explaining prison to a child again.

Macca sat on the bunk.

The wing was suddenly full of men angry about different things caused by the same locked door.

That was prison.

One incident.

Thirty separate losses.

---

## LEON

Leon lost gym.

To everybody else that sounded minor.

To Leon it was the middle of his day being removed without appeal.

He had built prison into blocks he could control.

Train.

Eat.

Read.

Sleep.

Take one block away and the others seemed less solid.

He sat on his bunk with a book open and read the same paragraph three times.

Someone shouted from another cell:

"Leon! Gym tomorrow?"

"If regime runs."

"You reckon it will?"

Leon closed his eyes.

"How would I know?"

"You're gym orderly."

As if that made him governor.

Leon looked at the page again.

He could feel irritation trying to become something larger.

That was exactly why he liked routine.

It gave anger somewhere to go.

Tonight it had nowhere.

---

## JAMO

Jamo had education work in his cell and no intention of doing it.

He was annoyed at the lockdown.

Then annoyed that he was annoyed, because six months inside should have made him better at this.

Then annoyed at Connor for existing three cells away even though Connor had nothing to do with it.

That part he recognised as unfair.

He kept it anyway.

His new cellmate was asleep.

Of course he was.

The man could sleep through structural collapse.

Jamo sat at the small table and heard Connor laughing somewhere down the landing.

Then Stuart saying something too low to make out.

Jamo found himself smiling.

Stopped.

"Pathetic," he muttered.

His cellmate snored in response.

Jamo looked at him.

"You agree with everything."

Another snore.

"Good talk."

---

## DARREN AND JOSH

Josh treated lockdown like an audience problem.

He had too much energy and nowhere to put it.

Within ten minutes he had shouted three questions through the door, started a debate about the best first car and somehow involved Bruce from another section of the wing.

"You cannot have an M3 as a first car," Darren told him.

"Why?"

"Because your insurance company would send you a sympathy card instead of a quote."

"Negative mindset."

"Financial mindset."

Bruce's voice carried from farther down.

"Get a Volvo."

Josh shouted back:

"I'm nineteen, not a geography teacher!"

"Volvos are dependable."

"That's not the same as exciting."

Darren laughed.

For a few minutes the closed door did not feel as close.

Then the laughter stopped and Darren felt the other thing underneath.

The restlessness.

The old urge to make the next hour disappear somehow.

Lockdowns were difficult for Darren because time became thick.

Josh did not know that.

He just kept talking.

Normally Darren would have told him to shut up.

Tonight he let him.

Josh moved from cars to food, from food to court, from court to a girl he had been seeing outside, then back to cars without any visible transition.

Darren listened.

The bad hour rose.

Then eased a little.

Not because Josh fixed anything.

Because sometimes another person's noise could hold the room open long enough for a difficult hour to pass.

"Daz?"

"What?"

"You listening?"

"Unfortunately."

Josh laughed.

Darren smiled in the dark.

---

## MALIK

Malik heard four explanations for the lockdown before any official explanation existed.

Fight.

Search.

Threat.

Someone had refused an order.

All four came from men who claimed certainty.

Malik sat back and did something new.

Nothing.

He did not repeat any of them.

Yusuf shouted from another cell:

"You gone quiet?"

"Thinking."

"Dangerous."

Malik smiled.

He thought about the last week.

Darren being called into Grant's office.

The staff interest around Jinx's wider circle.

People already whispering that somebody was talking.

One wrong sentence now could turn suspicion into a name.

A name could turn into a problem.

And Malik, for once, did not want to be the reason it moved.

Hear all.

See all.

Say nothing.

Mason's line had started as advice to Connor.

Malik was beginning to suspect it had been aimed at everybody.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer hated lockdowns for the same reason prisoners did, although she would never have said it that way.

Everything became delayed.

Meals.

Calls.

Movement.

Paperwork multiplied while patience disappeared.

The original incident had been contained quickly.

The consequences spread anyway.

Macca called to her when she passed.

"Miss. I was meant to ring my daughter."

Mercer stopped.

"I know."

"Can I get two minutes when this lifts?"

She looked at the time.

"If movement resumes before lock-up, I'll see what I can do."

Macca nodded.

"Please."

That word from him carried more weight than shouting would have.

Mercer moved on.

Bennett was farther down, dealing with another door.

He looked distracted again.

Craig, behind his own cell door, was nowhere near the centre of tonight's problem.

That interested Mercer almost as much as when he was.

The prison had become used to making every disturbance feel as if it belonged to his story.

Tonight proved it did not.

---

## CONNOR AND STUART

Connor sat on the floor with his back against the bunk.

Stuart was reading.

"How can you read when everyone's shouting?" Connor asked.

"I grew up with family."

"So did I."

"Then why are you surprised?"

Connor laughed.

Someone outside shouted about phone access.

Someone else shouted back that nobody cared.

Stuart closed the book.

"You notice everyone is angry about something different?"

Connor looked at him.

"Yeah."

"Same door. Different prison."

Connor frowned.

"That's annoyingly good."

"Thank you."

Connor thought about Macca waiting for his daughter.

Leon missing gym.

Jamo probably furious somewhere.

Darren, who hated being behind doors for too long.

And Connor himself.

Connor's biggest problem tonight was boredom.

That felt embarrassing once he compared it.

"You ever think prison teaches empathy by accident?" he asked.

Stuart smiled.

"Maybe God uses bad teachers."

Connor groaned.

"There it is."

"You asked."

"I didn't ask for church."

"You live with church now."

Connor laughed.

The door stayed shut.

For once, he did not mind the company.

---

## MACCA — LATER

Movement resumed at seven twelve.

Too late for the original call.

Mercer found Macca near the phones.

"Try now."

He did.

His partner answered.

"She's asleep."

Macca closed his eyes.

"Already?"

"She waited. Got upset. Then crashed."

Macca stared at the wall.

"Tell her I'm sorry."

"She knows."

"Does she?"

His partner did not answer immediately.

Macca swallowed.

"Tell her I wanted to hear about the costume."

"I will."

"Tell her I know she's a star."

"I will."

He put the phone down gently.

Connor was nearby, waiting for his turn.

Macca looked at him.

"Don't ever have kids in jail."

Connor's smile disappeared.

"Wasn't planning to."

"Good."

Macca walked away.

Connor watched him go.

An hour ago the lockdown had been annoying.

Now it had a child's face attached to it.
