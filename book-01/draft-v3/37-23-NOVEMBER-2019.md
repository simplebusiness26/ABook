# SATURDAY 23 NOVEMBER 2019 — SIDES

## YUSUF — C WING

Yusuf had spent most of his sentence becoming the person people could talk to without thinking too hard about which group he belonged to.

He liked that.

He liked people generally.

Prison was easier when every conversation did not need a flag attached to it.

That worked until two people wanted him to agree with them at the same time.

Jinx found him first.

"You were there Thursday, yeah?"

Yusuf knew immediately which conversation he meant.

A stupid argument near movement.

Deano had said somebody was stirring suspicion around Rico's old circle.

Jinx had taken it as an accusation against one of his lads.

Voices had risen.

Nothing serious happened.

Which meant prison had spent two days trying to turn it into something serious after the fact.

"I was there," Yusuf said.

"So you heard what Deano said."

"I heard everyone talking over everyone."

Jinx frowned.

"He said my lot were spreading the Darren thing."

Yusuf shrugged.

"Maybe."

"Maybe?"

"You want subtitles?"

Jinx almost smiled.

Then the point returned.

"If he asks, tell him I never said his people were behind it."

Yusuf looked at him.

"Why don't you tell him?"

"I have."

"Then why do I need to?"

Jinx stared.

Yusuf smiled.

"Exactly."

Jinx shook his head.

"You're annoying when you're neutral."

"I'm annoying full-time. Neutrality is optional."

Jinx walked off.

Ten minutes later Deano appeared.

Of course he did.

"You were there Thursday, yeah?"

Yusuf closed his eyes.

"No."

Deano stared.

"You were standing next to me."

"Then why are you asking?"

Deano laughed.

"Jinx been in your ear?"

"And there it is."

"What?"

"You lot keep asking me what the other one said. Speak to each other."

Deano's smile faded slightly.

"Easy for you."

"Why?"

"Because everybody likes you."

Yusuf looked at him.

That was supposed to be a compliment.

It sounded like a job description.

"That's not an agreement to work for everybody," Yusuf said.

Deano nodded slowly.

"Fair."

Then he left.

Yusuf watched him go.

Being everyone's mate had felt like freedom.

Today it felt more like everyone believing they owned a little part of him.

## BRUCE

Bruce found Yusuf sitting alone.

"Diplomatic crisis?"

Yusuf looked up.

"How do you know?"

Bruce shrugged.

"Jinx walked away annoyed. Deano walked away annoyed. You're sitting here like Switzerland with a headache."

Yusuf laughed.

"I'm not taking sides."

"Everyone says that until chairs are allocated."

"What does that mean?"

"No idea yet."

Bruce sat beside him.

For once he did not immediately say something stupid.

Yusuf looked over.

"You ever get dragged into people's stuff because you know everyone?"

Bruce considered it.

"Constantly."

"What do you do?"

Bruce opened a packet of biscuits.

"Become unreliable."

Yusuf stared.

Bruce smiled.

"Not dishonest. Unreliable for politics. If people learn you won't carry their messages, eventually they stop giving you messages."

Yusuf thought about it.

That was annoyingly sensible.

Bruce ruined it by adding:

"Same principle as lending money to someone who lives in another country."

"That's not the same principle."

"Close enough."

Yusuf laughed.

Bruce handed him a biscuit.

"Don't let being liked turn into unpaid employment."

Then he stood and left.

Yusuf stared after him.

There were days Bruce should have been studied professionally.

There were other days he should probably have been teaching classes.

Nobody would survive the timetable.

## LEON

Leon had a gym list problem.

Two men believed they should be on the same session.

One blamed Leon.

The other blamed staff.

Leon blamed the list.

"I don't make allocations," he said for the fourth time.

"You're gym orderly."

"That means I work there. It does not mean I secretly control the prison."

The man looked unconvinced.

Leon pointed at the paper.

"Your name isn't there. Put an app in."

"Apps disappear."

"Then put another one in."

"Easy for you to say."

Leon took a slow breath.

His whole philosophy was routine.

Do the work.

Avoid politics.

Keep life narrow enough to control.

But everybody kept bringing politics to the narrow places too.

Gym list.

Servery.

Cell move.

Who spoke to who.

Who stood beside who.

Neutrality was not a wall.

It was a position other people could still point at.

Connor passed nearby.

"You look happy."

Leon stared at him.

"I am living my dream."

Connor looked at the list.

"Gym politics?"

"Apparently I am now minister for exercise."

Connor laughed.

Leon did too.

Then the man returned with another question.

Leon closed his eyes.

Routine had filed an appeal.

## CONNOR

Connor was beginning to see the wing differently.

When he arrived, everyone had looked established.

Jamo knew everything.

Leon was always calm.

Malik always knew the answer.

Yusuf got on with everyone.

Mason understood prison.

Craig understood everything else.

Now Connor could see the joins.

Jamo missed people.

Leon got irritated.

Malik doubted himself.

Yusuf could be pulled by other people's expectations.

Mason sometimes looked tired in a way experience could not fix.

Craig remained harder to read, but even that no longer meant invincible.

It meant hidden.

Stuart came alongside him.

"You are staring again."

Connor smiled.

"Observing."

"Ah. Professional now?"

"Mason said hear all, see all."

"And say nothing."

Connor looked at him.

"You've heard that?"

"Everyone has heard that."

Connor laughed.

"Course they have."

Across the wing Yusuf was talking to Bruce.

"He's pissed off," Connor said.

"Yusuf?"

"Yeah. People want him taking sides."

Stuart nodded.

"Being useful to everybody can become another kind of trap."

Connor looked at him.

Kez flashed into his mind.

Then Tyler.

Then Darren being called by Grant.

Different stories.

Same shape.

"Prison's obsessed with making people useful," Connor said.

Stuart looked at him.

"People are. Prison just removes places to hide from it."

Connor smiled.

"You got a depressing answer for everything?"

"Not everything."

"Go on then. Say something cheerful."

Stuart thought.

"Dinner is soon."

Connor stared.

"That's your cheerful thing?"

"I did not say I was good at it."

Connor laughed.

---

## JINX AND DEANO

They found each other before evening association.

Not arranged.

Neither admitted looking.

Jinx spoke first.

"Stop asking Yusuf what I said."

Deano frowned.

"You asked him first."

Jinx paused.

"That's irrelevant."

Deano laughed.

"No, it's actually very relevant."

Jinx smiled despite himself.

The tension eased a fraction.

"Look," Jinx said. "People are getting twitchy about this Darren thing."

"I know."

"I don't want names getting thrown around because someone saw someone go in an office."

Deano nodded.

"Same."

Jinx looked at him.

"Then say that to your lot."

"You say it to yours."

"I am."

"Good."

A pause.

They had just agreed.

Both seemed slightly disappointed.

Jinx shook his head.

"This is getting weird."

"What?"

"Us being sensible."

Deano smiled.

"Don't tell anyone."

Jinx laughed.

For all the noise about sides, the thing protecting both groups that evening was two men deciding not to create one.

## YUSUF — NIGHT

Yusuf lay on his bunk thinking about the phrase *everybody likes you*.

Outside prison it had always sounded good.

Inside, it sometimes came with invoices.

Tell him this.

Back me on that.

Say you heard it.

Explain what he meant.

Confirm I'm right.

He decided Bruce was right about one thing.

People needed to learn he was unreliable for politics.

He smiled in the dark.

Tomorrow he would go back to being friendly.

Just slightly less employable.
