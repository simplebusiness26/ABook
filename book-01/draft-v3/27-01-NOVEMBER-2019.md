# FRIDAY 1 NOVEMBER 2019 — THE PERSON YOU SAID YOU WERE

## TYLER — C WING

Tyler had spent most of Thursday night thinking about a cousin who did not exist in quite the way Tyler had described him.

The cousin existed.

That was the annoying part.

If Tyler had invented the whole person, he could at least have blamed imagination.

Instead there really was an older cousin called Lee who knew people, had been around trouble and had once been enough of a name on Tyler's estate that younger lads mentioned him when they wanted an argument to become somebody else's problem.

Everything after that had been Tyler's editing.

Lee knew everyone.

Lee could sort anything.

Lee had people everywhere.

Lee only needed one phone call.

By the time Tyler came to Lewes, his cousin had become less of a relative and more of a fictional emergency service.

Now somebody wanted to use it.

The prisoner from the night before found him just after breakfast.

"You spoke to your cousin?"

Tyler's mouth answered before his brain had finished waking up.

"Yeah."

The man nodded.

"Sweet. He can help then?"

Tyler felt the lie grow another floor.

"Should be able to."

"Good. I'll catch you later."

He walked off.

Tyler watched him go.

Josh appeared beside him carrying a mug.

"You look like you've swallowed a battery."

Tyler frowned.

"What?"

"All tense. Like this."

Josh demonstrated by pulling his shoulders almost to his ears.

"I don't look like that."

"You do a bit."

"Why are you looking at me anyway?"

Josh shrugged.

"Waiting for Darren. Got bored."

Tyler looked around.

"Where is he?"

"No idea."

"Thought you followed him everywhere."

"I do. He's escaped."

Tyler laughed despite himself.

Josh smiled.

Then his attention jumped somewhere else entirely.

"You reckon that's a BMW engine on that magazine?"

Tyler stared at him.

"How would I know from here?"

"I can tell."

"You can't."

"Can."

Josh was already walking towards it.

Tyler watched him go and envied him for having a brain willing to abandon one problem because a picture of a car had appeared.

Tyler's brain had chosen the cousin.

It was staying there.

---

## JOSH

The magazine did not contain a BMW engine.

Josh regarded this as a personal betrayal.

"That's a Mercedes."

Darren had finally reappeared and was now being forced to participate.

"Looks like an engine."

Josh stared at him.

"That's disgusting."

"What is?"

"You can't just call all engines engines."

Darren looked at Connor, who was passing with the cleaning trolley.

"Help me."

Connor stopped.

Josh held up the magazine.

"BMW or Mercedes?"

Connor barely looked.

"Mercedes."

Josh pointed at Darren.

"See?"

Darren frowned at Connor.

"Why do you know that?"

"Because I've got eyes."

"Apparently mine are classist."

Josh laughed.

Cars could occupy him indefinitely.

Outside, he had talked about learning to drive properly, getting something stupidly fast one day, fixing cars, buying and selling them, working in a garage, maybe doing none of those things and becoming rich somehow instead.

The plans changed depending on what he had seen most recently.

The certainty never did.

"When I get out," Josh said, "I'm getting an M3."

Connor looked at him.

"With what money?"

"Future money."

Darren nodded solemnly.

"Strong currency."

"Exactly."

"What's the exchange rate?"

"Shut up."

They laughed.

For a few minutes, the wing contained three lads talking about cars instead of cases, court dates, debts or prison.

That mattered.

---

## CONNOR

Connor noticed Tyler before Tyler came to him.

Tyler kept looking over.

Then looking away.

Then looking over again.

By the fourth time Connor put the mop down.

"What?"

Tyler walked closer.

"Nothing."

"You've been staring at me like I owe you money."

"I weren't staring."

"You were doing it badly then."

Tyler looked around.

"You know when someone asks you to sort something?"

Connor laughed once.

"That sentence has already gone wrong."

"No, listen."

"I'm listening."

Tyler lowered his voice.

"Like if you said you knew someone outside and then someone wanted you to get them to do something."

Connor looked at him properly.

The Kez visit came back faster than he wanted.

Not the visit itself.

The feeling afterwards.

Being twenty-one years old and furious because an older man had understood Connor's ego better than Connor did.

"Do you actually know them?" Connor asked.

Tyler hesitated.

There was the answer.

Connor nodded.

"Stop adding to it."

"What?"

"Whatever you've said. Stop making it bigger."

Tyler looked defensive.

"I ain't lied."

Connor smiled without humour.

"Didn't say you did."

"My cousin does know people."

"All right."

"He does."

"Tyler."

"What?"

Connor picked the mop back up.

"You don't have to convince me. That's probably the point."

Tyler stood there another second.

Then walked off.

Connor watched him go.

He had been in prison long enough now to recognise somebody else making a mistake he had only just finished making himself.

That was an unpleasant form of experience.

---

## JAMO

Jamo had heard the conversation from the cell doorway.

Not every word.

Enough.

Connor came back later and found him folding a T-shirt.

"What's up with Tyler?" Jamo asked.

"Talking himself into trouble."

"And you're Yoda now?"

Connor looked at him.

"What?"

Jamo smiled.

"Nothing."

Connor's face changed.

"No, go on."

"You've been here five weeks and you're giving prison advice."

"I gave him advice about being a dickhead. I've got years of experience."

Normally Jamo would have laughed.

He did not.

Connor noticed.

"What's your problem?"

"Don't have one."

"You've always got one lately."

Jamo put the shirt down.

"Maybe I'm tired."

"Of what?"

Jamo looked around the cell.

Connor knew immediately the room was part of the answer.

"Forget it," Jamo said.

Connor exhaled sharply.

"You keep saying forget it and then acting pissed off."

"Then forget that too."

Connor stared at him.

Jamo went back to folding.

The argument did not happen.

That was worse in some ways.

It waited.

---

## OFFICER MERCER

Officer Mercer saw Connor talking to Tyler and found the sight oddly revealing.

Five weeks earlier Blake had arrived looking to everybody else for instructions.

Now newer prisoners were looking at him.

That happened quickly in prison.

Experience was relative.

A man who knew nothing in September could look established to a man arriving in November.

Mercer watched Tyler walk away and Connor return to his cleaning.

"Blake."

Connor looked over.

"Miss."

"You running induction now?"

Connor smiled.

"Better pay?"

"No."

"Then no."

Mercer nodded at Tyler.

"Everything all right?"

Connor gave the automatic answer.

"Yeah."

Mercer waited.

Connor sighed.

"He's just new."

"So were you."

"I'm basically staff now."

Mercer looked at the mop.

"Absolutely. Management material."

Connor laughed.

She moved on.

Behind the joke was something she did not like.

Blake was settling.

That should have been positive.

Instead Mercer found herself wondering what exactly he was settling into.

---

## TYLER — PHONE

Tyler rang home that evening.

His mum answered on the third ring.

"Ty?"

The name did what prison could not.

It made him nineteen again immediately.

"Yeah."

"You all right?"

"Yeah. Easy."

"Eating?"

"Mum."

"What?"

"I'm not six."

"Didn't say you were. You never ate properly at nineteen either."

Tyler smiled despite himself.

"I'm eating."

"Clean clothes?"

"Yeah."

"Brushing your teeth?"

Tyler looked around quickly to make sure nobody close enough had heard.

"Mum, for fuck's sake."

"Language."

"I'm in prison."

"And? Teeth still exist in prison."

Tyler laughed.

For a second the whole performance slipped.

His mum heard it.

"You sure you're all right?"

Tyler looked down.

There was an answer available.

No.

He could say it.

He nearly did.

Then somebody walked behind him and Tyler's shoulders changed.

"Yeah," he said. "Told you. I'm sweet."

His mum was quiet for half a second.

"All right, Ty."

She did not believe him.

That was one of the difficult things about mothers.

They remembered the person underneath whatever name you were trying to build.
