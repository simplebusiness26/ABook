# SATURDAY 30 NOVEMBER 2019 — BEFORE CHRISTMAS

## CONNOR — C WING

Connor realised Christmas was coming because prison started talking about it before he wanted to.

Visits.

Canteen.

Cards.

Who was getting what sent in.

Who had kids.

Who hated Christmas.

Who claimed not to care.

Who had already started counting how many sleeps until the day itself despite being grown men in prison.

Connor had not thought about Christmas properly since arrest.

September had been police.

October had been becoming Rush.

November had somehow disappeared into Jamo moving out, Stuart moving in, Kez, Craig, jobs, Tyler, Josh, Darren, officers, rumours and the strange normality of a life Connor had once imagined would never become normal.

Now December was waiting.

He was cleaning near the rail when Bruce appeared.

"Christmas strategy?"

Connor looked at him.

"What?"

"Everyone needs one."

"For Christmas?"

"Correct."

"Eat dinner. Ring home. Try not to be miserable."

Bruce nodded seriously.

"Weak but functional."

"What's yours?"

"Become emotionally unavailable by the nineteenth."

Connor laughed.

"You've been practising all year."

"Preparation is character."

Bruce walked off.

Connor shook his head.

Christmas strategy.

Ridiculous.

Then he thought about his mum.

His little brother.

His girlfriend.

The visit room.

His mum whispering *come home the same person*.

The phrase returned more often now.

Connor was not sure she would recognise all the new bits.

## CONNOR — PHONE

His mum answered first.

That still changed his body immediately.

Shoulders down.

Voice softer.

"All right, Mum?"

"You all right?"

"Yeah."

"Actually?"

Connor smiled.

"Actually."

That answer was closer to true than it had been in September.

Which was strange.

He was in prison.

He had a serious case.

He could not go home.

Yet he was less frightened than he had been two months ago.

That should have felt like improvement.

Some days it did.

Other days it felt like evidence he was becoming too good at being somewhere he should hate.

His mum told him his little brother had started a list for Christmas.

"How long?" Connor asked.

"Ridiculous."

"What's on it?"

"Everything."

"Helpful."

She laughed.

Then his little brother came on.

"Connor?"

"Yeah, mate."

The list was explained in detail.

Connor listened.

Normally he would have been thinking about money while the boy spoke.

What he could buy.

What he could sort.

Who he could ask.

That had been one of Connor's favourite ways of loving people.

Produce something.

Fix something.

Pay for something.

Now all he had was a phone call.

So he listened properly.

When his little brother finally finished, Connor said:

"Send me the list."

"How?"

Connor smiled.

"Give it Mum. She'll sort it."

His mum came back on.

"Don't start promising things."

Connor looked at the wall.

"Wasn't."

"Connor."

He laughed.

She knew him too well.

"I'll see what I can do."

"You being there matters more."

The sentence hit harder because he could not be.

Connor swallowed.

"I know."

That was new too.

Outside, he would have argued.

Money matters.

Providing matters.

He would have listed all the things money solved.

Inside, he had no argument available.

His mum was right and prison had made him learn it in the least convenient way possible.

## GIRLFRIEND

His girlfriend came on after his mum.

Her voice was normal.

Too normal, Connor thought.

That was unfair.

He knew it was unfair while thinking it.

"You good?" she asked.

"Yeah. You?"

"Yeah. Busy."

There had been a time when Connor would have demanded details immediately.

Busy doing what?

Who with?

Why had she not answered the other night?

Prison gave insecurity too much time.

Stuart had told him once that fear loved empty spaces because it could decorate them however it wanted.

Connor had taken the piss for ten minutes.

Then remembered the sentence.

"Work?" Connor asked.

"Mostly. Family stuff."

"Fair."

A pause.

She seemed surprised he had not pushed.

Connor noticed that too.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You've gone quiet."

"Just... you're calmer."

Connor looked around the wing.

Someone shouted Rush from farther down.

He ignored it.

"Maybe prison's relaxing."

She laughed.

"Yeah, definitely that."

The call ended warmly enough.

Still, Connor stood with the receiver a second longer after she was gone.

Something outside had moved.

He could feel it.

He did not know what yet.

For once, he resisted inventing the answer.

## JAMO

Jamo had started spending time with Connor again without either of them officially deciding to.

That was the best version.

No conversation called *sorting things out*.

No apology speech.

No dramatic handshake.

They simply began standing beside each other more often.

Jamo appeared while Connor was putting the phone down.

"Home?"

"Yeah."

"All good?"

Connor nodded.

"Little brother's Christmas list has its own economy."

Jamo smiled.

"How old?"

Connor told him.

Jamo shook his head.

"Dangerous age. Wants everything."

"You got kids?"

"No."

"Good."

"Why good?"

Connor looked toward Macca.

Macca was reading a letter near his cell.

"Just is."

Jamo followed the look and understood.

They stood quietly for a moment.

Then Jamo said:

"My new cellmate still snores."

Connor laughed.

"Move back then."

Jamo looked at him.

Connor looked back.

The joke sat there.

Not an invitation.

Not completely not one either.

Jamo smiled.

"Stuart would baptise me in my sleep."

"He probably would."

"I'll stay where I am."

Connor nodded.

That was fine.

Their friendship did not need the old cell to prove it existed.

## STUART

Stuart had received a letter from university.

Not good news.

Not bad news.

Administrative news.

His place remained complicated while the case continued.

There were processes.

Deferrals.

Possibilities.

Words designed to avoid saying whether the life Stuart had been building would still be waiting for him if he walked out.

Connor found him reading it for the third time.

"Everything all right?"

Stuart looked up.

"I don't know."

Connor sat down.

"That's prison's favourite answer."

Stuart smiled faintly.

He handed Connor the letter.

Connor read enough.

"They ain't kicked you out."

"No."

"So that's good."

"Maybe."

"You're definitely innocent though, yeah?"

Stuart looked at him.

Connor raised his hands.

"I know, I know. Court decides."

Stuart smiled.

"I believe I acted in self-defence."

"Then when you get out, go back. Finish university."

Stuart looked at the letter again.

"You say that as if prison pauses the world."

Connor went quiet.

He had said exactly that to himself in police custody.

Girlfriend would wait.

Family would wait.

Everything would still be there.

He knew better now.

"Fair," Connor said.

Stuart folded the letter carefully.

"That is why faith matters to me. Not because I think everything will return to exactly how it was."

Connor looked at him.

"Here we go."

Stuart laughed.

"You started it."

"I asked if you were all right."

"Dangerous question."

Connor smiled.

Stuart's uncertainty was different from Connor's.

Connor expected punishment because, underneath all the arguments and legal language, he knew he had chosen a life that had led him here.

Stuart believed prison might be taking months from him for something he would eventually be cleared of.

Same cell.

Different prison.

Connor understood that now without Stuart explaining it.

## DARREN AND JOSH

Josh had decided Christmas dinner in prison was "research".

Research for what remained unclear.

"I've heard it's either decent or terrible," he told Darren.

"Strong research."

"People disagree."

"People disagree about water."

"No they don't."

Bruce, walking past, said:

"Depends where it's from."

Josh pointed after him.

"See?"

Darren laughed.

Josh had started talking about home more as December got closer.

His mum.

A younger relative.

Mates.

Cars he would supposedly own.

A job he might get.

Plans came out of him constantly.

Darren encouraged them because he liked the version of Josh that assumed life was waiting.

"First Christmas in jail," Josh said.

"Hopefully last."

"Obviously."

Darren looked at him.

The confidence was so complete it hurt.

Josh still treated prison as an interruption.

Darren had stopped believing that about himself a long time ago.

"What?" Josh asked.

"Nothing."

"Face."

Darren laughed.

"Everyone's obsessed with faces now."

"You taught me."

Again.

That sentence.

Darren smiled.

He had taught Josh good things too.

Routine.

Who was safe to joke with.

When to leave someone alone.

How to make a bad day smaller.

He needed to remember that.

Because the darker part of Darren's life had started frightening him enough that he could no longer pretend proximity had no consequences.

## OFFICER GRANT

Grant did not call Darren in that Saturday.

Darren noticed more than he would have noticed being called.

Grant passed him twice.

Said nothing.

Darren spent the first half of the day waiting for the office door.

By afternoon he realised the absence was its own pressure.

That irritated him.

Grant had reached a point where he could unsettle Darren without speaking.

Darren watched him pass again.

Josh was beside Darren talking about Christmas canteen.

Grant's eyes moved between them and away.

Nothing happened.

Darren still felt his stomach tighten.

He hated how much power could exist inside nothing.

## MALIK

The Darren rumour had not disappeared.

It had simply failed to become official wing truth.

Malik knew his silence had helped.

He also knew that felt dangerously good.

There was ego in restraint too if you were not careful.

*Look at me. I could have ruined something and chose not to.*

Mason would probably have found a way to make even that sound stupid.

Malik found him during movement.

"Hear all, see all, say nothing."

Mason looked over.

"You been reading my merchandise?"

"You should print T-shirts."

"Prison fashion's crowded."

Malik smiled.

Then said:

"What if saying nothing lets something bad happen?"

Mason stopped.

The joke left his face.

"Then say something."

Malik frowned.

"That's not very catchy."

"Didn't say it was religion."

Mason looked at him properly.

"The point isn't silence. The point is knowing the difference between information and noise. Knowing when your mouth helps and when it just makes you important for five minutes."

Malik stared.

"You should write that down."

"Too long for the T-shirt."

They both laughed.

Malik walked away thinking about it.

The title had never meant never speak.

It meant do not confuse being able to repeat something with needing to.

Connor was not the only one learning it.

## YUSUF

Yusuf's experiment in being politically unemployable was working.

Jinx asked fewer questions.

Deano stopped using him as a telephone.

For two days both had been mildly annoyed with him.

Then everybody got bored and went back to normal.

Yusuf considered this a victory.

Bruce considered it evidence for a theory he had named "strategic uselessness."

"That's not a real thing," Yusuf said.

"It is now."

"You're gonna ruin it."

"I improve terminology."

Yusuf laughed.

Across the wing Jinx and Deano were talking directly.

Imagine that.

Two adult men using their own mouths.

Yusuf felt proud of them.

He would never tell them.

---

## LEON

Leon had his routine back.

For now.

Gym ran.

Lists made sense.

No one asked him to take a side for almost forty-eight hours.

It felt luxurious.

He knew better than to trust it.

That was the evolution in Leon's prison.

At the start, routine had meant control.

Now he understood it was borrowed control.

The prison could take it back whenever it wanted.

He still trained.

Still read.

Still ate at roughly the same times.

The routine mattered more because it was fragile, not less.

---

## MACCA

The letter from his daughter contained a drawing of the school play costume.

Silver star.

Stick arms.

Huge smile.

At the bottom, in handwriting that leaned uphill, she had written:

**DAD I WILL DO IT LOUD SO YOU CAN HEAR**

Macca read the line three times.

There was no version of the school hall where he could hear her from Lewes.

That was not the point.

He folded the letter carefully.

Then unfolded it because folding hid the picture.

Connor passed.

Macca held it up.

"Star."

Connor smiled.

"Told you."

"She says she's doing it loud so I can hear."

Connor read the line.

His smile changed.

"Kids are mad."

"Yeah."

Macca looked at the drawing.

"Best people on earth."

Connor nodded.

He believed him.

## OFFICER MERCER

Mercer finished the month more worried about Officer Bennett than she had started it.

That was professionally simple.

Document concerns.

Observe.

Speak to Patel.

Do not invent facts.

Craig was more complicated.

Nothing had happened between them.

That was the phrase she kept using.

Nothing had happened.

Yet she noticed when he was at court.

Noticed when he returned.

Noticed what sort of mood Connor was in after speaking to him.

Noticed Craig noticing Bennett.

And, worse, had begun using Craig's observations as information in her own head.

That was not misconduct.

It was not even necessarily inappropriate.

It was simply a direction.

Mercer understood enough about prison to know directions mattered before destinations did.

Craig passed near the office late in the shift.

"Officer Mercer."

"Craig."

He stopped.

"December tomorrow."

Mercer looked at him.

"Excellent calendar work."

Craig almost smiled.

"Wing'll change."

"Christmas?"

"Families. Court dates. People counting things."

Mercer knew what he meant.

December made absence louder.

"You looking forward to it?" she asked before deciding whether she should.

Craig looked at her for a moment.

"No."

A simple answer.

The first personal one she felt he had given her without turning it into a joke.

Mercer nodded.

"Fair enough."

Then she walked away.

She did not know his first thought.

His family.

His case.

Big Sharky.

Something else entirely.

Craig remained hidden.

That was part of the danger.

Mercer had started wanting to know what was behind the answer.

## CONNOR — NIGHT

The door shut.

Stuart settled below with his book.

November ended in exactly the way most prison months ended.

Without ceremony.

Connor lay on the top bunk and listened.

Jamo shouting something from another cell.

Josh laughing.

Darren telling him to shut up without meaning it.

Bruce beginning an argument nobody had requested.

A toilet flushing through the wall.

A television.

Keys farther away.

Normal prison noise.

Connor knew the sounds now.

That fact would have terrified the version of him from September.

He thought about all the people who had become normal too.

Mason.

Malik.

Macca.

Leon.

Yusuf.

Jinx.

Deano.

Tyler.

Josh.

Darren.

Officer Mercer.

Officer Bennett.

Craig.

A building full of lives Connor would never have known if a tiny cut on his finger had not followed him into a recovered car.

"You awake?" Stuart asked.

"Yeah."

"Thinking?"

Connor smiled.

"Don't start."

Stuart laughed softly.

Connor looked at the ceiling.

October had taught him prison could give you a name.

November had taught him everyone else had a story happening at the same time.

Some had nothing to do with Craig.

Some had nothing to do with Connor.

Some collided anyway.

Tomorrow would be December.

Christmas.

Court.

Family.

More waiting.

More people changing while pretending they were not.

Connor closed his eyes.

For once, he was not wondering what prison was like.

He was beginning to understand there was no single answer.
