# MONDAY 18 NOVEMBER 2019 — NO PROOF

## MALIK

Malik had a name.

That was the problem.

Not proof.

Not certainty.

A name.

Darren had been seen going into Officer Grant's office more than once.

That fact had begun travelling through the wing in the particular way facts travelled when nobody knew what they meant.

Quietly at first.

Then with confidence.

Someone said Darren had been in there after the staff interest around Jinx's wider circle.

Someone else said Grant had called him in before that as well.

A third person had apparently decided this was enough to explain everything.

Malik listened.

He did not repeat it.

That felt unnatural.

Information normally wanted movement.

He had built half his prison identity around knowing something before the next man did.

Now he was holding a sentence that could damage somebody whether it was true or not.

Yusuf found him near lunch.

"You look constipated."

Malik stared at him.

"Thank you."

"Emotionally."

"Much better."

Yusuf smiled.

"What's up?"

Malik looked along the wing.

Darren was with Josh.

Josh was talking with both hands again.

Darren was laughing.

Normal.

Too normal for the story forming around him.

"People are saying Darren's talking to Grant."

Yusuf's smile disappeared.

"People say everything."

"He's been in the office."

"So?"

Malik looked at him.

"Timing."

Yusuf shook his head.

"Timing isn't proof."

Malik knew.

That was why the sentence felt heavy.

"Jinx is asking questions."

"Then let Jinx ask his own."

Malik looked at Yusuf.

"You know what happens if someone is talking and nobody says nothing."

"And you know what happens if they're not and someone says they are."

Silence.

Malik hated conversations where both sides were right.

He looked back toward Darren.

Hear all.

See all.

Say nothing.

For once, silence felt less like cowardice and more like responsibility.

---

## DARREN

Darren knew people were looking at him differently before anybody said why.

Prison had its own weather.

You felt pressure before the rain arrived.

Josh did not notice at first.

That was almost comforting.

He was arguing with Tyler about whether a car counted as ruined if the interior was horrible but the outside looked perfect.

"Obviously it counts," Josh said.

Tyler shook his head.

"You can change an interior."

"You can change your face. Doesn't mean it doesn't matter now."

Darren laughed.

Then saw two men across the landing look at him and stop talking.

His laugh died.

Josh followed his eyes.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"People keep saying nothing when it's definitely something."

Darren looked at him.

There it was again.

Josh learning.

Not just routine.

Atmosphere.

Faces.

The gap between an answer and the truth.

Darren forced a smile.

"You're becoming unbearable."

"Good teacher."

Josh grinned.

That hurt more than it should have.

Darren had liked being the experienced one.

Liked having Josh ask him things.

Liked being somebody worth following.

Now every bad thing Darren carried felt closer to Josh because Josh had chosen to stand beside him.

---

## JINX — A WING

Jinx did not like informant rumours.

They made stupid men feel intelligent.

One coincidence became a pattern.

One office conversation became betrayal.

One prisoner everybody already thought was weak became the obvious answer because obvious answers felt satisfying.

Deano approached him near movement.

"You heard Darren's name?"

Jinx looked at him.

"Yeah."

"What do you think?"

"Think everyone needs to calm down."

Deano raised his eyebrows.

"That's not an answer."

"It's the only one I've got."

"He keeps going in Grant's office."

"Maybe Grant keeps calling him."

"Same thing."

"No, it isn't."

Deano considered that.

Jinx leaned against the wall.

"Rico already made enough mess. Last thing we need is people creating another enemy because they're bored."

Deano nodded slowly.

"So we do nothing?"

"We don't accuse anyone without knowing."

"That's almost mature."

"Don't spread it around."

Deano smiled.

"You know Malik already knows."

"Malik knows what everyone had for breakfast."

"And?"

Jinx looked toward C Wing direction as though he could see through walls.

"If Malik ain't repeating it yet, that tells me something."

Deano nodded.

That was the strange part.

Malik's silence had become information of its own.

---

## OFFICER GRANT

Grant called Darren in again that afternoon.

Darren arrived already angry.

"People are looking at me."

Grant sat back.

"People look at everyone."

"Don't do that."

"Do what?"

Darren laughed without humour.

"That officer thing where you pretend words don't mean what they mean."

Grant's face stayed neutral.

"What do you think people think?"

Darren stared at him.

"You know."

Grant did know.

He also believed the fear was useful.

That was where Darren and Grant understood the world differently.

Grant saw disorder and thought pressure could prevent worse disorder.

Darren felt the same pressure and experienced it as a door closing around him.

"You gave me one piece of information," Grant said. "That's all."

"And then staff moved on him."

"You don't know why staff acted."

Darren looked at him.

"Did you use what I said?"

Grant paused.

"I don't discuss sources or decisions."

Darren stood.

"Then stop calling me one."

"Sit down."

Darren did not.

Grant's tone hardened.

"Darren."

He sat.

Not because Grant had won an argument.

Because doors, reports and decisions belonged to staff in ways they did not belong to Darren.

Grant folded his hands.

"You hear something that prevents trouble, you tell me. That's the responsible thing to do."

Darren stared at him.

"Responsible for who?"

Grant frowned.

"Everyone."

Darren nearly laughed.

The word felt enormous compared with the tiny office.

Everyone.

Josh outside.

Jinx's people.

Malik.

The man staff had acted around.

Darren himself.

Grant said it as if all those interests lined up neatly.

They did not.

"I ain't got nothing," Darren said.

Grant watched him.

This time Darren let the silence stay empty.

---

## JOSH

Josh was waiting when Darren came back.

"You all right?"

Darren sighed.

"Do I look all right?"

Josh studied him far too seriously.

"Bit pale."

"Cheers."

"Want tea?"

Darren looked at him.

Josh shrugged.

"What? Tea fixes some things."

"Not this."

"Still tea though."

Darren smiled despite himself.

"Go on then."

Josh disappeared before Darren could change his mind.

Macca passed and slowed.

"Kid's adopted you, hasn't he?"

Darren watched Josh at the kettle.

"Apparently."

"Could be worse."

Darren nodded.

That was what frightened him.

It could be much worse.

And Darren was no longer sure whether the danger around him belonged only to him.

---

## MALIK — EVENING

Jinx found Malik before lock-up.

"You heard Darren's name?"

Malik looked at him.

There it was.

The moment.

He could become important again with one sentence.

Could say Darren had been in Grant's office.

Could mention timing.

Could add the pieces together and sound clever.

Instead he said:

"I've heard people chatting."

Jinx waited.

"And?"

"And nobody knows anything."

Jinx looked at him for a long second.

"That's really your answer?"

Malik nodded.

"Yeah."

Jinx accepted it.

Not happily.

Enough.

After he left, Bruce appeared beside Malik as if summoned by uncomfortable moral choices.

"You didn't tell him."

Malik looked at him.

"How the fuck do you know what I didn't tell him?"

Bruce shrugged.

"Conversation ended with everyone still alive and not shouting. Strong indicator."

Malik laughed.

Bruce opened a packet of biscuits.

"Silence suits you. Makes you look mysterious instead of nosy."

"Cheers."

"Growth."

Bruce walked away.

Malik leaned on the rail.

For years he had believed the person with information had power.

He was beginning to understand that sometimes the power was deciding the information stopped with you.
