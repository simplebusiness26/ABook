# 21 OCTOBER

## MALIK

By breakfast, Connor had become a story.

This irritated Malik because Connor had not been interesting enough to become a story forty-eight hours earlier.

Now everybody had a version.

Connor had seen it coming.

Connor had not seen anything.

Connor had jumped in because Craig had already promised him money.

Connor had jumped in because he was stupid.

Connor and Craig knew each other from outside.

Connor was Craig's cousin.

That last one had appeared from nowhere and was spreading extremely well.

"They're not even from the same place," Malik said.

His cellmate shrugged.

"Cousins can move."

"That's your defence?"

"I'm not saying it's true."

"You just repeated it."

"So did you."

Malik hated when people used his own profession against him.

The more important information arrived later.

A man Malik trusted slightly more than usual gave him a name.

Not Sharky.

A person outside who sat somewhere between Sharky and the noise inside Lewes.

That mattered.

It turned the story into a chain rather than a cloud.

Malik did not need every link.

He needed enough to know the direction.

Big Sharky had not simply been attached to the rumour because he was a famous old name.

The pressure really did lead outward and upward.

Malik felt no satisfaction at being right.

Being right was much more enjoyable when the answer was harmless.

He found Mason.

"You were right."

Mason looked up.

"Obviously. About what?"

"The kid collected the problem."

Mason's expression tightened.

"Don't call him that around people."

"What? The kid?"

"Craig's problem."

Malik smiled slightly.

"Why?"

"Because if you say something enough, prison turns it into a job title."

Malik thought about Connor's new reputation.

That was exactly what had happened overnight.

## LEAH

The report made the incident look cleaner than it had felt.

Times.

Names.

Movements.

Intervention.

Separation.

No paragraph could properly capture the second before trouble started, when everybody in the room had still been having an ordinary day.

Leah read Connor's account again.

Simple.

He saw movement towards Craig.

He reacted.

He had not known about any planned assault.

Nothing she had seen suggested otherwise.

The complication was what happened now.

Prisoners did not need evidence to classify people.

Connor had been classified.

She saw it when he came through later.

Men who had ignored him a week ago acknowledged him.

Some nodded.

One moved aside.

Connor noticed every bit of it and tried to pretend he noticed none.

That worried her.

Attention could be addictive to somebody who had spent his outside life building importance through money and younger lads listening to him.

She did not know that history in detail yet.

She could see the shape of it.

Craig appeared a few minutes later.

Leah watched Connor's posture change.

Not fear.

Ease.

Craig said something.

Connor laughed.

Leah looked away before she could become one more person watching Craig.

Then looked back almost immediately.

That annoyed her.

## DEANO

"It's done."

Deano said it to three men at once.

"What is?" one asked.

"Whatever yesterday was."

"Didn't work though."

"Exactly why it's done for us."

The youngest frowned.

"What if the money's still there?"

Deano looked at him.

"You know what money you definitely won't spend?"

"What?"

"Money you ruin your life trying to earn."

"Bit dramatic."

"You watched yesterday?"

The lad went quiet.

Deano lowered his voice.

"Listen. Sharky ain't in this prison. Craig is. Staff are here. We are here. Every consequence is here. Stop treating outside people like they can spend our years."

One of the men nodded.

The youngest did not.

Deano noticed.

That was tomorrow's problem.

Today he had made the rule clear.

His people were out.

Whether prison allowed people to stay out was another question.

## CONNOR

Connor had not expected respect to feel embarrassing.

He had expected it to feel good.

Outside, being known had felt good.

People rang him. Asked him for things. Needed him. Younger lads listened when he spoke.

Inside, men Connor did not know were nodding at him because of something he had done without thinking.

He did not know what to do with the nods.

Nod back?

Too much?

Don't nod?

Disrespectful?

Prison had turned basic neck movement into politics.

Bruce found this hilarious.

"You've got fans."

"Shut up."

"Want me to manage you?"

"No."

"Ten percent."

"Of what?"

"Exactly. Very cheap."

Connor smiled.

Mason appeared behind Bruce.

"Don't encourage him."

"I'm building his brand."

"He doesn't need a brand."

Bruce looked at Connor.

"Everyone needs a brand."

Connor pointed at his ginger hair.

"Is that yours?"

Bruce looked offended.

"Natural marketing."

Mason walked away shaking his head.

Connor was still smiling when Craig came over.

Bruce's expression changed just enough to show he noticed.

"I'll leave the celebrities," he said.

"Please do," Craig replied.

Bruce pointed at Connor.

"Remember who knew you before fame."

"You met me four days ago."

"Exactly. Day one."

He vanished into the landing.

Craig stood beside Connor.

"How's the arm?"

"Fine."

"Still lying."

"Bit sore."

"Better."

Connor looked around.

"Everyone's weird with me now."

Craig's face gave away nothing.

"Told you."

"Told me what?"

"Reputation you didn't earn can still cost you."

Connor frowned.

"I did earn this one."

Craig looked at him.

There was no anger in it.

But Connor immediately heard himself.

"You know what I mean."

"Do I?"

Connor sighed.

"I helped you."

"You did."

"So people know."

"They know what they saw."

"Same thing."

Craig shook his head.

"Never the same thing."

Connor thought of the burglary rumours outside. Three guns. Three people arrested. Watches piled to the ceiling.

He understood the point.

"So what do I do?"

Craig shrugged.

"Be Connor."

"That's your advice?"

"You want a speech?"

"Mason gives better speeches."

"Then go ask Mason."

Connor laughed.

Craig started to walk away.

"Craig."

He turned.

Connor hesitated.

"Who was that yesterday?"

Craig looked at him for a moment.

"A man making a bad decision."

"Because of you?"

"Apparently."

"Why?"

Craig smiled faintly.

"There you are again."

"What?"

"Chasing the car."

Connor rolled his eyes.

Craig walked off.

Connor watched him go.

He still did not know why there was trouble around Craig.

What he knew now was worse.

The trouble was real.

## JAY

Rico had become everybody's problem and nobody's friend.

That happened quickly in prison.

Men encouraged you until an event became real, then suddenly remembered appointments elsewhere.

Jay sat with him for ten minutes.

Rico barely spoke.

"Anyone outside been in touch?" Jay asked.

Rico shook his head.

"Course not," Jay said.

Rico looked up angrily.

"What?"

"They wanted a result. Didn't get one. Now you're an awkward conversation."

"You don't know that."

"I know people."

Rico stared at the floor.

Jay's anger softened despite himself.

"You all right?"

Rico laughed bitterly.

"Now you ask?"

"I asked you yesterday."

"You called me stupid."

"You were being stupid. Both things can be true."

Rico said nothing.

Jay leaned back.

"This is what I was trying to tell you. People outside talk about loyalty because it's free for them."

Rico finally looked at him.

"You think Craig's gonna come for me?"

Jay did not know.

That was the answer Rico feared most.

"I think you need to stop making choices based on what people might do and start dealing with what you've already done."

It sounded annoyingly like something an older man would say.

Jay hated that.

He was becoming Mason.

## BIG SHARKY

This time the update came in the evening.

It had failed.

Big Sharky listened to the explanation without interrupting.

Connor's name appeared for the first time.

"Who?" Sharky asked.

The caller repeated it.

Young lad. New arrival. Apparently stepped in.

Sharky stared at the wall.

"He with Craig?"

"Don't think he was."

"Was?"

The caller paused.

Big Sharky heard the answer in the pause.

"Right," he said.

"What do you want done?"

Sharky smiled without humour.

"Nothing."

"Nothing?"

"Not today."

He ended the call.

Craig had always been good at collecting loyalty.

That was one of the things people admired about him.

It was also one of the things Big Sharky had once admired.

Years ago.

Before prison.

Before the misunderstanding that had become a fact through repetition.

Before Craig's name grew while Sharky's life narrowed.

A new boy had stood beside him after less than a week.

Big Sharky found that irritating in a way he could not quite explain.

He put the phone down.

Craig was still in Lewes.

For now.
