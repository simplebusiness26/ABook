# 19 OCTOBER

## BIG SHARKY

Big Sharky did not chase answers.

That was one of the differences between being young and being older.

Young men sent three messages because the first had not been answered quickly enough.

Older men learned that silence could do work of its own.

He had made his position known.

That was enough.

For now.

A man called him late morning with an update that contained very little actual information.

People had heard.

People were talking.

Some were interested.

Some were nervous.

Craig knew there was attention around him.

Big Sharky listened.

"And?"

The caller hesitated.

"Nothing yet."

Big Sharky looked out at a patch of grey sky through reinforced glass.

"Then why are you calling me?"

"Thought you'd want to know."

"I want to know when there's something to know."

He ended the call.

The old anger was not hot anymore.

Hot anger burned itself out.

This had had years to cool into something denser.

Craig had once been younger than him. Behind him. Learning from the same men. Listening in rooms where Sharky spoke first.

Then Sharky had gone away and the world had rearranged itself without asking permission.

Now people said Craig's name the way they used to say his.

Maybe that was the real insult.

Maybe it wasn't.

Big Sharky no longer trusted memory enough to separate what had happened from what years inside had done to it.

He only trusted the conclusion.

Craig had done him wrong.

Or had allowed wrong to happen.

Or had benefited when it did.

Sometimes, after enough years, those became the same thing.

## DEANO

"You're not doing anything."

Deano said it quietly.

The young prisoner opposite him laughed.

"Who said I was?"

"Your face."

"What's wrong with my face?"

"You've got pound signs where your common sense should be."

The lad looked away.

Deano knew him from outside. Not closely. Close enough that if something stupid happened, somebody's mother would eventually ring somebody else's mother and the whole thing would become everybody's business.

"I'm just hearing things," the lad said.

"Hear them. That's free."

"You don't even like Craig."

"Don't know him."

"Exactly."

Deano leaned forward.

"That's exactly why I'm not adding time to my sentence over him. Or Sharky. Or whoever else wants to have a disagreement from somewhere they aren't standing."

The lad smiled.

"You scared?"

Deano nodded immediately.

That took the air out of it.

"Yeah. Of extra years. Of my mum getting another phone call. Of doing something for people who'll forget my name six months later. Pick one."

The lad had no answer.

Deano sat back.

"Being scared of stupid consequences is called getting older. Try it."

## CONNOR

Connor had been inside less than a week and already hated the word *induction*.

It sounded like something that should take twenty minutes.

Prison had managed to stretch it across days.

He sat beside Craig waiting for another part of it to begin.

"How many times can they tell you where the fire exits are when you're not allowed to use any exits?" Connor said.

Craig looked at him.

"Important to know where freedom is during a fire."

Connor laughed.

"You take the piss out of everything."

"Not everything."

"Name one thing you take seriously."

Craig thought about it.

"Breakfast."

"That's it?"

"People who skip breakfast make bad decisions."

Connor shook his head.

Across the room two men were talking quietly.

One looked over.

Connor noticed.

This time he did not stare back.

Progress.

Craig noticed that too.

"Learning."

"Mason's been threatening me with wisdom all week."

"Dangerous stuff."

Connor smiled.

Then a different man walked past and nodded at Craig.

Craig nodded back.

Connor waited until he was gone.

"You know everyone?"

"No."

"Everyone knows you."

"Different problem."

There was that word again.

Problem.

Mason said Connor should not collect Craig's.

Officer Mercer had practically said the same thing.

Connor was beginning to get annoyed at everybody speaking around something he could not see.

"Is something going on?"

Craig looked at him.

"Something's always going on."

"You know what I mean."

"Do I?"

"People are weird."

Craig smiled.

"Connor, you're in prison. That's not breaking news."

Connor wanted to press him.

Then Bruce appeared from nowhere and dropped into the seat on Connor's other side.

"Morning, celebrities."

Connor looked at him.

"Who invited you?"

"Life."

Craig sighed.

"We're being punished."

"You've only just noticed?"

Bruce leaned forward and looked at Connor.

"You still sitting with him then?"

Connor frowned.

"Why does everyone keep saying that?"

Bruce looked at Craig.

Craig looked at Bruce.

Something passed between them Connor could not read.

Bruce stood.

"No reason."

Connor grabbed his sleeve lightly before he could leave.

"Oi."

Bruce looked down at the hand, then at Connor.

Connor let go.

"What?"

Bruce's usual grin softened slightly.

"Just keep your eyes open, new boy."

Then he left.

Connor watched him go.

"What's that meant to mean?"

Craig leaned back.

"Probably means Bruce wants to feel mysterious."

From across the room Bruce shouted, "I *am* mysterious."

Connor laughed.

Craig did too.

But Connor noticed Craig's eyes move around the room afterwards.

Only once.

Quickly.

That was the first time Connor wondered if Craig was watching people too.

## BRUCE

Bruce was not mysterious.

He was nosy.

There was a difference, though he considered both flattering.

He had heard enough now to know the Craig situation had moved past entertainment.

Money was real enough.

Sharky's name was real enough.

A couple of men were talking too carefully.

That was usually a bad sign.

People with nothing to hide spoke loudly because they wanted an audience.

People with something to hide became polite.

Bruce found Mason.

"Your boy's gonna get himself adopted."

Mason looked up.

"Who?"

"Connor."

"He's not my boy."

"See? Already denying paternity."

Mason ignored that.

"What's he done?"

Bruce nodded towards Connor and Craig.

"Keeps being there."

Mason's face changed.

"You heard something?"

Bruce rocked his head side to side.

"Heard lots. Believe less."

"Bruce."

"Money's not imaginary anymore."

Mason went still.

Bruce's humour disappeared for exactly one sentence.

"Tell the kid to stop looking like Craig's little brother."

Then he walked away before Mason could ask more.

Humour was useful like that.

You could put something serious inside it and leave before anybody made you explain.

## LEAH

Leah stopped Connor near the stairs.

"You again."

Connor smiled.

"What have I done?"

"Interesting first question."

His smile disappeared.

"Nothing."

"Relax."

He visibly tried to relax and failed.

Leah almost felt sorry for him.

"I've noticed you spending a lot of time around Craig."

Connor's face hardened in that young-man way where fear tried to dress itself as annoyance.

"We're just talking."

"I didn't say you weren't."

"Everyone keeps going on about it."

"Maybe consider why."

"Nobody tells me why."

That was fair.

Leah could not tell him rumours she could not verify. She could not tell him half the wing appeared to be running an unofficial intelligence operation around one prisoner. She could not tell him staff had started noticing little changes without having enough concrete information to act on all of them.

"Because Craig attracts attention," she said. "And attention in prison is rarely free."

Connor looked past her.

"He's been sound with me."

"That might be completely true."

"So what's the problem?"

"I'm not telling you he's a problem. I'm telling you other people's interest in him can become yours if they see you as attached."

Connor considered that.

"I'm not attached."

Leah looked over his shoulder.

Craig was waiting for him.

Connor followed her gaze.

"All right," he said. "That looks bad."

Leah laughed before she could stop herself.

Connor smiled.

"See?"

"Go on."

He walked back towards Craig.

Leah watched him go.

Bennett came around the corner a few seconds later, nearly bumping into her.

"Sorry."

"You all right?"

"Fine."

Too quick.

Leah watched him continue.

Two different problems, she thought.

Two men insisting they were fine.

Prison produced a lot of that.

## MASON

Mason waited until Connor was alone.

"We need a word."

Connor groaned.

"If this is about collecting problems—"

"It's about collecting problems."

"Mason."

"Listen."

Something in Mason's voice worked because Connor actually did.

Mason chose his words carefully.

"There's talk around Craig. More than normal talk."

Connor's eyes sharpened.

"What talk?"

"Doesn't matter."

"It obviously matters if you're warning me."

"It matters that you understand standing beside someone can make people think you've chosen a side even if you haven't."

Connor looked irritated.

"What side?"

Mason sighed.

"That's exactly the point. You don't even know there are sides."

Connor went quiet.

Mason could see curiosity fighting self-preservation.

Curiosity was winning.

It usually did at twenty-one.

"Is someone gonna do something to him?" Connor asked.

Mason did not answer.

He did not know.

Not properly.

"I'm telling you to keep your head."

"If you know something—"

"I know prison. That's what I know."

Connor looked towards Craig.

Mason followed his eyes.

"And that's the other thing," Mason said.

"What?"

"You looked at him before you looked at the door."

Connor frowned.

"So?"

Mason shook his head.

"Nothing."

"Why does everyone say nothing when they mean something?"

"You'll fit in beautifully."

Mason walked off.

Connor stood where he was for a while.

Then, despite every warning he had received that week, he went back to Craig.
