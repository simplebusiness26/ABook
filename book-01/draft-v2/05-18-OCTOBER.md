# 18 OCTOBER

## LEON

Leon liked prison best when it was boring.

Boring meant predictable.

Predictable meant manageable.

Wake up. Eat. Train if he could. Read. Keep his cell clean. Speak to the same people. Avoid arguments that began with somebody saying *I'm not being funny, but...*

Nothing useful followed those words.

Craig had made the place less boring without actually doing anything.

Leon resented him slightly for that.

He was stretching when Rico came over.

"You heard?"

Leon kept stretching.

"Probably."

"About Craig."

"Definitely."

Rico lowered himself onto the bench.

"People are serious now."

Leon looked at him.

"People are always serious when they're talking about somebody else's consequences."

"I'm just saying."

"Everybody's just saying. That's how things become everybody's problem."

Rico rubbed his hands together.

"Some of the lads might get asked where they stand."

Leon stopped stretching.

There it was.

Not *you've been asked*.

Not *will you help*.

Just a sentence placed in the middle of the floor to see whether Leon would step around it.

"I stand here," Leon said.

Rico frowned.

"What?"

"In the gym. Doing my own sentence."

"You know what I mean."

"Yeah. That's why I answered."

Rico smiled without humour.

"People might take not choosing as choosing."

Leon picked up his towel.

"Then people need hobbies."

He walked away.

For the rest of the session he trained harder than usual.

That annoyed him too.

He had refused to be involved, yet the conversation had still changed his day.

Neutrality, Leon realised, only worked if everybody else agreed it existed.

## JAY

Jay got the call he had been expecting and hoping would not come.

He listened more than he spoke.

That was usually the safest way to deal with people who had already decided what they wanted you to say.

The person on the other end talked about loyalty.

Talked about history.

Talked about people Jay knew outside.

Talked around Craig without saying anything direct enough to be useful later.

Jay stared at the wall while he listened.

"You know people near him," the caller said eventually.

Jay's jaw tightened.

"I know people everywhere."

"Exactly."

"That wasn't me agreeing."

Silence.

Then: "Nobody said it was."

That was the whole call.

No explicit instruction.

No threat.

Nothing Jay could repeat afterwards without sounding paranoid.

He put the phone down and stood there for a moment.

When he turned, Malik was twenty feet away pretending very badly not to watch him.

Jay pointed at him.

"Don't."

Malik raised both hands.

"Didn't say nothing."

"Your eyebrows did."

Malik smiled.

Jay did not.

He found Rico later.

"Anyone asks you anything about Craig, you come to me first."

Rico's expression changed.

"Why?"

"Because I'm asking."

"You just said you weren't involved."

"I'm not."

"Sounds involved."

Jay stepped closer.

Not threatening.

Enough.

"Keeping you from doing something stupid is not the same as doing it with you."

Rico looked away.

Jay knew that look.

It meant the conversation had arrived late.

"Who spoke to you?"

"Nobody."

"Rico."

"I'm telling you."

Jay stared at him.

Prison taught you to hear the shape of a lie.

This one had corners.

"Don't be stupid," Jay said.

Rico shrugged.

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"Maybe listen to the consensus."

## CONNOR

Connor's girlfriend finally answered on the second try.

Her voice lifted when she heard him.

"Oh my God."

Connor smiled automatically.

"Nice to hear you too."

"No, I mean— are you okay?"

There was that question again.

Everybody outside asked it because they had no better one.

Everybody inside lied because there was no better answer.

"Yeah."

"You don't sound okay."

"I'm in prison. I'm not meant to sound like I'm in Ibiza."

She laughed.

Connor needed that laugh more than he expected.

They talked quickly. Home. His mum. Who had been asking about him. Rumours about the case. Somebody had apparently already said Connor had been arrested with three guns, two cars and enough watches to open a shop.

"That's not even what happened," Connor said.

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Connor."

He heard irritation and immediately regretted the question.

"Sorry."

There was a pause.

"How long are you there for?"

His stomach tightened.

"Don't know."

"But like... roughly?"

"I don't know."

He wanted her to say it did not matter.

Wanted her to say she'd wait if it was a year, five, fifteen.

Instead she said, "Right."

It was not betrayal.

It was one word.

But Connor stored it anyway.

"I'll ring you again," he said.

"Yeah. Please do."

That helped.

A little.

When he put the phone down, Craig was nearby again.

"Girlfriend?" Craig asked.

Connor looked at him suspiciously.

"How do you know?"

"Different face."

"What different face?"

"Mum face is guilt. Girlfriend face is panic."

Connor laughed despite himself.

"You some relationship expert?"

"Absolutely not."

They walked together for a while.

Connor had stopped noticing that they did that.

Other people had not.

"Mason keeps telling me not to get other people's problems," Connor said.

"Good advice."

"You're apparently the problem."

Craig looked at him.

"Apparently?"

"Everyone's weird around you."

"You're not."

Connor thought about that.

"Maybe I don't know enough."

"Definitely."

Connor laughed.

"You always this annoying?"

"Only with people who ask too many questions."

"So what are you in for then? Properly."

Craig glanced at him.

"Conspiracy."

"Yeah, everyone says that. Conspiracy to what?"

"According to them? Supply Class A drugs on a scale that makes me sound much more organised than I feel before breakfast."

Connor grinned.

"You're taking the piss."

"Bit."

"So you're a dealer."

Craig stopped walking.

Connor stopped too.

For the first time Craig looked at him in a way that made Connor remember everybody else's reaction to the name.

Not anger.

Assessment.

Then it passed.

"That's one word for it," Craig said.

They carried on.

Connor did not ask the next question.

He wanted to.

He was learning.

## BENNETT

Bennett had promised himself he would not look at his phone until his break.

He lasted twenty-seven minutes.

Nothing new.

That should have made him feel better.

It didn't.

He was beginning to understand that waiting for a message could be worse than receiving one.

Senior Officer Patel found him near the office.

"Everything all right?"

Bennett almost laughed.

Why had everyone suddenly become interested in whether he was all right?

"Fine."

Patel looked at him for a moment.

"You sure?"

"Dad's not well."

This was becoming his official explanation.

It even had the advantage of being true.

"Take the time if you need it."

"Can't afford it."

The words escaped before Bennett could stop them.

Patel's eyes sharpened slightly.

"Everything okay financially?"

"Figure of speech."

"Right."

Bennett smiled.

Patel did not quite smile back.

Across the wing there was a burst of laughter.

Craig stood with Connor and another prisoner.

Bennett watched them for half a second.

Patel followed his gaze.

"That lad's getting attached quickly."

"Connor?"

"Yeah."

"Maybe Craig's just being friendly."

Patel looked at Bennett.

"You believe in coincidences now?"

Bennett's stomach turned for reasons Patel could not possibly know.

"Sometimes."

Patel walked on.

Bennett checked his phone at the next opportunity.

One new message.

**Need to know you're still good.**

He stared at it.

Then typed:

**Yeah.**

He did not feel good.

## MACCA

Macca had heard about Craig.

He did not care.

Not because he was brave.

Because his daughter had a school assembly on Tuesday.

That was the only thing in the world with sufficient space in his head.

"She got a line," he told Darren.

Darren was lying on the bunk with his arm over his eyes.

"What line?"

"In the assembly."

"Oh."

"One line. Been practising it all week apparently."

Darren moved his arm enough to look at him.

"That's good."

"I'm missing it."

Darren put the arm back.

There was nothing useful to say.

Macca hated prison most when people tried to be positive about it.

*There'll be other assemblies.*

*She'll understand.*

*At least you can ring.*

All technically true.

All useless.

He had been measuring his sentence in events rather than months.

Birthday.

Christmas.

First day back at school.

Now assembly.

Some men measured time with gym sessions. Some with canteen. Some with court dates.

Macca measured it by things his children did without him.

Somebody shouted down the landing about Craig.

Another man laughed and shouted something back about the money.

Macca closed his eyes.

"If these idiots get us all locked up over some bloke I don't know," he said, "I'm going to lose my head."

Darren smiled faintly.

"Thought you didn't care."

"I care about my phone call. Everything that interferes with my phone call becomes my politics."

Darren nodded.

That, at least, made sense.

## MALIK

Malik had stopped asking how much.

That was progress.

He had moved on to a more worrying question.

Who believed they could actually collect it?

He did not ask that out loud either.

More progress.

He watched Rico talking to two men he did not usually talk to.

Watched Jay notice.

Watched Jay pretend not to notice.

Watched Deano arrive, see the same group and change direction.

Nothing happened.

That was what made it interesting.

Prison stories always sounded obvious after the event.

*Everyone knew.*

*You could see it coming.*

*The signs were there.*

The truth was the signs looked exactly like ordinary prison life until they didn't.

Men talked.

Men moved around.

People borrowed things.

Somebody owed somebody money.

Somebody had an argument and made up ten minutes later.

You could build a conspiracy out of any afternoon if you already knew the ending.

Malik did not know the ending.

He simply knew the atmosphere was becoming expensive.

Craig walked past with Connor.

Connor was telling him something about his little brother.

Craig was listening.

Malik watched them go.

"You know what I don't get?" he said to Mason.

Mason looked up from what he was doing.

"Most things, probably."

"Connor."

Mason followed his gaze.

"What about him?"

"Why him?"

"Why him what?"

"Craig."

Mason shrugged.

"Maybe Craig likes him."

Malik stared.

"That's your analysis?"

"You wanted a documentary?"

"Craig doesn't just pick up some random kid for no reason."

Mason's face changed slightly.

"Careful."

"What?"

"You're starting to sound like the rumours you sell."

Malik smiled.

"I don't sell rumours."

Mason laughed so loudly two men looked over.

Malik ignored him.

Connor and Craig disappeared around the corner.

Somewhere else on the wing, Rico was still talking.

Malik thought about Jay's warning.

You know too much about things you're not in.

For the first time, he wondered whether there was a point where knowing became involvement all by itself.
