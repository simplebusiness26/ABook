# 22 OCTOBER

## CONNOR

Connor had been in prison eight days when somebody he did not know asked him for Craig.

That was new.

"What do you mean?" Connor said.

The man looked at him as though Connor were being difficult on purpose.

"Is he about?"

"Why you asking me?"

A shrug.

"You're always with him."

Connor felt something warm and stupid move through his chest.

Importance.

He recognised it immediately because he had lived on it outside.

People used to ring him because he knew who had what, who was where, who could sort something. Younger lads waited for him to make decisions. His phone buzzing had once felt like proof that he mattered.

Now a stranger had asked him where Craig was.

Different building.

Same drug.

Connor tried not to enjoy it.

"I'm not his secretary."

The man laughed.

"Didn't say you were."

"Then ask him yourself."

"I would if I knew where he was."

Connor almost answered.

Then Mason's voice arrived in his head like an unwanted parental-control feature.

*Don't collect other people's problems.*

"Dunno," Connor said.

That was true enough.

The man walked away.

Craig appeared ten minutes later.

Connor told him.

"Some bloke was asking me where you were."

"What bloke?"

Connor described him badly.

Craig waited.

"That's all you've got?"

"He had a grey top."

"Excellent. Narrowed it to half the prison."

Connor laughed.

"Why are they asking me?"

Craig looked at him.

Connor answered his own question.

"Because I'm always with you."

"Apparently."

Connor heard the warning in Mason's voice again.

He pushed it away.

"You make it sound like a disease."

Craig smiled.

"Reputation spreads faster."

They walked.

Connor had started to notice people made space without always realising they were doing it.

He had started to notice something worse too.

Sometimes they made space for *him* now.

## DARREN

Darren did not care who Connor stood beside.

He cared that his hands would not stop shaking.

The morning had begun badly and developed ambition.

He had slept in pieces. Dreamed in fragments. Woken angry at the wall for existing.

Somebody on the landing was discussing Craig again.

Darren wanted to throw something at him.

Not because he disliked Craig.

Because the world seemed unfairly interested in people who could afford to have dramatic problems.

Darren's problem was small, repetitive and humiliating.

Get through the hour.

Then the next.

Do not borrow.

Do not promise tomorrow's canteen for today's relief.

Do not ring home and lie.

Do not think about how many times he had already promised himself the same things.

Macca sat nearby reading a letter.

"Good news?" Darren asked.

Macca folded it.

"Daughter did her assembly."

"How'd it go?"

"Apparently smashed it."

Macca smiled.

Then the smile fell.

"Missed it."

Darren nodded.

Two men shouted something about Connor being Craig's new bodyguard.

Macca rolled his eyes.

"They've got another hobby now."

Darren looked at him.

"What?"

"The new lad. Connor."

Darren shrugged.

"Good for him."

"You don't care?"

"Macca, if Craig becomes king of England today, my morning stays exactly the same."

Macca considered that.

"Fair."

Darren looked down at his hands.

Not quite exactly the same.

Prison politics always found a way to disturb the smallest lives eventually.

But for now, Craig's war belonged to somebody else.

Darren was busy fighting his own.

## LEAH

Connor was changing.

Not dramatically.

That would have been easier.

Small things.

He walked differently.

Not tougher exactly. Less uncertain.

He had stopped checking every doorway as though it might contain an exam.

Men spoke to him now.

More importantly, he expected them to.

Leah watched him cross the wing and disliked how quickly the building could reward a young man for exactly the wrong lesson.

He had done something brave.

That part was true.

The danger was what he decided the bravery meant.

Craig joined him.

Connor said something. Craig laughed.

Leah found herself wondering what.

She immediately went back to her paperwork.

Thirty seconds later she looked up again.

Craig was gone.

Connor remained.

Leah was annoyed to discover she had noticed the difference.

"You all right, Mercer?" Patel asked.

"Fine."

Patel smiled.

"Everyone in this place is fine."

Leah looked at him.

"Occupational requirement."

"Apparently."

Across the office Bennett dropped a pen.

It hit the floor.

He flinched as though it had exploded.

Leah and Patel both looked at him.

"Fine," Bennett said immediately.

Patel raised an eyebrow.

Leah almost laughed.

Nobody did.

## BENNETT

The message had changed.

That was the problem.

At first it had been about his father.

Then money.

Then favours.

Now somebody had asked a question about Craig.

A simple question.

Nothing Bennett could prove was connected to anything.

**What's everyone saying about yesterday?**

He read it three times.

He could answer truthfully.

Everyone was saying everything.

Instead he did not answer.

Another message arrived forty minutes later.

**Just asking mate.**

Mate.

Bennett hated the word now.

People who called you mate while reminding you they knew where your father lived were not mates.

He put the phone away.

During shift he saw Connor talking to Craig.

He wondered what Connor knew.

Then wondered whether the people messaging him knew Connor's name already.

Then stopped himself.

This was how paranoia grew: one unanswered question fertilising another.

Patel appeared beside him.

"You keep staring at that lad."

Bennett's heart kicked.

"What lad?"

"Connor."

"Wasn't."

Patel watched him.

Bennett heard the bad answer after it had left his mouth.

Too defensive.

"Everything okay?" Patel asked.

"Fine."

Patel sighed.

"Of course it is."

He walked away.

Bennett waited until he was out of sight before exhaling.

## MALIK

Connor's value had changed.

Malik did not mean as a person.

He meant as information.

A week ago, Connor was useful only because he was new and obviously frightened, which made him mildly entertaining.

Now people wanted to know things about him.

Who was he?

What was his case?

Did he know Craig outside?

Why had he stepped in?

Was Craig protecting him now?

Malik knew more than most because Mason talked when you asked the right way and Connor had not yet learned that casual conversation could become public property.

Twenty-one.

Aggravated burglary.

First time in prison.

Family boy.

Drug line outside.

That last piece interested Malik.

"What sort of line?" he asked Connor casually.

Connor looked at him.

"Why?"

"Conversation."

"You don't have conversations. You interview people."

Malik smiled.

The kid was learning quickly.

"Fair."

Craig was nearby.

Connor glanced at him, then back at Malik.

"Just weed. Nothing major."

Craig's eyes moved up from what he was reading.

Only for a second.

Malik saw it.

Connor did not.

"Who were you getting through?" Malik asked.

Connor laughed.

"Nice try."

"See? Conversation."

"No."

Craig went back to reading.

But Malik had noticed that tiny reaction.

Interesting.

He filed it away.

Information belonged to the patient.

## MASON

Mason found Connor enjoying himself.

That was the problem.

The lad was sitting with three prisoners he had not known a week ago, telling a story that had all of them laughing.

Good.

Normal.

Healthy, probably.

Then a man passed and nodded at Connor with a seriousness Connor had not earned through time.

Connor nodded back.

Mason saw the tiny lift in his expression.

There.

That was what worried him.

"Walk with me," Mason said later.

Connor sighed.

"Am I in trouble?"

"Not with me."

"Then no."

Mason started walking anyway.

Connor followed because curiosity was still his strongest weakness.

"You like this," Mason said.

"Like what?"

"People knowing you."

Connor frowned.

"I don't care."

"Course you do. Everybody does a bit."

"You don't know me."

Mason looked at him.

"I know twenty-one."

Connor rolled his eyes.

"Here we go."

"You did a good thing. Don't let prison turn it into permission to do stupid ones."

Connor stopped walking.

"Why does everyone think I'm gonna become an idiot because people nod at me?"

Mason stopped too.

"Because you've already noticed the nods."

Connor's face changed.

Mason softened.

"That's not an insult. Attention feels good, especially when you're scared. Makes the place feel less big. Just remember where it came from."

"I helped someone."

"Exactly. Keep it that simple."

Connor looked away.

"Craig's been sound."

"I believe you."

"You don't like him?"

"I don't know him."

"Everyone's got an opinion though."

Mason smiled.

"Welcome to prison."

They started walking again.

Behind them, somebody called Connor's name.

Connor turned automatically.

Mason watched him.

The lad liked being known.

That did not make him bad.

It made him vulnerable in a completely different way.
