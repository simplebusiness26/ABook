# 17 OCTOBER

## CONNOR

By the third morning Connor had learned three things.

One: nobody ever told you enough.

Two: everybody else acted like they had been told everything.

Three: if you asked too many questions, somebody would eventually laugh at you.

He was standing beside Craig when he learned a fourth.

People could look at you because of who you were standing beside.

Connor noticed a lad across the way glance at Craig, then at him, then back at Craig.

Connor checked his own clothes in case something was wrong with them.

Craig saw him do it.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You've looked at your top three times."

"That bloke keeps looking over."

Craig did not turn.

"Maybe he likes your top."

"I'm serious."

"That makes one of us."

Connor stared at him.

Craig smiled faintly.

It still felt strange seeing that. Craig smiling. Craig waiting in a queue. Craig complaining that tea tasted like somebody had described tea to hot water.

Connor had expected a man with a reputation to behave like a reputation.

Craig behaved like a person.

That somehow made the reputation worse.

"Why does everyone know you?" Connor asked.

Craig looked at him.

"Everyone doesn't."

"Feels like it."

"That's because you're new. Everyone feels like everyone when you don't know anyone."

Connor thought about that.

"Still didn't answer."

"No."

"You do that a lot."

"Keeps conversations short."

The lad across the way looked over again.

This time Connor held his gaze a second longer than he should have.

Craig noticed that too.

"Don't."

Connor looked at him.

"Don't what?"

"Start inventing problems because you're bored."

"He keeps looking."

"Let him wear his eyes out."

Connor wanted to say he wasn't bothered.

Instead he looked away.

## BRUCE

Bruce was five foot nothing according to men who enjoyed exaggerating downwards.

He was actually taller than that.

Not enough to matter.

He had ginger hair, narrow shoulders and the sort of face that made officers assume he was about to ask a reasonable question shortly before he said something ridiculous.

He also seemed to know everybody.

Connor first saw him walking backwards while arguing with a man twice his size.

"I'm telling you, that ain't what happened."

"You weren't even there."

"Exactly. Means I've got no reason to lie."

The bigger man started laughing.

Bruce turned, nearly walked into Connor, corrected without breaking stride and said, "New boy."

Connor stopped.

"What?"

Bruce pointed at him.

"Definitely new boy."

"How do you know?"

"Still got hope in your face."

Then he walked off.

Connor looked at Mason.

"Who the fuck was that?"

Mason watched Bruce disappear through a group of men like a ferret entering a hedge.

"Bruce."

"What's his problem?"

"Time."

"What?"

"He's got too much of it."

Later Connor heard somebody telling a story about Bruce involving a tuna tin and a sock.

The story changed three times before the sentence ended.

Connor looked at Bruce across the room.

Bruce was eating crisps and trying to swap flavours with somebody.

"That little ginger bloke?"

"Don't let the packaging fool you," Mason said.

Bruce looked over as if he had heard him from twenty feet away.

"I'm very dangerous, Mason."

"Course you are."

"Tell him."

"I just did."

Bruce gave Connor a solemn nod.

Connor had no idea what was real anymore.

Bruce grinned.

That appeared to be the point.

## LEAH

Leah noticed atmospheres before she noticed rumours.

Rumours were words.

Atmosphere was behaviour.

Men who normally argued stopped arguing when somebody walked past. People stood in slightly different groups. Questions arrived disguised as jokes.

Craig had been at Lewes three days and the building had not changed.

The way people occupied it had.

Leah watched two prisoners break off a conversation when she approached.

"Morning," she said.

"Morning, miss."

"What were we discussing?"

"Football."

"Must have been devastating football."

Neither laughed.

That interested her.

Further along, Craig stood beside Connor.

Again.

Leah frowned.

Not because she thought Craig had done anything.

Because Connor was doing what inexperienced prisoners often did: attaching himself to the first person who made the place feel less unfamiliar.

Sometimes that person was harmless.

Sometimes they were the worst person available.

Connor had somehow selected the most complicated option in the building.

She called him over.

"Connor."

He came across immediately.

Too immediately.

Still first-timer.

"You settling?"

"Yeah."

"That wasn't convincing."

"I'm all right."

"Better. Still not convincing."

Connor gave a small smile.

Leah lowered her voice.

"You don't need to impress anybody."

His face closed slightly.

"I'm not."

"Good."

"Why does everyone keep saying that to me?"

"Because everybody can see you trying not to."

Connor looked irritated.

Leah preferred irritated to frightened.

"Just keep your head," she said. "You're here for your own case. Don't accidentally start serving somebody else's."

Connor looked towards Craig before he could stop himself.

Leah saw it.

So did he.

"I'm not involved in nothing," Connor said.

"Excellent. Stay that way."

He walked off.

Bennett appeared beside her a minute later.

"Making friends?"

"Saving paperwork."

Bennett looked towards Craig.

"You think something's coming?"

Leah turned to him.

"Why?"

He hesitated half a second too long.

"Just all the attention."

Leah watched him.

"If you hear something concrete, you tell me."

"Course."

He smiled.

Leah did not like the smile.

Not because it was false.

Because it was trying too hard to be normal.

## DEANO

Deano did not care about Craig.

This was something he had now told four people, which was usually how you knew somebody cared at least a little.

"I'm saying it's not our business," he said.

His cellmate lay on the top bunk staring at the ceiling.

"Didn't say it was."

"Everyone's making it everyone's business."

"You're the one talking about it."

Deano looked up.

"Do you want to be helpful or do you want to be clever?"

"Clever."

"Thought so."

The problem was not Craig.

The problem was money.

Money made men develop opinions very quickly.

Yesterday, half the wing had barely known whether Craig was actually there.

Today, men were discussing Sharky like they had attended family Christmas with him.

"You heard the figure?" his cellmate asked.

"I've heard six figures."

"One I heard was—"

"Don't care."

"You literally just said—"

"I care that people care. Different thing."

That was Deano's real concern.

He had his people. Friends from outside. Men he had known in other prisons. Lads who trained together and shared food and argued about stupid things.

He did not want one of them deciding that somebody else's grudge was a shortcut to money.

Because the money, if it existed at all, lived outside.

The consequences would be served inside.

"If anyone asks," Deano said, "we're not involved."

His cellmate sat up slightly.

"Who's asking?"

"Nobody."

"Then why—"

"Because I want the answer ready."

The cellmate smiled.

"You care."

Deano threw a sock at him.

## MALIK

The rumour now had geography.

That was when Malik knew it had become dangerous.

At first it was simply *Craig*.

Then *money*.

Then *Sharky*.

Now people were talking about who knew men near Craig, who had been on a wing with somebody before, whose cousin knew whose cousin.

Not plans exactly.

Paths.

Connections.

Malik disliked connections when people started drawing them out loud.

He found Jay near the servery.

"It's spreading."

Jay did not need to ask what.

"Everything spreads."

"This one's growing legs."

"Then stop feeding it."

Malik frowned.

"You think I'm feeding it?"

Jay looked at him.

"You live on information."

"That's unfair."

"You literally traded me a court date for coffee last month."

"That was commerce."

Jay laughed despite himself.

Then his face changed.

"Listen. People outside are asking stupid questions."

"What questions?"

"The sort where the question isn't really the question."

Malik knew exactly what he meant.

"Anyone said yes?"

Jay looked at him sharply.

"Don't ask me that."

Malik raised his hands.

"Fair."

Across the room Bruce was telling Connor something that made Connor look both horrified and fascinated.

Craig sat nearby with the expression of a man waiting for a bus.

Malik looked at him.

"Does he know?"

Jay followed his gaze.

"Craig?"

"Yeah."

"If half of us know, he probably knows more than half of us."

Malik nodded.

That made sense.

What did not make sense was Connor.

Still there.

Still talking.

Still apparently unaware that proximity had started becoming information of its own.

## MASON

Mason watched Connor laugh at something Bruce said and thought the lad was beginning to look human again.

That was good.

Then Connor moved across and sat near Craig.

That was less good.

Mason waited.

Connor noticed him watching.

"What?"

Mason walked over.

"Nothing."

"You've got that face."

"What face?"

"The one where you're about to tell me something old-man-ish."

Bruce made a choking sound trying not to laugh.

Mason ignored him.

"You remember what I said?"

Connor sighed.

"Don't collect other people's problems."

"See? Learning."

"I'm sitting down."

"Next to a very complicated chair."

Craig looked up.

"Am I the chair?"

Mason looked at him.

"You said it."

Craig smiled.

Connor shook his head.

"Everyone's acting like I'm joining a gang because I spoke to the bloke."

Mason's expression softened.

"No. They're not."

Connor looked at him.

"Then what?"

Mason glanced around the room.

Three men were pretending not to watch Craig.

One was pretending not to watch Connor.

"They're noticing," Mason said.

"Noticing what?"

"You."

Connor frowned.

"Why?"

Mason could have explained reputation.

Could have explained that prison often assigned meaning before a person chose one.

Could have told him that people had started attaching Craig's name to money outside.

But Mason did not know enough to frighten the lad properly, and half-information was sometimes worse than none.

"Just don't mistake being noticed for being respected," he said.

Connor looked unimpressed.

Bruce leaned across.

"That was extremely old-man-ish."

Mason nodded.

"Thank you."

Craig laughed.

Connor did too.

Mason walked away thinking that one day Connor would understand exactly what he meant.

He just hoped it wasn't tomorrow.
