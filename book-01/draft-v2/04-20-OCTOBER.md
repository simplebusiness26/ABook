# 20 OCTOBER

## CONNOR — G WING

By Thursday morning Connor had started to believe G Wing was prison.

That was how quickly a person could adjust to anything.

Three days earlier the doors, shouting, keys and endless instructions had felt impossible. Now he knew which noises mattered and which ones could be ignored. He knew roughly when food appeared. He knew which officer would answer a question properly and which one would answer it while already walking away.

He even knew where the toilets were.

Progress.

Then an officer told him to pack his things.

Connor stared at him.

"Why?"

"Moving."

"Where?"

"C."

Connor waited for more.

There was no more.

"C Wing?"

The officer looked at him as if Connor had asked whether Thursday was a day of the week.

"That's what C means, yeah."

Then he moved on.

Connor looked at the small collection of property that had somehow already become his entire world.

He had spent days wanting to leave G Wing.

Now that somebody had told him he was leaving it, he wanted details.

Across the landing, Craig was packing as well.

Connor looked over.

"You going C?"

Craig nodded.

"Apparently."

"You know what it's like?"

"A prison wing."

"Helpful."

"I'm here for you."

Connor laughed.

The laugh disappeared when he picked up his bags.

This felt different from being moved around reception.

Reception had been something happening *to* him.

G Wing had been temporary because everybody kept saying induction, induction, induction, as if the word itself promised another stage.

C Wing sounded permanent.

Not forever.

But longer.

Long enough to have a bed somebody considered his.

Long enough to have a cellmate.

Long enough for people to learn his name.

He followed the officer out.

## CONNOR — C WING

The first thing Connor noticed about C Wing was that everybody seemed to notice him.

He came through carrying his property and felt heads turn almost before he had stepped properly onto the landing.

Not all at once.

That would have been easier.

One man looked up from a conversation.

Another watched Connor's bags.

Someone further along leaned against the rail and said something to the man beside him.

Connor suddenly became aware of everything he owned.

The clothes.

The toiletries.

The little bits of prison property that had felt meaningless five minutes ago and now seemed brightly labelled NEW PERSON.

He adjusted his grip.

Craig walked a short distance behind him carrying his own things.

That created a second wave of looking.

Connor did not know whether people were looking at him anymore or past him.

Probably both.

"Fresh one," somebody called.

Another voice answered with something Connor did not catch.

He kept moving.

He had made it one flight of stairs when the alarm sounded.

The whole atmosphere changed.

Connor stopped.

People who had been leaning casually against rails moved. Voices changed. Officers started giving instructions. Men stepped back from an area near the showers.

Connor looked because everyone looked.

A prisoner was brought out quickly with staff around him, holding the side of his face. For one horrible second Connor thought he had seen something far worse than he actually had.

The image hit him harder because nobody had prepared him for it.

A minute earlier people had been talking about tea and trainers.

Now an alarm was going and a man was being taken away for treatment.

Connor stood frozen with his bags.

One of the prisoners nearby glanced at him.

"Welcome to C Wing."

A few men laughed.

Connor did not.

Craig had stopped a few steps behind him.

Connor looked at him.

Craig's face had changed only slightly.

Not frightened.

Alert.

That was somehow more unsettling.

"Keep moving," an officer said.

Connor moved.

Later, much later, he would hear that the injury had looked worse in the confusion than it actually was. He would even see the same prisoner again after it had healed.

None of that helped Connor now.

Now, he was carrying all his possessions up the stairs of a wing he had never seen before while every instinct in his body screamed that he had misunderstood what prison was.

G Wing had been prison with stabilisers on.

C Wing had just taken them off.

## JAMO — CELL 3-27

Jamie Mills had been in prison six months and therefore considered himself extremely experienced whenever somebody newer than him walked through the door.

This confidence disappeared around men who had done ten years.

But Connor had done three days.

Compared with Connor, Jamo was practically staff.

He was lying on the bottom bunk when the door opened.

An officer pointed Connor inside.

"Blake. You're in here."

Connor stepped through with his bags.

Jamo looked at him.

Connor looked at Jamo.

There was a brief moment where both men assessed whether the next several weeks of their lives had just become significantly worse.

Jamo sat up.

"You snore?"

Connor frowned.

"Don't think so."

"Good start."

"You?"

"Only when I'm asleep."

Connor stared at him.

Jamo smiled.

"I'm Jamo."

"Connor."

"I know. Officer just said it."

Connor put his bags down.

The cell was smaller than he had hoped and larger than he had feared, which seemed to be prison's favourite compromise.

Two bunks.

Two people's things.

Not enough room for either person to pretend the other one wasn't there.

"How long you been in?" Connor asked.

"Six months."

Connor looked impressed before he could stop himself.

Jamo noticed.

That was enjoyable.

"First time?" Connor asked.

"Yeah."

Connor's expression changed.

"Thought you knew everything."

"I do. Ask me again in another six months and I'll know less."

Connor laughed.

Jamo nodded towards the top bunk.

"That's yours."

"Why?"

"Because I was here first. That's basically constitutional law."

Connor accepted this.

A minute later he was trying to arrange his things without touching Jamo's things, which was impossible because the entire cell appeared to be Jamo's things.

"You just come off G?" Jamo asked.

"Yeah."

"Three days?"

"About that."

"Standard."

"Thought induction was two weeks."

Jamo shrugged.

"Induction's whatever they say induction is. Doesn't mean you sit on G for two weeks. You'll still be doing bits of it while you're living here."

Connor nodded slowly.

"So this is main wing?"

"This is C. Double cells. A's doubles too. M's mostly singles."

Connor looked around the cell again.

"Singles sounds better."

"Depends who you're locked up with."

Jamo looked at him deliberately.

Connor smiled.

"Give it a day."

"Fair."

Noise travelled through the door.

Connor listened.

Jamo watched him listening.

"You heard the alarm on the way in?"

Connor nodded.

"Yeah."

"Don't let your head run away with it. First week everything looks ten times worse because you've got no idea what's normal."

Connor thought of the man being taken from the shower area.

"That normal?"

Jamo considered it.

"No. But everyone carrying on afterwards is. That's the bit that'll mess with you."

That answer stayed with Connor.

The alarm had stopped.

Somewhere outside, somebody was already laughing again.

## CONNOR / JAMO — WHO'S WHO

Jamo's greatest pleasure that afternoon was discovering Connor knew almost nobody.

"Right," he said when the doors opened for association. "Education."

Connor looked suspicious.

"What?"

"You need to know who's who."

"Why?"

"Because at the minute you look at everyone like they might eat you."

"I don't."

Jamo looked at him.

Connor sighed.

"Fine."

They stepped onto the landing.

Jamo immediately caught Connor pointing with his chin.

"Don't do that."

"Do what?"

"Make it obvious I'm giving you the prison tour."

"You literally called it education."

"Privately."

Connor smiled.

Jamo leaned against the rail.

"See the ginger one?"

Connor looked.

Bruce was talking to three different people while eating something from a packet.

"Bruce. Already met him."

Jamo looked disappointed.

"Ruined my best one."

"He says he's dangerous."

"Bruce says lots of things. Half the wing's got a Bruce story. None of the stories match."

Bruce looked up, saw them watching and waved.

Jamo waved back.

"See? Knows everyone."

"Mason?" Connor asked.

"Reception job. Older bloke. Been in and out enough times he probably knows the building's birthday. He's on M at the minute. Single cell."

Connor nodded.

"He's been giving me advice."

"Listen to him."

"He told me hear all, see all, say nothing."

Jamo's expression became serious for perhaps the first time.

"That's good advice."

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"Because everyone learns it after they should've."

A man came along the landing and stopped twice to speak to different people before reaching the stairs.

Jamo lowered his voice.

"That's Malik."

"What's his thing?"

"Information."

"What does that mean?"

"Means if you tell him your mum's buying a new washing machine, by tea time he'll know the make, the price and who's delivering it."

Connor laughed.

"Useful though?"

"Very. That's why you don't tell him anything you don't want becoming useful to somebody else."

Malik glanced up.

Jamo immediately started discussing toothpaste.

Connor nearly laughed again.

Further along, a stockier man stood near the phones looking irritated before he had even made a call.

"Macca," Jamo said. "Family man. Don't bother him when calls go wrong."

"Why?"

"Because his whole sentence happens through that phone."

Connor understood that more than he wanted to.

He thought of his mum.

Jamo noticed the change in his face and moved on.

"Darren."

A thin man was sitting slightly apart from everyone else, staring at nothing.

Jamo's voice lost its joking edge.

"He's got his own battles. Don't judge him on a bad day."

Connor nodded.

That was all Jamo said.

They moved towards the stairs.

"Leon is mostly over A side when we're mixed for things. Gym bloke. Proper routine merchant. Doesn't like drama."

"Sounds sensible."

"That's why prison keeps trying to give him drama."

Connor smiled.

"Jay?"

Jamo looked at him.

"How do you know Jay?"

"Heard the name."

"See? Three days and you're already collecting names. Mason's gonna be furious."

"Who's Jay?"

Jamo sighed theatrically.

"Usually A Wing. Knows people. Proper people-person, but don't mistake that for harmless. He's got outside links and his own lot around him. Doesn't mean he wants every problem brought to his door."

"And Deano?"

Jamo looked more impressed.

"You've definitely been listening."

"Hear all."

"Fair enough. Deano's A as well. Different circle. Knows lads from outside. He and Jay aren't exactly mates, aren't exactly enemies. Depends what day it is and what the problem is."

Connor looked across the wing.

Names were starting to attach themselves to faces.

That changed everything.

Before, prison had been a crowd.

Now it was becoming people.

Then Craig came onto the landing.

Jamo stopped talking.

Connor noticed immediately.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Don't do that."

"Do what?"

"Everyone says nothing when they mean something."

Jamo looked at Craig, then back at Connor.

"You know him?"

"Been talking to him."

"Yeah. I heard."

Connor frowned.

"How?"

Jamo gave him a look.

"You're in prison."

Connor had heard Craig say almost exactly the same thing.

"So what's the story?"

Jamo shook his head.

"Which one?"

"About Craig."

"That's the problem. There are about fifty. London. Sussex. Money. Clubs. Drugs. Violence. People owing him. Him owing people. Big conspiracy. Half the stories make him a businessman. Other half make him Darth Vader."

Connor laughed.

Jamo did not.

"What?"

"Just don't assume because someone is sound with you that everybody else sees him the way you do."

Connor looked at Craig.

Craig noticed and nodded once.

Connor nodded back.

Jamo saw that too.

"See?"

"See what?"

"You've been on C Wing twenty minutes and Craig knows your name."

"He knew it on G."

"That is not the defence you think it is."

Connor laughed.

Jamo shook his head.

"Fresh people are exhausting."

## JAY — A WING

By the time Jay heard Connor and Craig had both moved onto C, the information had already been given three different meanings.

Version one: coincidence.

Version two: Craig had asked for it.

Version three: Connor was already working for him.

Jay believed none of them.

That did not mean none would become important.

A man beside him said, "C makes it easier."

Jay turned.

"Easier for what?"

The man shrugged.

That annoyed Jay more than an answer would have.

"Don't start speaking in riddles."

"I'm just saying people know where people are now."

Jay looked at him.

"People always know where people are. Doesn't mean everybody needs to make something of it."

The man raised both hands.

"I didn't say me."

"Good. Keep not saying it."

Jay walked away.

He had already decided he did not want people around him being used for somebody else's old argument.

Now the prison had begun arranging the pieces closer together anyway.

That was how trouble worked sometimes.

It did not need a plan.

It only needed enough people to believe somebody else had one.

## DEANO — A WING

"Craig's on C."

Deano kept eating.

"Congratulations to C."

"That Connor kid's gone there too."

Deano looked up.

"Who?"

"Young lad. New one. Been walking around with him."

"Walking around with him or existing near him?"

"Same difference to some people."

Deano put his fork down.

That was the sentence he disliked.

Same difference to some people.

Prison could turn coincidence into allegiance before lunch.

"Leave the kid out of it," Deano said.

"I'm not doing anything."

"Didn't say you were."

"Then why tell me?"

Deano smiled without humour.

"Because apparently everyone in this place needs everything explained twice."

## LEAH — C WING

Leah watched Connor arrive carrying his property and recognised the expression immediately.

Main-wing shock.

Reception frightened people because everything was unknown.

A main wing frightened them differently because suddenly the unknown had names, cells, routines and people who had been living there long before you arrived.

Then the alarm went.

Leah moved with the other staff.

By the time things had settled, Connor looked as if somebody had shown him the trailer for the next several years of his life.

She found him later outside his cell.

"You all right?"

"Yeah."

"You know I've worked here long enough to recognise that answer?"

Connor looked away.

"Just wasn't expecting that."

"Nobody expects anything on their first day. That's why it's their first day."

Connor looked towards the shower area, then away again.

Leah kept her voice level.

"Don't build the whole prison out of one incident. Most of your day will be waiting, eating, talking rubbish and trying to get things done. Then sometimes something happens very quickly."

Connor nodded.

"Does everyone just get used to it?"

Leah thought about that.

"People get used to reacting. That's not quite the same thing."

Connor looked at her.

For once he did not have a joke ready.

Then Craig came along the landing.

Connor's attention shifted.

Leah noticed.

Of course she did.

Craig stopped.

"You settled?" he asked Connor.

"Got a cellmate who thinks six months makes him governor."

From inside the cell Jamo shouted, "I can hear you."

Craig smiled.

Leah almost did too.

Then she remembered the amount of attention already collecting around Craig.

C Wing had just become more complicated.

## MALIK — C WING

Malik liked C Wing because information did not have to travel far.

He disliked C Wing for exactly the same reason.

By evening he knew Connor had been moved in with Jamo.

He knew Craig was several cells away.

He knew the alarm earlier had already produced six versions of an incident that most people had not actually seen.

And he knew the money attached to Craig was no longer being discussed as abstract gossip.

That last part bothered him.

He saw Connor and Jamo on the landing.

Jamo noticed him and immediately started talking about toothpaste.

Malik smiled.

At least somebody was teaching the kid properly.

Connor looked over.

Malik nodded.

Connor nodded back.

Then Connor looked towards Craig.

Malik followed the glance.

There it was again.

Proximity becoming meaning.

Malik wondered how long it would take Connor to understand that everyone else had started writing a story about him before he had decided what his own story was.

## MASON — M WING

Mason heard Connor had gone to C before he saw him again.

He also heard about the alarm.

By the time the story reached M Wing, the injured man had apparently died twice and returned to association in time for tea.

Mason ignored all of it.

He knew how prison stories worked.

What interested him was Connor's move.

C Wing meant a proper cellmate.

A proper landing.

People with routines and histories and grudges that had begun long before Connor arrived.

It was where new prisoners started learning prison from prisoners instead of officers.

That could be useful.

It could also be disastrous.

Mason saw Connor later during movement.

"C treating you all right?"

Connor nodded.

"Yeah. Cellmate's all right. Jamo."

Mason knew him.

"Good lad. Talks too much."

"He gave me the whole prison Wikipedia."

"Believe half."

"Which half?"

"Exactly."

Connor smiled.

Mason looked at him carefully.

"Remember what I told you?"

Connor sighed, but this time there was humour in it.

"Hear all. See all. Say nothing."

"Good."

"Jamo says the same."

"Then for once prison has achieved consistency."

Connor laughed.

Craig appeared further along.

Connor's eyes moved towards him automatically.

Mason saw it.

He said nothing.

Hear all.

See all.

Say nothing.

Sometimes advice was easiest to give before you needed it.

The difficult part was remembering it when you did.
