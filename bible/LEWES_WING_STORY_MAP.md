# HMP Lewes Wing Story Map — Creator-Supplied Period Detail

This file records the creator's lived/remembered prison geography for the period being fictionalised. It is **story canon for drafting**, but it is not yet treated as publicly verified current HMP Lewes information. Once the exact story year is locked, period-specific details should be checked and adjusted if necessary.

## Core progression for Connor

### G Wing — induction / first arrivals

- Connor first enters the prison system through reception and then reaches **G Wing**, which functions as the induction/early-arrival wing in the creator's remembered period.
- The broad idea of an induction period may be described as lasting around two weeks administratively, but in practice Connor should spend only about **two to three days physically living on G Wing** before being moved onward.
- G Wing should feel temporary: everybody is new, waiting for information, learning basic routines, asking questions and trying to work out where they will be sent next.
- Connor and Craig initially overlap here after reception, which allows Connor to begin talking to Craig before either settles into a main wing.

### C Wing — main wing, double cells

- Connor and Craig are then moved to **C Wing**.
- C Wing is remembered by the creator as **double bang-up**: Connor shares a cell.
- Connor's move to C Wing is a major emotional step because it is the first time the prison begins feeling less like a reception process and more like the place he may actually have to live.
- Craig should be housed on C Wing at the same broad time but **not in Connor's cell**.
- Connor's cellmate becomes an important guide to the social world of Lewes.

### A Wing — main wing, double cells

- **A Wing** is also remembered as double bang-up.
- Important recurring characters can live there so that the prison feels larger than Connor's immediate landing.
- Information should move between wings through ordinary social contact, association, legal visits, shared routines and people who know each other, without the novel turning prison geography into a security manual.

### M Wing — single cells

- **M Wing** is remembered as **single bang-up**.
- Characters housed there can experience prison differently from Connor because privacy, loneliness and status all change when a prisoner has a cell alone.

## Story function of the wing map

The reader should gradually learn the prison through Connor rather than receiving an institutional map in exposition.

The clearest introduction comes when Connor reaches C Wing and his new cellmate informally explains who people are: not as an encyclopaedia, but as a young prisoner who has been inside long enough to know faces, friendships, reputations and rumours.

That scene can point Connor — and therefore the reader — towards recurring characters such as Mason, Bruce, Jay, Deano, Malik, Leon, Darren, Macca and others as they appear naturally around the prison.

The cellmate must not be omniscient. Some of what he says should be true, some incomplete, some exaggerated and some simply what prisoners believe. This preserves the series rule that reputation and truth are not the same thing.

## Connor's first move onto C Wing

The move from G Wing to C Wing should feel significant.

Connor carries his property/bags onto the wing and immediately feels watched because he looks new. Other prisoners notice a fresh arrival and Connor becomes self-conscious about what he is carrying and where he is supposed to go.

Very early in that arrival, an alarm interrupts the ordinary wing noise. Connor witnesses the aftermath of a serious but **non-graphically described** assault near the shower area. In the confusion, Connor initially believes the injury is worse than it later turns out to be. Much later he sees the same prisoner healed and understands how fear and first impressions can distort what a newcomer thinks he has seen.

The scene's purpose is **not** to sensationalise violence. Its purpose is to establish that prison can change from ordinary to frightening in seconds, while experienced prisoners around Connor react with a level of familiarity that shocks him even more.

Do not describe weapon construction, attack technique or graphic injury detail.
