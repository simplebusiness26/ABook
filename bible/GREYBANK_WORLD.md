# HMP Greybank — Book One World Map

Greybank is a fictional British local/remand prison used as the main stage for Book One.

The prison should feel geographically small but socially enormous. The same landing can contain people who barely understand one another's version of prison.

## Narrative zones

### Reception / First Night
Where Connor and Craig enter the story. Everyone arrives stripped of outside certainty. Reputation nevertheless begins operating almost immediately.

### Induction
The place where Connor and Craig spend enough time together for Connor's instinctive intervention to become possible.

### Main wing / landings
The social heart of Book One. Most recurring prisoners should repeatedly cross paths here so that POV chapters feel interconnected rather than like separate short stories.

### Exercise yard
Useful for visible alliances, conversations and the way groups arrange themselves physically without needing exposition.

### Gym
Leon's territory. One of the few spaces where prisoners from very different groups can have ordinary shared routines.

### Healthcare
A place where status can temporarily fall away. Nurse Hannah Reed can become a future POV here.

### Staff office / staff-only spaces
Leah Mercer, Tom Bennett and Senior Officer Patel's version of Greybank. The exact same incident becomes paperwork, risk decisions, colleague conversations and career consequences.

### Visits / phones / family contact
Macca's emotional centre and one of the best ways to remind readers that every prison story extends outside the walls.

## Social structure

Greybank does **not** have two simple teams called “Black prisoners” and “white prisoners.” Instead it has overlapping networks:

- neighbourhood friendships;
- outside criminal associations;
- family links;
- race and cultural familiarity;
- religion;
- age;
- gym friendships;
- cell/landing relationships;
- people who have served previous sentences together;
- people who simply like one another.

Some visible clusters are predominantly Black. Some are predominantly white. Some are mixed. The important thing is that every member remains an individual.

### Jay's network
Predominantly Black, with links to an outside street network. Some members feel strong loyalty to the outside; others think outside figures ask prisoners to take risks they would never take themselves.

Officer Bennett's coercion/corruption storyline becomes connected to people around this network, but the specific individuals applying pressure — not race — are responsible for their choices.

### Deano's circle
Predominantly white, with its own friendships and outside history. It is not automatically Craig's side. Deano mostly wants somebody else's feud kept away from his own people.

### Cross-group characters
Malik, Yusuf, Leon, Bruce and others can move between circles for different reasons. Everyday prison life should constantly complicate neat labels.

## Craig's gravitational effect

Craig does not arrive and instantly “take over.” The more interesting effect is indirect:

1. people recognise the name;
2. people ask what they know;
3. outside information arrives;
4. money changes the stakes;
5. groups have to decide whether the issue concerns them;
6. Connor makes one very public choice;
7. everyone reinterprets Connor because of it.

The reader should feel Craig changing Greybank before Craig himself appears to be doing very much.

## Big Sharky's reach

Big Sharky is not physically at Greybank in Book One. He is held in the dispersal/high-security system.

His power is demonstrated by the fact that information and pressure can travel between establishments through existing human relationships. We do not need to explain operational methods.

Book One gives only fragments:
- Big Sharky knows Craig;
- their history is personal;
- he wants Craig harmed;
- serious money is attached;
- the failed Greybank attempt does not end the feud;
- Craig's transfer into the dispersal system will eventually put them in the same world.

The *reason* remains protected for Book Two.

## Officer mirror

Leah Mercer and Tom Bennett are designed as opposites.

**Leah:** emotionally compromised because she begins caring too much about a prisoner.

**Bennett:** ethically compromised because outside pressure and fear pull him into corruption.

Both storylines ask the same question in different ways: **How much of yourself can this institution take before you become somebody you did not expect to be?**

## Continuity rule

Before drafting any chapter, check:
- where the POV character is housed/working;
- who they have already met;
- what they know about Craig;
- what they know about Big Sharky;
- what rumours they believe;
- which social groups they can naturally interact with;
- what happened in the previous 24–48 story hours.

This keeps the ensemble feeling like one living prison rather than disconnected plots.
