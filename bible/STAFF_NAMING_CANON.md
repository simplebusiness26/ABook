# Staff Naming Canon — Book One

## Core rule

Prison officers are presented to prisoners and to the reader primarily by **rank/title + surname**, not by first name.

Examples:
- **Officer Mercer**
- **Officer Bennett**
- **Officer Grant**
- **Senior Officer Patel**

POV headings should follow the same convention, for example:
- `## OFFICER MERCER`
- `## OFFICER BENNETT`
- `## OFFICER GRANT`
- `## SENIOR OFFICER PATEL`

In ordinary narration before any deliberate first-name reveal, use **Mercer**, **Bennett**, **Grant** and **Patel** rather than their first names.

## Mercer

**Leah is her first name. Mercer is her surname.**

For most of Book One she is therefore **Officer Mercer** to prisoners and **Mercer** in narration.

Craig learning that her first name is **Leah** should be treated as a meaningful intimacy/boundary beat. Until that moment, the manuscript should not casually give prisoners or the reader routine access to her first name.

Once Craig knows it, his selective use of **Leah** can signal that their relationship has become more personal than the normal officer/prisoner boundary.

## Bennett

**Bennett is his surname.** His first name exists only as author-side character information and should not normally appear in Book One prose.

He should be **Officer Bennett** in headings/identification and **Bennett** in ordinary narration.

## Grant / Patel

Use **Officer Grant** and **Senior Officer Patel** (or Officer Patel where rank is not being stressed). First names are not needed on page unless a later plot beat specifically earns their disclosure.

## Why this matters

The naming convention reinforces prison social reality: staff surnames are public working identities while first names feel private. A first-name reveal therefore carries story meaning rather than being background information.

## Draft cleanup rule

When preparing reader-facing copies of Draft V3, automatically normalise old headings/references such as `LEAH`, `TOM BENNETT`, `BENNETT` and `GRANT` to the appropriate officer-title/surname convention, while preserving any future deliberately written first-name reveal.
