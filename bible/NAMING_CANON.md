# Naming Canon — Prison Names, Street Names and Earned Nicknames

This file controls how prisoners are referred to in the active Book One draft.

## Core naming principle

Not every prisoner should sound as though he was named by a character list.

Names should reflect how people actually know one another:

- **Older heads / established serious figures** can be powerful enough that their ordinary name is the name: **Craig**, **Mason**.
- **Distinctive personalities** may also simply keep their ordinary/natural nickname: **Bruce**, **Macca**.
- **Younger street-connected or crew-affiliated prisoners** should more often be known by short street names, tags or long-standing nicknames rather than formal first-and-surname combinations in dialogue.
- Officers, legal paperwork and narration can still use legal names where appropriate, which lets the reader understand that the street name and the actual person are not the same thing.
- Do not make every nickname sound theatrical. A believable mix is better: some names come from initials, childhood, surname, area, a joke or an incident and may have almost nothing to do with how dangerous the person actually is.

## Connor — earned prison name

Connor must **not** enter prison with the nickname that defines him later.

He starts as simply **Connor** / **Connor Blake**. That matters because the reader watches prison create a second identity around him.

### Earned nickname: RUSH

The working nickname is **Rush**.

It is earned only after Connor instinctively intervenes when Craig is attacked.

### Exact birth of the name

After the fight, Connor is initially treated as one of the participants because staff saw him rush into the violence. He is separated from the wing and spends the night in segregation while CCTV and accounts are reviewed.

The following day he is cleared of being part of the original attack and returned to **C Wing**.

By then the story has travelled around the prison without him.

**Bruce introduces the nickname.** It should feel casual and funny rather than like a gang ceremony. Connor walks back onto the wing and Bruce calls something like:

**“Here he is. Rush.”**

Connor does not immediately understand it. Bruce explains that Connor saw trouble and rushed straight into it before anybody with sense could stop him. Jamo finds this funny. Somebody else repeats the name. Then somebody Connor does not know uses it later.

That is the moment Connor realises he has not simply been *given* a nickname by Bruce - the wing has accepted it.

The important point is that Connor does **not choose the name**. The prison chooses it for him.

The name carries several meanings:

1. To other prisoners, **Rush** is the young lad who did not hesitate when Craig was in trouble.
2. It literally describes the act that made him known: he rushed into a fight he could have avoided.
3. To the reader, it describes Connor's deeper strength/flaw: he often acts before he understands the consequences.

Craig should usually continue calling him **Connor**, even after the wing starts calling him Rush. That subtly distinguishes Craig's personal relationship with him from the reputation everyone else is building around him.

Connor can initially dislike or laugh at the nickname, then gradually begin answering to it. Later, when he starts enjoying Craig's reflected status too much, hearing **Rush** shouted across a wing should show how far he has moved from the frightened young man who arrived carrying his bags.

## Nickname rule for the series

A nickname should do at least one of three things:

- reveal history;
- reveal how other prisoners see the person;
- create a gap between reputation and reality.

Avoid giving every dangerous person an obviously dangerous name. The ordinary, stupid or accidental nicknames are often the ones that feel most real.
