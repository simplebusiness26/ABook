# HMP Lewes — Book One Research Reference

This file separates **publicly verifiable facts** from fictional material so the novel can use a real prison without accidentally inventing facts about it.

## Stable public facts

- HMP/YOI Lewes is in Lewes, East Sussex.
- It is a men's prison.
- Recent official descriptions identify it as a **Category B reception/resettlement prison**.
- The present prison was built in the early 1850s and first used in **1853** as the county prison for Sussex.
- Historic England records the prison as **Grade II listed** and dates the principal construction to **1850–55**, with an extension in 1868.
- The historic fabric includes flint, stone and red-brick detailing, high boundary walls and prominent towers.
- Historical inspection material describes the prison as having a **semi-radial design**.
- A newer house block was added in the 2000s, meaning the modern prison combines Victorian and newer fabric.

## Useful Book One atmosphere

The opening should exploit the contrast between:

- a prison that visibly comes from another century;
- generations of prisoners having passed through the same institution;
- Connor experiencing it for the first time;
- old architecture surrounding modern bureaucracy and prison routines;
- the normal town continuing outside while Connor's world has suddenly narrowed.

HMP Lewes should feel daunting without becoming Gothic fantasy. The power comes from the fact that it is a real, functioning institution built in the nineteenth century.

## Remand / reception relevance

Recent inspection reporting describes Lewes as a reception prison with a high proportion of remanded or unsentenced prisoners. That supports Book One's premise of Connor arriving while his case/sentence is unresolved and multiple new prisoners entering the story at different stages of the court process.

## Accuracy rule

Population figures, wing functions, staffing, daily regime, providers and other operational details change over time.

Before finalising scenes we must therefore:

1. lock the calendar year in which Book One takes place;
2. research Lewes for that period;
3. use public sources for institutional facts;
4. use creator-supplied lived experience for sensory/social details where appropriate;
5. fictionalise plot events and characters;
6. avoid presenting invented security or smuggling methods as real operational information.

## Current public research sources used to establish the setting

### GOV.UK — Lewes Prison
Official HMPPS/MOJ prison information. Confirms Lewes as a men's prison in East Sussex.

### Historic England — Lewes Prison, List Entry 1043892
Grade II listing. Records construction in 1850–55, extension in 1868, flint construction with stone/red-brick dressings, towers and boundary walls.

### HM Inspectorate of Prisons — HMP Lewes inspection, February 2024
Describes Lewes as a reception and resettlement Category B prison for adult and young adult men, records the 1853 origin and documents a substantial remand/unsentenced population at the time of inspection.

## Fiction boundary

Connor, Craig, Bruce, Big Sharky, Leah Mercer, Tom Bennett and the wider cast are fictional characters. Their stories must not imply wrongdoing by real named HMP Lewes employees or prisoners.

This reference should grow as the manuscript reaches new areas of the prison. Research only what the story actually needs.
