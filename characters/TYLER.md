# Tyler — Book One Character Canon

## Core role

Tyler is another young first-timer, but he reacts to fear very differently from Connor.

Connor receives a reputation he did not ask for. Tyler manufactures one because he is terrified, then discovers that other prisoners may take his lies seriously.

## Age

Working age: **19–20**.

## Outside-life backstory

Tyler grew up around enough trouble to recognise the language and names of the criminal world without ever truly being an important part of it.

Preferred working direction:

- rough estate / chaotic social environment;
- funny, loud kid at school;
- exclusions and poor direction after school;
- short jobs such as warehouse, labouring or casual work;
- very close to his mum, who sees straight through his bravado;
- an older cousin or local connection with a genuine reputation that Tyler has spent years borrowing socially.

His mum should puncture the prison persona completely. To everybody else he is Tyler the mouthy lad. To her he is still **Ty**.

## Offence

Working direction: a messy group robbery / public-order / violent incident involving mates rather than a professional criminal enterprise.

Tyler insists he 'barely did anything.' That should contain at least some truth, but not enough to make him innocent of all responsibility.

Exact offence remains unlocked.

## Personality

- loud;
- funny;
- cocky when nervous;
- desperate not to look frightened;
- exaggerates people he knows;
- pretends to understand prison before he does;
- occasionally mocks people he thinks look weaker because he is trying to move attention away from himself;
- lies to family on the phone and says prison is easy.

The reader should see the fear underneath before many of the other prisoners do.

## Core arc

Tyler begins making claims about who he knows, what he has done and what he can arrange.

At first the lies are harmless performance.

Eventually somebody believes one of them and asks Tyler to prove it or act on it.

That traps him inside the personality he created to protect himself.

His central question becomes whether he can admit vulnerability before the fake reputation creates real consequences.

## Story purpose

Tyler is a mirror to Connor:

- Connor becomes **Rush** because other people interpret something real that he did.
- Tyler builds a fictional tougher version of himself because he is frightened.
- Both discover that once prison gives a story about you enough momentum, you do not completely control it anymore.

Tyler needs humour, family life and ordinary moments so he feels like a complete person rather than a cautionary device.
