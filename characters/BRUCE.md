# Bruce — Character Canon

## Core identity

Bruce is small, skinny, ginger, unpredictable, funny and repeatedly underestimated.

He should never sound like a generic comic-relief character. When Bruce speaks, the line should feel as though **only Bruce would say it**.

His humour is slightly unhinged, oddly specific and delivered with complete confidence. He can make bizarre observations, appear in conversations nobody expected him to be part of, and treat serious situations with an angle nobody else would think of.

The key rule is that Bruce is **quirky, not stupid**.

## Presence in Book One

Bruce is deliberately an in-and-out character.

He can disappear for stretches, then reappear at exactly the right moment with information, a strange comment, an unexpected friendship or a version of events nobody else has heard.

He is not required to carry a large self-contained Book One plot in the same way Connor, Darren or Jinx do. His story function is cumulative: every appearance adds another piece to the question of who Bruce actually is and why he seems to know everybody.

He is the person who creates Connor's nickname **Rush** after the Craig incident.

## Voice rules

Bruce dialogue should usually have at least one of these qualities:

- strange logic that somehow makes sense by the end;
- unexpected confidence;
- oddly specific comparisons;
- inappropriate timing without deliberate cruelty;
- casually knowing something nobody expected him to know;
- saying the serious thing as a joke, then leaving before anybody can ask what he meant.

Do not make every line a gag. Bruce becomes more interesting when an apparently ridiculous comment occasionally turns out to be perceptive.

## Reputation

Other prisoners have stories about Bruce that Connor cannot initially separate into truth, exaggeration and complete invention.

His violent mythology should remain high-level and non-graphic. The point is that his appearance does not match the reputation people attach to him.

## Long-term series role

Bruce becomes much more important after Book One.

He later ends up in the **high-security / dispersal estate** around the same broader period as Craig. He does not simply choose to follow Craig; his own sentence, security position and prison movements must make the transfer plausible.

Once there, Bruce becomes unexpectedly useful.

Why he is useful should emerge gradually through later books rather than be fully explained in Book One. Possible strengths include:

- social reach across very different prisoner groups;
- being underestimated by both prisoners and staff;
- remembering small details;
- hearing information because people do not treat him as a serious political figure;
- forming strange but genuine friendships;
- understanding prison personalities better than people realise.

His exact offence, sentence and reason for later high-security placement remain **UNLOCKED** until the Book Two architecture is developed.

## Emotional layer

Bruce should occasionally give flashes that suggest the humour is partly how he survives prison.

Do not over-explain him. A large part of Bruce's charm is that the reader never receives a neat psychological essay explaining why Bruce is Bruce.

He can be extremely funny on one page and unexpectedly loyal or perceptive on the next.

## Series promise

Book One teaches the reader to enjoy Bruce whenever he appears.

Later books reveal that the funny little man who kept appearing at the edge of everybody else's stories was quietly becoming one of the most useful recurring characters in the entire prison universe.
