# Josh — Book One Character Canon

## Core role

Josh is one of the younger prisoners the reader should genuinely grow attached to. He is not introduced as doomed. He should be funny, energetic, irritating in an affectionate way, curious and easy to like.

Darren gradually takes him under his wing. That relationship gives Darren a rare chance to feel useful and experienced, while Josh gets the older-mate figure he naturally gravitates toward.

## Age

Working age: **18–20**. Exact age remains to be locked during scene planning.

## Offence

Working offence: **vehicle theft / taking a motor vehicle**. The exact legal charge and case circumstances should be checked against the final 2019 chronology before publication.

The offence should feel impulsive and youthful rather than like part of an organised-crime identity.

## Personality

Josh has very high-energy, impulsive, distractible, fast-moving behaviour — an **ADHD-like energy** without automatically diagnosing him with ADHD in canon unless later chosen.

Traits:

- talks quickly and jumps topics;
- struggles to sit still for long;
- gets bored easily;
- asks endless questions;
- laughs loudly;
- can turn an ordinary prison task into a story;
- gets excited about small things such as canteen or a decent meal;
- often acts first and thinks afterwards;
- is fun company when his energy is up;
- becomes noticeably different as prison and substance use wear him down.

The humour must come from Josh as a person, not from mocking neurodivergence.

## Outside-life backstory

Josh should have a normal outside life worth wanting back.

Preferred working direction:

- mostly raised by his mum;
- an inconsistent or unreliable father figure, creating a habit of attaching himself to slightly older lads;
- possibly a younger sister he is protective of;
- talks about ordinary plans for release: work, driving, family, girls, getting his life sorted;
- does not initially think prison is going to define him.

These details remain flexible until scenes make one version feel definitive.

## Darren relationship

Josh begins following Darren around naturally rather than being formally recruited into anything.

Darren enjoys becoming the experienced one for once. Other prisoners normally see Darren as somebody struggling; Josh sees somebody who knows prison and can explain it.

Their relationship should include genuine warmth and ordinary humour before deterioration begins.

The tragedy is not that Darren deliberately harms Josh. It is that Darren gradually normalises his own damaged world for somebody younger who trusts him.

Josh eventually becomes involved with substances and later dies following a substance-related medical collapse. The death should be handled non-graphically and focus on aftermath, shock and Darren's guilt rather than physical detail.

Darren survives and carries the knowledge that although Josh made his own choices, Darren helped make that world feel normal.

## Story purpose

Josh shows:

- how quickly prison can absorb a young person who initially still feels connected to ordinary life;
- how mentorship can become harmful without malicious intent;
- the difference between being experienced and actually being a good influence;
- why Darren cannot keep telling himself that his addiction only affects Darren.

The reader should know and like Josh well before his deterioration becomes central.
