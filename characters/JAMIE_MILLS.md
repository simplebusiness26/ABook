# Jamie "Jamo" Mills

**Working role:** Connor's first C Wing cellmate and informal guide to the social world of HMP Lewes.

## Core concept

Jamie Mills, usually called **Jamo**, is in prison for the first time too, but he is roughly six months ahead of Connor.

That makes him useful because he occupies the exact middle ground Connor needs: he is no old lag and does not pretend to understand everything, but he has been inside long enough for the building to stop feeling impossible. He knows names, faces, routines, which rumours are probably nonsense and which people are worth treating carefully.

Jamo should feel like the version of Connor that Connor could become after six months if nothing dramatic happened to him.

## Personality

- Friendly without being instantly trusting.
- Talks a lot when locked in the cell because conversation makes time move faster.
- Enjoys knowing who is who and explaining things to somebody newer than himself.
- Sometimes presents rumours as facts, then quietly admits he only heard them from somebody else.
- Has enough prison confidence to look experienced to Connor while still being very inexperienced beside Mason and the older repeat prisoners.
- Finds Connor's first-day assumptions funny because he remembers making almost identical ones.

## Function in Book One

When Connor and Craig move from G Wing to C Wing after the early induction period, Connor is placed in a double cell with Jamo.

Jamo becomes the reader's first informal social map of the prison. From the cell door, landing, association and ordinary conversations, he can identify recurring people Connor has already glimpsed or will soon meet:

- Mason — experienced repeat prisoner who knows the institution better than freedom.
- Bruce — underestimated wildcard who somehow knows everybody.
- Malik — information man; useful but dangerous to tell too much.
- Jay — socially confident, connected and dealing with outside loyalties.
- Deano — has his own circle and wants other people's feuds kept away from it.
- Leon — lives through routine and training rather than prison politics.
- Darren — dealing with dependency and a completely different version of prison.
- Macca — family-focused; measures prison in calls, visits and missed life outside.
- Craig — the one Jamo talks about most carefully because even he cannot separate what he knows from what he has heard.

Jamo's explanations must **not** function as an omniscient character list. The fun is that the reader later discovers where Jamo was right, where he exaggerated and what he completely misunderstood.

## Connor relationship

At first Connor regards Jamo as experienced because six months sounds enormous to someone who has been inside for days.

Jamo regards Connor as painfully fresh.

The relationship should become warm and funny. Jamo can teach Connor small harmless institutional habits and social expectations while Mason gives Connor the deeper long-term wisdom.

Jamo also notices Connor's growing relationship with Craig before Connor understands how visible it has become.

## POV potential

Jamo can remain a recurring non-POV for Book One or receive a short mosaic POV later if useful.

A later Jamo POV could reveal how strange Connor's transformation looks to someone who shared a cell with him when he was still frightened of everything.

## Safety / realism rule

Jamo can discuss reputations, relationships, wing culture and consequences. He should not provide practical instructions for violence, contraband, evading staff or other wrongdoing.
